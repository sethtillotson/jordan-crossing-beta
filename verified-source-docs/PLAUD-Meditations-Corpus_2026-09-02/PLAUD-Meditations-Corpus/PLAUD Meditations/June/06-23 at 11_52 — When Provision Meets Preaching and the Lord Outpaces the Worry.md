# 06-23 at 11:52 — When Provision Meets Preaching and the Lord Outpaces the Worry

\*\*Title:\*\*06-23 Call Reflection: Faith, Provision, and Releasing Outcomes to God

**Recorded:** 2026-06-23 11:52:40

**Source Discernment:** C — Recorded Conversation or Event (Live Capture)

**Classification:** Call Reflection

## Opening Observation

I was on break when Brother Samuel called, and what started as a quick logistical check-in about payment accounts and ad recording turned into something the Spirit was clearly steering from the beginning. There is a tension I am sitting with as I revisit this conversation: the gap between what a man confesses with his mouth and what his anxious hands are already doing. And underneath all of it, there is a quiet testimony of provision — raw, timed, and unmistakable — that I cannot let pass without giving it the weight it deserves.

**Why This Matters:**

This matters because the Body of Christ is full of brothers and sisters who carry genuine faith in their hearts while simultaneously carrying a spirit of anxious self-management in their hands — and most of the time, they cannot feel the contradiction. The call to trust God is not merely doctrinal furniture; it is the operational posture of the Kingdom. When we confess faith but then scramble to make everything go right through our own precision and control, we are not living in unbelief exactly — but we are living beneath the provision and the glory that God intends to display through our weakness. The stakes are not just personal peace; they are the visibility of God’s glory. If I perfect everything myself, I leave no room for Him to show up. And if He shows up in the gap, the testimony belongs to Him alone.

## The Worldly Mind’s Interpretation

From a purely natural vantage point, this conversation reads as exactly what two entrepreneurial friends navigating a startup would sound like on a break-time call. One partner is energized, projecting forward, trying to solve three problems at once — account structures, ad talent, payment logistics, budget allocation. The other is grounding him, reminding him to slow down and focus. The anecdote about the Cash App overdraft extension is a welcome coincidence, a small fortunate turn in an otherwise tight financial window. There is nothing here, in the natural reading, that rises above the ordinary texture of two men trying to make something work while supporting each other through the stress of it. The theology Seth drops into the middle of the conversation would read, to the natural mind, as a coping mechanism — a spiritualized way of managing performance anxiety and softening the sting of potential failure. Job is invoked not as revelation but as comfort. The warning about not holding things too tightly is simply good advice dressed in religious language.

**The Limitation:**

But the natural mind cannot perceive what is actually happening beneath the surface of this exchange, because the natural mind reads provision as coincidence and warning as sentiment. It has no category for a God who is actively watching a man preach to an AI in the middle of the night while broke, and then choosing that exact moment — not the morning, not payday, not a formal prayer meeting — to drop a notification onto his phone that opens just enough oxygen for the next few days. The natural mind also cannot see the prophetic function of a brother speaking truth into another brother’s anxiety, because it reads that as relational friction rather than the iron sharpening iron that Proverbs 27:17 describes. What looks like two men talking logistics is, at the level of the Spirit, two men being trained — one in the school of provision by faith, one in the school of releasing the outcomes he is gripping too tightly.

## What the Spirit Reveals Through It

As I listen back to this exchange, the first thing that arrests me is the speed at which Brother Samuel moves. He is not a dishonest man, and his faith is real — I know it. But within the span of a single call, he cycles from logistics to excitement to budget projections to a declaration that nothing is ending this week without a thousand dollars spent on ads. And I hear myself in that moment saying, *stop projecting into the future and enter into the now.* That phrase did not come from a business book. It came from something the Spirit has been forming in me, and I felt it press through me in that moment with more urgency than I expected.

There is a Hebrew concept that underlies this kind of present-moment attentiveness. The word שָׁמַר (shamar, shah-MAR) — to keep, to guard, to watch with diligent attention — appears throughout the Old Testament as a posture of active, present faithfulness. It is the word used when God tells Adam to *keep* the garden, and it is the word used when Israel is commanded to *keep* the commandments. שָׁמַר (shah-MAR) is not passive waiting; it is alert, present tending. And what I notice is that anxiety, by contrast, is perpetually elsewhere — it is always in the next week, the next outcome, the next sale, the next campaign. Anxiety cannot keep the garden because it is always somewhere the garden is not.

I said to Brother Samuel, *if you try to perfect everything, where is the weakness for God to show His glory?* And the moment I said it, I felt the weight of it land on me too, not just on him. This is not a management principle. This is a theology of divine co-authorship. The Apostle Paul carries this thread when he writes in 2 Corinthians 12:9 that the Lord told him, *“My grace is sufficient for you, for My strength is made perfect in weakness.”* The Greek word there for *perfect* is τελειόω (teleioō, tel-ay-OH-oh) — to complete, to bring to its appointed end. God’s power is brought to its τελειόω (tel-ay-OH-oh) precisely in the space that human capability leaves unfilled. If I fill every gap myself, I foreclose the very territory in which His strength would have been made visible.

This is why I invoked Job. Not lightly, and not as a dark threat, but as a genuine pastoral warning given in love. Job is the canonical case study for the man who had everything aligned — wealth, family, righteousness, reputation — and who in losing it all was brought to something none of his prosperity had ever given him. Job 42:5 is the verse I keep returning to: *“I have heard of You by the hearing of the ear, but now my eye sees You.”* The Hebrew distinction here is staggering. שֶׁמַע (shema, SHEH-mah) — hearing, report, rumor — is what Job had before. He knew *about* God. He was righteous before God. But it was the stripping that opened the eye. And I mean that word *eye* in its full weight: עַיִן (ayin, AH-yin) — the seeing faculty, the witness, the place of true perception. What came through the suffering was not merely endurance. It was encounter. It was the direct knowledge of God that no amount of prosperity or religious performance had produced.

I told Brother Samuel, *you will be depressed for five years and then one day you will be great.* I want to be honest about what I meant. I was not prophesying ruin over him. I was describing the anatomy of transformation, which almost never feels like transformation while it is happening. The seed in John 12:24 must fall into the ground and die before it bears fruit. That dying is real. It has a duration. It has grief. But it has an outcome that the unbroken seed never reaches. The Spirit was pressing me to say: *do not hold this so tightly that God has to pry it from you to show you what He had planned all along.*

And then I am listening to myself say all of this to my brother, and the Spirit turns the lens. Because later in that same call, I am telling him what happened to me the night before — dead broke, can’t get to work, up past midnight not in anguish exactly, but occupied in an unusual way. I had built an AI to mirror back to me what I was preaching to myself. I was going through thousands of emails. And in that moment of what might look like distraction or restlessness, God dropped a notification on my phone — Cash App extending an overdraft coverage, no fees, exactly enough for gas until the next paycheck. Just like that. Quietly. Precisely. Without drama.

I need to sit with the timing of that provision, because it is not incidental. The pattern of Scripture is that God often provides in the moment of engaged faithfulness, not in the moment of desperate petition. Elijah’s brook did not dry up until it was time to move; and when it dried up, the Word of the Lord came — *arise and go to Zarephath* (1 Kings 17:9). The provision was never in the brook. The provision was in the obedience. And I was not sitting in anguish that night asking God to fix my account. I was doing something — something arguably strange, preaching to an AI, working with what was in my hand — and the provision arrived in the middle of that. This is the same God who told Moses *what is that in your hand?* (Exodus 4:2). He works with what is present, not what is wished for.

There is also something I want to name about what I said near the beginning of the call, almost as a throwaway line but which carries the most theological freight of anything spoken: *Who is the one who makes things go well or not well? The Lord Jesus Christ, the Lord our God.* That sentence is not a platitude. It is a claim about sovereignty, and if it is true — and it is — then the spirit of anxiety that tries to control outcomes is not just emotionally unpleasant, it is theologically dissonant. It is living as though the answer to that question were *me*. Proverbs 21:31 says, *“The horse is prepared for the day of battle, but deliverance is of the Lord.”* You prepare. You work. You make the ad. You put the two hundred dollars on it. But you do not *deliver* the outcome. That is not your office. And mixing those two offices — worker and deliverer — is precisely what produces the frantic energy I was hearing in Brother Samuel’s voice.

I also want to honor what he said near the end of the call, because it stopped me: *I am not worried about anything, to be quite frank, because I am worried about getting closer to God.* That sentence has the shape of genuine kingdom reordering. Matthew 6:33 is not a formula; it is a description of what happens to a man’s priorities when the Kingdom of God becomes his actual first pursuit. *“Seek first the kingdom of God and His righteousness, and all these things shall be added to you.”* The Greek verb here is προστίθημι (prostithēmi, pros-TITH-ay-mee) — to place alongside, to add in addition to. The things are not withheld. They are simply repositioned. They become the byproduct of a pursuit rather than the object of it. When Brother Samuel says his primary concern is drawing closer to God, he is not being pious. He is, whether he feels it fully yet or not, stepping into the gravitational order that causes all other things to be added.

I want to press one more thing, because the call ended with me saying this would be good work on his insecurity. I said it lightly but I meant it deeply. Insecurity and anxiety about outcomes are not simply personality traits to be managed; they are indicators of where our identity is still anchored. When my identity is anchored in Christ — in the finished work, in the adoption, in the sealed inheritance — the failure of a business campaign, while genuinely disappointing, cannot threaten who I am. But when my identity has some of its roots in the success of the venture, then every dollar spent on ads and every sale that does or does not come carries an existential weight it was never meant to carry. The work of the Spirit in both of us through this season is not merely to produce business success. It is to produce men whose identity is unmovable regardless of the outcome of the campaign.

What the Lord is doing through the texture of these conversations — the logistics, the laughter, the warnings, the testimonies, the Cash App notification — is building a testimony in real time. Not a cleaned-up testimony for a stage someday, but a living, breathing account of two men in the middle of something who are learning, imperfectly and honestly, what it means to trust God with the whole thing. That is a sacred thing. And I do not want to lose it in the noise of making things go right.

## Implications and Challenges

First, let the grace be stated plainly: the faith that Brother Samuel carries is real, and the work of trusting God in the middle of a venture — rather than after it is secured — is harder than it looks from the outside. This is not a rebuke of a faithless man. This is the invitation deeper, offered to a man already in the water.

That said, there is a pattern in modern Christian entrepreneurial culture that conflates Kingdom fruitfulness with business momentum. The language of *Kingdom* gets layered onto commercial activity in ways that can subtly shift the primary metric from obedience and character formation to revenue and scale. I am not saying business is ungodly — I am saying that when the spirit of the marketplace colonizes the spirit of the man, the anxiety and the controlling energy Brother Samuel was expressing become normalized rather than named. The call is to notice it, name it, and return to the right ordering.

There is also a challenge to the way we use faith language as emotional management. When we say *I have faith, I know it will be fine* in the same breath as projecting exactly how we will spend the first sale, we are often using faith vocabulary to manage our anxiety rather than to genuinely surrender outcome. True faith does not need the outcome pre-visualized. Abraham went out, not knowing where he was going (Hebrews 11:8). The faith that honored God was the faith that moved without the destination in view.

The testimony of the Cash App provision should not be read as a formula — *preach to an AI and God will fund your gas.* The danger of testimonies is that we abstract them into methods. What actually happened is that a man in a posture of engaged, present faithfulness received provision in the middle of what he was already doing. The lesson is posture, not technique. The Spirit moves in present engagement, not in anxious future-management.

Finally, the willingness to speak hard truth to a brother — *you might spend five years in it before you see the glory* — is itself an act of love that the modern church often lacks the courage for. We have traded prophetic friendship for emotional comfort, and in doing so we have left brothers and sisters underprepared for the genuine weight of the path. Speaking the anatomy of transformation to someone is not pessimism. It is, in the truest sense, preparing them for the fullness of what God intends.

## What This Means For Us

**For Spiritual Maturity:**

Maturity in this context looks like learning to hold open hands toward outcomes we have worked hard to build — genuinely working, genuinely preparing, and genuinely releasing. It looks like recognizing the gap between confessed faith and operational anxiety, and choosing not to live in the gap but to close it by returning to the sovereignty of God as a lived reality, not merely a stated belief. The man who can make a *shitty ad* and mean it — who can put two hundred dollars on it and rest — is further along in the Kingdom than the man paralyzed by perfection or inflated by projection.

**For Kingdom Obedience:**

The obedience this calls forth is the obedience of the present moment: the next step, taken faithfully, with what is in hand. Not the whole strategy mapped to the thousandth dollar. Just this. Record the ad. Post it. Pray over it. Let God move through it. And when the provision comes — whether large or small, whether timely or delayed — mark it, speak it, and give the testimony to its rightful author.

**For Prophetic Posture:**

The prophetic posture modeled in this conversation is friendship that does not flatter. It is the willingness to speak the Job passage into a season of rising excitement, not because we want our brothers to fail, but because we want them to be built on the rock that neither the business’s success nor its failure can shake. To watch, to listen, to discern what the Spirit is actually doing beneath the logistics — and to say it plainly, with love, in the middle of a break-time phone call — this is the prophetic life lived on the ground.

## A Closing Prayer

Father, I come before You as one who has seen Your hand move in the middle of the night, in an inbox, in a notification on a phone, in the middle of something that looked like nothing — and I know You are not far from any of us. I thank You that You are not waiting for perfect conditions to show up in our lives. You showed up when I was broke and preaching to an AI, and that testimony belongs entirely to You. I intercede now for my brother, that the faith he carries would be rooted deeper than the outcome of any campaign, that his identity would be sealed so completely in Christ that neither the first sale nor the absence of it could move him from that ground. I ask You to build in both of us the patience, the presence, and the open hands that make room for Your glory — not the glory of a well-managed plan, but the glory that shines through the cracks of our insufficiency. Let every step we take in this season be taken in the now You have given us, and let every provision — however small, however quietly delivered — be received as the faithful word of a God who has never once forgotten where we are. Be glorified in the weak ad, in the small budget, in the sleepless night, and in the friendship forged in truth. You are the one who makes things go well or not well, and we return that authority to You completely. Amen.
