# 07-21 Call Reflection: Pregnancy, Covenant Love, and Finding the Sacred in the Ordinary

**Title:** 07-21 at 12:11 — Mango, Morning Sickness, and the Baby Growing on the Left Side

**Recorded:** 2026-07-21 12:11:21

**Source Discernment:** C — Recorded Conversation or Event (Live Capture)

**Classification:** Call Reflection

## Opening Observation

There is something quietly sacred about an ordinary lunch break call — mango in hand, a sick and tired wife at home, two cats stealing chips, and somewhere on the left side of her body, a baby growing in secret. I did not expect to find anything worth meditating on in this small exchange, and yet that is precisely where I find myself arrested — by the sheer tenderness of the unremarkable. The Kingdom of God has a habit of hiding itself in exactly these moments.

**Why This Matters:**

So much of what we call spiritual life gets located in the dramatic — the altar call, the open vision, the thunderous sermon. But the saints who endure are not only those who meet God on the mountain; they are those who learn to recognize His fingerprints on the mundane table of daily life: on a slice of mango eaten in the middle of a workday, on a woman too tired to wash dishes but faithful enough to carry new life, on a cat named Monty who comforts a grieving wife without being asked. If we cannot see God in this, we may not recognize Him when He comes disguised as a stranger at the gate. Learning to inhabit the present moment with gratitude and attentiveness is not a minor devotional exercise — it is one of the great disciplines of the Kingdom.

## The Worldly Mind’s Interpretation

A secular observer watching this exchange would see nothing more than a brief domestic check-in between a working husband and a pregnant wife managing discomfort at home. The natural mind would categorize it efficiently: pregnancy fatigue is a biological phenomenon, emotional heaviness is a hormonal side effect, the cats are pets exhibiting conditioned attachment behavior, and the whole conversation is unremarkable relationship maintenance. There is nothing wrong with that reading on its own level — it is accurate as far as it goes. The world is very good at naming the surfaces of things. It can describe morning sickness, identify oxytocin, explain grief triggers, and recommend rest. It sees a lunch break. It sees a symptom. It sees a to-do list involving moringa pruning.

**The Limitation:**

But the natural mind, however precise its instruments, cannot perceive the weight of glory that rests quietly on the ordinary. It has no category for the holiness of two people loving each other imperfectly and warmly across a phone call while a hidden life grows on the left side of a body that is tired and brave. It cannot hear in the laughter about stolen chips the sound of covenant friendship. It cannot recognize in “keep growing the baby” a prayer dressed as a joke. The mind that has not been renewed by the Spirit of God processes this call as data, but the Spirit-taught heart receives it as gift — and gift always points back to a Giver.

## What the Spirit Reveals Through It

As I listen back to this call, what strikes me first is a word Gladys used almost in passing — *heavy*. She said she felt heavy, tired, and even a little mad. I know that feeling well from watching her carry this season. But I find myself sitting with that word longer than I expected to, because heaviness in pregnancy is not merely a complaint — it is a sign. It is the body announcing that something of real weight is being formed within it. The Hebrew imagination would not have been surprised by this. The word כָּבוֹד (kavod, kah-VODE) — the glory of God — carries the same root as the word for heaviness, for weight. Glory is not always light and easy. Sometimes it is the kind of weight that makes you want to lie down.

There is something deeply Scriptural about the weariness of one who is carrying life they did not manufacture. Gladys did not create this child through her own ingenuity. Life was placed within her. And now her body bends under the dignity of that calling. I think of Hannah, weeping so intensely before the Lord that Eli mistook her for drunk — her heaviness was not weakness, it was the travail of one who was about to receive a promise she had been aching toward. I think of Mary, who was told she was highly favored and immediately began a journey that included disgrace, displacement, and a sword through her own soul. The favor of God and the weight of God are not opposites. They arrive together.

What Gladys is bearing is not merely biological. She is a vessel. And what arrests me in this reflection is that I almost missed it — caught up in my mango, checking the time, wondering whether I should come home early. The Father is growing something on the left side of her body, and I could not feel it when she told me to put my hand there. That detail will not leave me. She said the baby was literally hiding. And I wonder how many times the Spirit has been pressing something real against the walls of my life, and I have simply not been still enough to feel it.

I notice that the comfort in this call did not come from sophisticated counsel or spiritual instruction. It came from cats. Monty curled close to a crying woman watching a video that brought grief to the surface, and his small, warm presence was enough to interrupt the sorrow. I do not want to overstate this, but I also do not want to dismiss it. The Lord has always used small and unlikely comforters. He sent ravens to feed Elijah. He used a burning bush to arrest a fugitive shepherd. He chose a donkey to correct a prophet. The category of “too small to matter” does not appear to exist in the economy of God. Monty mattered on 2026-07-21. He was there when I was not.

There is a thread of joy running through this entire exchange that I want to honor carefully. The laughter about ice cream, the teasing about chips, the back-and-forth about the Doppler — this is not frivolous. This is the fruit of a covenant that has been tended. Joy in marriage is not a decoration added on top of the serious work of faithfulness; it is evidence that the faithfulness is working. Proverbs 5 does not merely tolerate delight between husband and wife — it commands it. “Rejoice with the wife of your youth,” the text says (Proverbs 5:18). The playfulness in this call is an act of obedience whether we named it as such or not.

I am also moved by the small weight of “I need a resume.” That phrase lands in the middle of laughter and mango and cats like a quiet gravity. There is something shifting in the vocational landscape of this season, and Gladys immediately responded — *I will refine it tonight.* Tired, unwell, carrying a child, still grieving something she saw in a video — and her first response to my need was *I will help you.* That is Proverbs 31 without the performance. That is the kind of love Paul was trying to describe in Ephesians 5 when he reached for the metaphor of Christ and the Church: not symmetrical transactions, but mutual, sacrificial orientation toward the other’s flourishing.

The moringa tree appears near the end of the call, almost as an afterthought — her grandfather’s voice reaching through her to tell her not to sleep too long, to tend what is growing in the garden. I find this quietly significant. Moringa is one of the most nutritionally dense plants known — deeply used in communities where bodies need fortifying. Gladys is pregnant. She is planting and pruning. The grandfather’s voice is reminding her to stay engaged with the living things around her even in her exhaustion. There is something here about generational wisdom, about not withdrawing entirely even when the body demands rest, about remaining connected to the growing things in our care.

The Doppler conversation stopped me. She wants me to be able to hear the heartbeat. She wants me present to the life I helped begin. And there is a tenderness in that desire that mirrors something I believe the Father feels toward us — not merely that we would acknowledge His works from a distance, but that we would press close enough to hear the rhythm of what He is doing, to feel the small bump on the left side where the promise is hiding. The Psalmist did not merely know about God; he pressed into nearness: “My soul thirsts for God, for the living God” (Psalm 42:2). There is a difference between knowing a baby exists and pressing your ear to where the heartbeat is.

What this call ultimately says to me — when I receive it not as a transcript but as a living moment — is that I am in a season of quiet abundance dressed as ordinary difficulty. A wife who is sick but faithful. A baby growing in a place I could not yet feel. Cats who grieve and comfort. Ice cream guarded jealously. A garden that needs pruning. A resume that needs refining. None of this is dramatic. All of it is real. And the Spirit who searches all things, even the deep things of God, is fully present in rooms where mangoes are eaten on lunch breaks and love is spoken through laughter and the word *bye* is interrupted by something ridiculous and wonderful.

The baby is hiding. But the baby is there. And I think that is the word the Spirit wants me to carry out of this call: *what is hidden is not absent*. What you cannot yet feel does not mean nothing is growing. The Kingdom of God is like a seed, Jesus said — it goes into the ground invisible, and the ground does not announce the moment of germination. You tend the soil. You rest when you must. You prune the moringa. You buy the Doppler. And one day you press close enough to hear the heartbeat.

## Implications and Challenges

The first challenge this meditation quietly presents is to the assumption that spiritual attentiveness belongs only to designated “devotional time.” I was on a lunch break. I was eating mango. The sacred was not waiting for me to open my Bible before it showed up — it was already in the conversation. The believer who trains themselves only to hear God in formal settings will miss the majority of what He is saying. The whole earth is full of His glory (Isaiah 6:3), and that includes the left side of a pregnant woman’s abdomen during a phone call on a Tuesday.

The second challenge is to the tendency toward spiritual heroism — the idea that meaningful faith looks like mountain-top intensity. This call holds no dramatic spiritual content, and yet it is saturated with the fruits of covenant love: patience, tenderness, humor, mutual service, attentiveness to life. These are not lesser things. The person who can love well in the small and the tired and the ordinary is doing something profoundly Kingdom-shaped, even if no one photographs it.

Third, the detail about not feeling the baby when Gladys offered me the chance is worth sitting with honestly. How often do those closest to us extend an invitation into presence — *hold here, feel this, hear this* — and we are close enough to respond but not quite still enough to receive? Proximity is not the same as attentiveness. I was on the phone. I was not far away. But I did not feel what she was trying to show me. The invitation to come closer, to press ear to skin, to buy the Doppler — this is not merely practical parenting advice. It is a picture of the kind of nearness God desires from His sons.

Fourth, there is a quiet challenge here for those who are in seasons of physical or emotional heaviness — particularly in pregnancy, in grief, in exhaustion — to resist the lie that their weariness is a spiritual failure. Gladys is tired and that tiredness is honorable. She is not failing by needing to rest. She is being faithful to a calling that is genuinely costly. The Body of Christ must do better at honoring the beloveds in its midst who are carrying heavy and holy things and need permission to lie down without shame.

Finally, the sound of laughter in this call should not be spiritually minimized. Joy is armor. Joy is witness. Joy is the natural overflow of a heart that knows it is not alone, not abandoned, not managing life by willpower alone. The enemy does not fear our grimness; he fears our gladness. A husband and wife who can still laugh about ice cream while she is sick, tired, and carrying new life — that is not a small thing. That is the strength of the Lord expressed in the language of delight.

## What This Means For Us

**For Spiritual Maturity:**

Maturity is not the ability to extract theology only from formal study. It is the ability to receive even a brief and ordinary phone call as a moment thick with the presence of God — to be awake to what is growing on the left side, to press close enough to hear what is hidden, to let the small and the tender and the funny and the tired all speak without flattening any of it into noise.

**For Kingdom Obedience:**

The obedience this moment calls for is simple and not simple at all: be present. Buy the Doppler. Come home when you can. Put your hand where she tells you to. Tend the garden. Help refine the resume. Let covenant love be practiced in the kitchen and in the fruit you eat on your lunch break, not only in the prayer closet. The Kingdom is built in these rooms too.

**For Prophetic Posture:**

The prophetic word for this season may not arrive with thunder. It may arrive as a small bump on the left side, a hidden heartbeat, a cat that curls close when grief breaks open unexpectedly, a grandfather’s voice saying tend the living things. The servant who abides learns to hear the still small voice — and to recognize it even when it sounds like laughter about stolen chips.

## A Closing Prayer

Father, I thank You for this small, bright, ordinary moment — for mango on a lunch break, for a tired and brave wife, for a baby hiding on the left side learning to be known, for cats that comfort without being asked, and for the laughter that is still possible in the middle of heaviness and weariness and the quiet upheaval of a new season. You are not absent from any of it. You are not waiting for the dramatic before You show up. I receive this day as a gift from Your hand — the whole of it, the heavy and the playful and the uncertain and the tender. Teach me to press close enough to feel what is growing. Teach me to hear the heartbeat I cannot yet hear. Teach me to be present the way You are present — fully, warmly, without distraction — to the ones You have placed in my life to love and be loved by. Let this season of new life be met with attentiveness and wonder. And may the child growing in secret be received into a home that already knows how to find You in the unremarkable and the beautiful. To You be the glory in the church and in this household, now and throughout all generations. Amen.
