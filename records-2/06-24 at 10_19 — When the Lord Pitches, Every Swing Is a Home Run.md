# 06-24 at 10:19 — When the Lord Pitches, Every Swing Is a Home Run

**Title:** 06-24 at 10:19 — When the Lord Pitches, Every Swing Is a Home Run

**Recorded:** 2026-06-24 10:19:52

**Source Discernment:** C — Recorded Conversation or Event (Live Capture)

**Classification:** Call Reflection

## Opening Observation

Something shifted in me as I listened to my brother’s voice — not just the excitement of two sales and eight hundred dollars, but the unmistakable texture of a word coming to pass. I had spoken it before it happened, and now here it was: consistent, on-schedule, unhurried. What arrested me most, though, was not the fruit itself but the posture the Lord was calling us both into — a posture of stewarding Kingdom assignment in the marketplace as men who know they are being watched, and who know who is doing the watching.

**Why This Matters:**

The convergence of business faithfulness and prophetic expectation is not an accident, nor is it merely motivational language dressed in spiritual clothing. When brothers labor together under genuine Kingdom commission, the dynamics of provision, opposition, and fruitfulness are no longer governed purely by market forces or human strategy — they become sacramental signs. How we handle a client, how we make the video, how we book the meeting: these are not neutral economic acts. They are demonstrations of stewardship before the Lord of the harvest. The Body of Christ desperately needs men who can stand in the marketplace without becoming *of* the marketplace — men who understand that faithfulness in small things is not preparation for the Kingdom, it *is* the Kingdom, already breaking in.

## The Worldly Mind’s Interpretation

From the outside looking in, this is a straightforward entrepreneurial story: two men building a business together, celebrating early traction, encouraging each other through the friction of a slow start. The natural mind would identify the turning point as a strategic one — perhaps the videos gained organic reach, perhaps the timing aligned with demand, perhaps the founder’s persistence simply outlasted the slow season. The emotion is understandable, the celebration is appropriate, and the momentum is real. A business coach would say to document what worked, scale it, stay disciplined, and manage the founder’s psychology so anxiety does not disrupt the upward curve. Nothing about this conversation would strike the natural observer as anything more than two friends navigating the early stages of a promising venture.

**The Limitation:**

But the natural mind cannot perceive what was actually happening in that call. It cannot account for the fact that the word of provision was spoken *before* the provision arrived — not as wishful thinking, but as prophetic declaration rooted in discerned commission. It cannot explain why the consistency Brother Samuel was noticing felt qualitatively different from statistical probability. The natural mind has no category for the marketplace as a theater of covenant, no framework for understanding that clients might function as divine appointments, no ears to hear the prayer that followed the call as anything other than sentimental closure. The flesh reads this as a good Tuesday morning. The Spirit reads it as the unfolding of a sent word.

## What the Spirit Reveals Through It

As I sat with what had just taken place on that call, I found myself returning to something I said to my brother — almost in passing, but not in passing at all: *I am prophesying it because it has been shown to me.* That is not boasting. That is accountability. When the Lord shows a man something, he becomes responsible for declaring it, stewarding it, and holding it before the one who needs to hear it. The prophet does not invent the word; he receives it and remains faithful to it even when the circumstances appear to contradict it. The slow videos. The ads that would not launch. The two-hundred-view reels that felt like nothing. These were not failures in the process — they were the narrow path being laid underfoot.

I think of Paul writing to the Corinthians: *“For we are God’s fellow workers; you are God’s field, you are God’s building”* (1 Corinthians 3:9). The Greek word translated “fellow workers” is συνεργοί (synergoi, sin-er-GOY) — those who labor *with* God, not merely *for* Him. There is a world of difference between those two postures. When a man labors *for* God, he brings his strategy and asks for blessing on top of it. When a man labors *with* God, he waits for the pitch. He does not swing at every opportunity — he swings at the ones the Lord throws. And when the Lord throws, it is always a home run.

That image — the Lord as pitcher, the servant as batter — is not whimsy. It is covenant mechanics. Throughout the Scriptures, the Lord consistently acts as the initiating party. He called Abram before Abram sought Him. He pursued Moses at the burning bush. He sent the disciples out two by two and told them that the harvest was already plentiful — *already*. The laborers were the variable, not the harvest. What my brother was experiencing was not market validation. It was the harvest responding to sent laborers.

And yet I noticed something tender in how Brother Samuel received this. He heard me. He agreed in his spirit. But the anxiety kept pressing — projecting forward, imagining worst cases, interrogating the consistency as if it might stop. I recognized that posture because I have lived in it. The enemy’s most effective work against a commissioned servant is not outright opposition — it is premature projection. Get the mind ahead of the present assignment, and suddenly the provision that is *here, today* looks insufficient for the imagined need of *tomorrow*. Jesus addressed this with surgical precision: *“Therefore do not worry about tomorrow, for tomorrow will worry about its own things. Sufficient for the day is its own trouble”* (Matthew 6:34). The word translated “worry” is μεριμνάω (merimnaō, meh-rim-NAH-oh) — to be divided in mind, to be pulled in multiple directions at once. It is the opposite of the single eye Jesus describes in the same passage.

So I said to him: stop projecting and take each day as it comes. Make the video. Book the meeting. Get the sale. This is not an oversimplification — it is a Kingdom algorithm. The manna in the wilderness was not stockpilable. When Israel tried to hoard it, it bred worms (Exodus 16:20). The Lord structured it that way deliberately — not to be cruel, but to train a people whose trust had been conditioned by Egypt’s scarcity logic into a new logic entirely: daily dependence, daily faithfulness, daily provision. What my brother and I are walking is not a business plan. It is a manna season. And manna seasons are not managed — they are inhabited.

But then the Spirit pressed me deeper into something I said during the call that I want to sit with more carefully. I said: *what if every client is an angel messenger of the Lord, dressed as a client, to see how we steward the Kingdom in the marketplace economy?* I am not speaking this as fanciful allegory. I am speaking it as the operational reality of Hebrews 13:2 — *“Do not forget to entertain strangers, for by so doing some have unwittingly entertained angels.”* The word “angel” is ἄγγελος (angelos, ANG-el-os) — messenger. Every person who comes through a marketplace door carries with them an embedded test of stewardship. Will I serve them as unto the Lord, or as unto a revenue target? Will I deliver with excellence because I fear the Lord, or because I fear a bad review? The marketplace is not secular ground. It is contested territory that belongs to the King, and every transaction is an opportunity to either advance or retreat His testimony in that space.

Paul captures the theater of this in 1 Corinthians 4:9 — the passage I alluded to in the call: *“For I think that God has displayed us, the apostles, last, as men condemned to death; for we have been made a spectacle to the world, both to angels and to men.”* The word “spectacle” is θέατρον (theatron, theh-AH-tron) — from which we get our English word *theater*. The world is a stage, and the heavens are the audience. Every sent servant is performing — not in the sense of pretending, but in the sense of acting out the drama of the Kingdom before a watching cosmos. When my brother makes a video with excellence, with honesty, with the Spirit undergirding the content, that is not just marketing. That is testimony. That is the Kingdom made visible in the marketplace theater.

This reframes the entire conversation about excellence in delivery. I said to Brother Samuel: we are to deliver on what we owe our clients with excellence, but not *to* them — *to the Lord*. This is the Colossians 3:23 principle made concrete: *“And whatever you do, do it heartily, as to the Lord and not to men.”* The word “heartily” is ἐκ ψυχῆς (ek psychēs, ek psoo-KAYS) — out of the soul, out of the deepest interior self. Excellence in Kingdom labor is not a performance standard. It is an act of worship. When we deliver a result to a client, we are laying it before the Lord and saying: *this is how we steward what You assigned us.* That is an entirely different motivational architecture than fear of client dissatisfaction.

I also need to sit with the friction piece. When I prayed after the call, I said: *the opposition, the friction, the attack — these are not evidence that we have gotten something wrong. They are evidence that the Lord has already gone ahead.* This is the logic of the Exodus — Israel did not move through unchallenged terrain. The pillars of cloud and fire did not lead them around resistance; they led them *through* it. The Red Sea did not part before they arrived at its edge. The pattern in Scripture is consistent: the Lord custom builds the narrow path, makes straight the feet, and allows the friction because the friction is the signature of a real assignment. Easy paths are not necessarily divine paths. But neither is difficulty alone proof of commission. The distinguishing mark is whether the Lord commissioned it, whether He has sustained it, and whether the fruit is appearing — consistently, as my brother said.

I close the main meditation here with the thing that moved me most deeply in the prayer I prayed after hanging up. I asked the Lord to remind Brother Samuel that *when the Lord pitches, it is always a home run*. I want to be precise about what that means theologically — because it is easy to hear that as prosperity-speak, as if commissioned servants always win in worldly terms. That is not what I mean. What I mean is this: a sent man operating in faithful obedience within a genuine divine commission does not swing blindly. He swings at the Lord’s pitch. And the Lord’s pitches are not random — they are covenant throws. They land where they are supposed to land. The outcome is not always what we imagine it to be. A home run in the Kingdom economy may look like a closed deal. It may also look like a relationship planted, a testimony deposited, a seed sown that will bear fruit in a season we will not see. But it is never wasted. It is never the Lord throwing wild. And that is the word my brother needs to carry with him today: *you are not swinging blindly.*

## Implications and Challenges

The Western Christian mind has largely accepted a binary: either you are in “ministry” or you are in “business,” and the two operate by fundamentally different rules. This call challenges that binary at its root. If the marketplace is genuinely a theater of Kingdom witness, then the sacred-secular divide that most believers have inherited is not a biblical reality — it is a Greek philosophical import dressed in church clothing. The New Testament knows no such divide. Every sphere of human activity falls under the Lordship of Christ, and every laborer is a steward, not a proprietor.

There is also a challenge here around prophetic responsibility. When a man receives a word from the Lord — a genuine disclosure of what is coming — he bears an obligation to speak it, hold it, and remain steady in it even when the circumstances do not yet confirm it. This is not presumption; it is faithfulness. Presumption claims a word without commission. Faithfulness declares a word *because* of commission. The difference is everything. Too many believers in our generation have either abandoned prophetic expectation entirely (suspicious of any forward declaration) or inflated it into charismatic performance. Both ditches miss the road. The road is: hear the Lord, declare what He shows, steward the word with sobriety, and let the word do its work.

The anxiety loop my brother was caught in — celebrating provision while simultaneously projecting scarcity into the future — is not unique to him. It is the default posture of any believer who has not yet been fully formed in the logic of daily dependence. The manna principle must be taught, received, and practiced. It is not automatic. The Israelites had to learn it through failure before they learned it through faith. We are not above that same curriculum. The question is whether we will receive the lesson the first time the Lord offers it or whether we will require the worm-breeding lesson first.

A more subtle challenge: our language around business and commerce can subtly re-center the self even when wrapped in spiritual language. When I said *we won’t even need the ad spend* — that was celebratory shorthand, not doctrine. The Spirit corrected the deeper point: we need to make the video, book the meeting, get the sale — not because we have found a clever organic funnel, but because those are the acts of obedience the Lord assigned. The strategy is not the point. The obedience is the point. And obedience is only as durable as the conviction that the Commander is real and the commission is genuine.

Finally, prayer as the posture *after* the call deserves more attention than we usually give it. I did not schedule a prayer time. I did not open a devotional. I hung up and immediately brought my brother before the Lord by name, with specific intercession shaped by what I had just heard. That is the priestly function of a Kingdom covenant brother — to take what passes through your hands and lay it before the throne. Intercessory prayer is not a spiritual extra. It is the maintenance infrastructure of sent Kingdom labor. If we do not carry each other before the Lord, we are operating the assignment on natural fuel alone.

## What This Means For Us

**For Spiritual Maturity:**

Spiritual maturity in the marketplace means learning to distinguish between the Lord’s pitch and every other pitch — and having the discipline not to swing at the wrong ones. It means receiving the consistency of provision with gratitude rather than anxiety, and training the mind to inhabit the present assignment without projecting fear onto the unknown future. Every consistent fruit is an invitation to deepen trust, not merely to celebrate success.

**For Kingdom Obedience:**

The specific obedience here is simple and demanding: make the video, book the meeting, deliver with excellence, carry your brother in prayer. These are not glamorous assignments. They are manna-level instructions — daily, unglamorous, and absolutely sufficient. Kingdom obedience does not always look like a burning bush moment. Sometimes it looks like posting a two-hundred-view video because that is what the Lord said to do today.

**For Prophetic Posture:**

The saints must learn to receive and hold prophetic declarations with sobriety. When the Lord shows something, it is not for entertainment — it is for anchoring. The prophetic word is a stake in the ground that the covenant servant returns to when the terrain gets rough. Hold the word. Declare it when appropriate. Let it steady your brother when his anxiety rises. And when the word comes to pass, let that passing be the fuel for the next season of faith.

## A Closing Prayer

Father, I come before You as one sent, as one who has been shown things and asked to speak them, as one standing between heaven and earth in the company of Your servants the prophets — and I am grateful. I am grateful that You pitch, that You are not random, that Your commissioning includes the provision, the friction, the timing, and the fruit. I bring my brother Samuel before You now with specific thanksgiving: two sales, eight hundred dollars, consistency emerging, faith rising. Let those signs be stakes in the ground for him — let them be the manna he returns to when the enemy tries to project scarcity onto the future. Deepen in him the settled knowing that You sent him, that You have gone ahead, that the narrow path is already custom-built beneath his feet. Where anxiety speaks, let the Word speak louder. Where projection rises, let present obedience anchor him. And let every client who comes through that door be received as one watched by the heavens — served with the excellence that belongs to You, delivered with the integrity that honors Your Name in the marketplace theater. Not our will, Lord, but Yours — in every pitch, in every swing, in every season of fruit and every season of discipline. You are faithful. The assignment is real. And we are not swinging blindly. To You be the glory, in the earth as it is in heaven. Amen.
