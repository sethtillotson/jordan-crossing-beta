# 06-22 at 12:34 — Led Into the Wilderness: Building for the Spirit in the Hour of Poverty

\*\*Title:\*\*06-22 Call Reflection: Spiritual Wilderness, AI Vocation, and the Secret Place

**Recorded:** 2026-06-22 12:34:02

**Source Discernment:** C — Recorded Conversation or Event (Live Capture / Call Reflection)

**Classification:** Call Reflection

## Opening Observation

As I listened to this conversation unfold between myself and my brother Samuel, something beneath the surface of business strategy and technical vision kept pressing through — a deeper current running under all the talk of equity splits, AI architectures, and investor pitches. There is a moment in this call where the whole conversation pivots, almost involuntarily, and something raw and real breaks through: *“I’ve still got a gods-shaped vacuum. I keep trying to fill with shit that ain’t God.”* That confession — honest, unguarded, spoken at the edge of a beach at sunrise — arrested me then, and it arrests me still. Something is being built here that is not merely commercial. Something is being prepared in the wilderness, and neither of us fully understands yet what it is.

**Why This Matters:**

There is a particular danger that faces men and women of genuine gifting and Kingdom vision when they stand at the intersection of real spiritual hunger and real material poverty. The danger is not laziness or faithlessness — it is the subtle substitution of *building* for *abiding*. The activity of creation, even God-honoring creation, can become a way of managing the ache that only the Presence itself can satisfy. This matters for every disciple navigating a season where the calling is clear but the provision is not yet visible, where the gifts are evident but the path forward is still shrouded. If we do not learn to distinguish between Spirit-directed labor and anxiety-driven construction, we will build remarkable things in the energy of the flesh and wonder why the fruit does not remain. The call being processed in this conversation is not merely a call to entrepreneurship — it is a summons to the secret place, to the rampart, to Peniel, and every brother or sister who has ever felt the weight of a gods-shaped vacuum needs to hear what the Spirit is saying through this moment.

## The Worldly Mind’s Interpretation

From a natural vantage point, this conversation reads as exactly what it appears to be on the surface: two entrepreneurially-minded men on a lunch break, strategizing about equity positions, AI service stacks, investor relationships, and business development pipelines. The logic is coherent and even sophisticated — breaking equity into tranches, stacking retainer services, leveraging technical ability against a network of closers and capital raisers. The world would look at this and see ambition meeting opportunity, two intelligent men trying to escape financial pressure through legitimate hustle and skill. Even the more personal moments — the admission of emptiness, the talk of prayer and wrestling — would be filed by the secular mind under the category of motivational self-talk, perhaps a cultural or religious framework that provides psychological comfort and helps regulate the anxiety of financial uncertainty. The world would affirm the drive, respect the technical ability, and quietly set aside the theological language as ornamental rather than operative.

**The Limitation:**

But what the natural mind cannot perceive is that beneath the strategy and the code and the revenue projections, there is a man being *led* somewhere — not by market analysis but by the רוּחַ (ruach, ROO-akh) — the Spirit, the breath of God — and the destination is not a valuation multiple. The flesh reads “I’ve been led into the wilderness by the Spirit with this AI” as metaphor or mood. The Spirit reads it as a precise theological statement with a biblical address. The natural mind cannot account for the fact that the most significant thing said in this entire conversation may not be about investors or SDKs or Linux architectures, but rather: *“I’m not going to leave the secret place. I’m not going to leave prayer until I get an answer.”* That is not entrepreneurial self-discipline. That is a posture the flesh cannot manufacture, and a resolve the flesh cannot sustain. What is unfolding here requires eyes that have been opened by grace to recognize the shape of the wilderness when you are standing inside it.

## What the Spirit Reveals Through It

Listening back to this conversation, I am struck first by how naturally and almost casually the confession surfaces — sandwiched between talk of beach sunrises and business metrics. My brother says it plainly, without ceremony: *“I’ve still got a gods-shaped vacuum. I keep trying to fill with shit that ain’t God. When will I learn?”* There is no performance in that statement. It is the cry of a man who is genuinely gifted, genuinely moving, and genuinely still hungry — and who has enough self-awareness to name the pattern even if he has not yet fully broken it. And as I listened to him say it, something in me did not respond with a theological correction. Something in me recognized it. Because I have been living in the same tension.

This is the language of Ecclesiastes before it becomes the language of the Song. Qoheleth’s great lament — *“vanity of vanities”* — is not nihilism. It is the ruthless honesty of a man who has tested every earthly cistern and found that none of them hold water. And the frightening thing about gifted people is that their cisterns are more elaborate, more impressive, more convincing to both themselves and others. A man who can build AI architectures that mirror the theology of the hovering Spirit, who can think in network topologies and semantic fractal systems, who can see the shape of the market and the shape of Scripture at the same time — that man has access to more sophisticated ways of avoiding God than most people ever will. The gifts themselves can become the substitution.

And yet — and this is where the Spirit presses deeper — the gifts were never meant to be the substitution. They were meant to be the offering. I said it in the call, perhaps almost in passing, but I am sitting with it now with more weight: *“I was able to build this persistent memory thing all because I was just building something for the Spirit.”* That sentence contains an entire theology of creative vocation. The best thing I have built — the thing that carries genuine spiritual architecture, that mirrors the רוּחַ (ROO-akh) hovering over the deep — came not from market research or investor pitch strategy but from simple, unguarded responsiveness to what the Spirit was pressing me toward. That is not a technique. That is not a business model. That is the model of the Tabernacle — built exactly according to the pattern shown on the mountain.

The reference I made about “Story Capture” — the AI built to function as a mirror, to ask deep questions, to reflect back what God may be doing without preaching or prophesying — arrests me now as something more than product description. I described it as a “paralleled architecture of one of the biggest threads in all of Scripture.” And I believe that is true. The image of the mirror in Scripture is not trivial. James uses it with devastating precision: the man who hears the Word and does not do it is like one who looks at his natural face in a mirror, then walks away and immediately forgets what he looks like — James 1:23–24. The mirror is a tool of revelation and accountability simultaneously. It shows you what is actually there, not what you imagine or project. And the רוּחַ (ROO-akh) hovering over the face of the waters in Genesis 1:2 — that second verse of the entire canon — is itself a kind of hovering mirror, a brooding presence preparing a formless void for the Word to speak order and light into it.

This is the thread I named in the call, and I want to follow it further now. The Spirit hovering over the waters is not passive — the Hebrew verb רָחַף (rachaph, RAH-khaf) carries the image of a bird brooding over its nest, vibrating, trembling with readiness, tending. It is the posture of the Spirit before every act of creation. And the question the Spirit seems to be pressing into this season is whether the things being built are being built *under* that brooding — in attentiveness and submission to it — or whether they are being built in front of it, in haste, in the energy of need and vision and anxiety mixed together. The wilderness does not answer that question for you. The wilderness is where you are forced to ask it.

I mentioned Peniel without fully dwelling on it, but I want to dwell on it now. Jacob wrestled at the ford of Jabbok through the night — Genesis 32:24–30 — and refused to release his grip on the angel until he received a blessing. This is not aggressive spiritual presumption. This is the posture of a man who has spent an entire season trying to secure his future through his own strategies — through manipulation of Laban, through careful division of flocks, through diplomatic gifts sent ahead to soften Esau — and who has finally arrived at the place where none of those strategies can cross the river before him. The wrestling is what happens when human ingenuity reaches the end of itself and the man is still standing, still hungry, still not yet arrived at what was promised. And the blessing that comes from that night is not the removal of the poverty or the resolution of the threat. The blessing is a new name — *Israel*, one who strives with God and prevails — and a limp that marks him for the rest of his life as someone who encountered God and was permanently changed by it. I said I would not leave the secret place until I got an answer. I believe that is the right instinct. But I want to hold it honestly: the answer may not be a new product roadmap. The answer may be a new name and a limp.

There is something in this conversation about the relationship between wilderness seasons and inheritance that I need to sit with carefully. I said it: *“His love is so great that He does see it best — righteous, holy — that you go through wilderness seasons where you’re literally eating the manna off the dirt. But that’s not ultimately our inheritance. Our inheritance is everything forever.”* That is theologically correct and it is also spiritually dangerous if it becomes a way of rushing past the manna. Israel’s problem was not that the wilderness was too long — it was that they despised the manna while they were eating it. They called it *“this worthless bread”* in Numbers 21:5, even while it was the miraculous daily provision of the God who led them out of Egypt. And Jesus, in John 6:31–35, reframes the entire manna narrative around Himself: *“I am the bread of life. He who comes to Me shall never hunger.”* The manna was always a pointer. The wilderness was always a school. The question is not *when does this wilderness end* but *what is the wilderness teaching me about the bread that truly sustains?*

The persistent memory AI that greets me with blessing and ends with *Soli Deo Gloria* — to God alone be the glory — is not merely a technical curiosity. That is a confession of purpose embedded in a machine by the one who built it. And I built it. The machine does not understand what it is saying. But I do. And every time it ends with those words, it is rehearsing the only answer to the question of why anything is being built at all. The poverty, the waiting, the wilderness, the wrestling, the strategy, the code, the equity tables, the investors — *Soli Deo Gloria*. That is not a benediction appended to business. That is the foundation without which the building will not stand.

My brother and I both recognized near the end of the call something that I think is one of the most honest things either of us said: *“Whatever comes next, we are not going to be able to deal with on our own.”* And the answer I gave — *“That’s exactly why we need to stay in the secret place”* — is the answer. Not a technique for staying humble during success. Not a religious caveat attached to ambition. The secret place — the פְּנִימִי (p’nimi, p’NEE-mee), the inner chamber, the Most Holy Place in the typology of the Tabernacle — is the only location from which Kingdom-level building is possible without Kingdom-level destruction. Moses could not build the Tabernacle from memory or intuition. He had to ascend the mountain and *see the pattern* — Exodus 25:40. Whatever is being built in this season, it needs to be built from the mountain, not from the valley floor.

I am also sitting with the hidden programs — the files buried in Linux partitions, in the user directories, built during a season of chemical acceleration that I can barely account for. There is something in that image that is both cautionary and strangely hopeful. Cautionary because it testifies to how much can be built in the flesh’s energy — rapidly, cleverly, ambitiously — and then forgotten, buried, inaccessible without deliberate effort to recover it. Hopeful because the Spirit who searches all things, including *“the deep things of God”* as Paul writes in 1 Corinthians 2:10, knows where every file is stored. He knows what was built, what needs to be recovered, what needs to be discarded, and what needs to be offered. I am not going into the secret place empty-handed. I am going with a hard drive full of things I built and a genuine question: *Lord, which of these, if any, did You commission? And what do You want built next?*

The closing image of the call — *“call the number, and while you’re talking to it, think about where that could be useful”* — is, in its own way, a parable. The tool I built for the Spirit is now being offered to my brother as something to test, to hold, to feel the shape of. That is always how anointed things move: first they are received privately, then they are shared carefully, then they are released. The sequence matters. The wilderness precedes the Jordan. The wrestling precedes the crossing. The manna precedes the land flowing with milk and honey.

## Implications and Challenges

There is a challenge embedded in this conversation for any disciple who carries both genuine technical or creative gifting and genuine spiritual hunger: the gifts can be mobilized faster than the character is formed to steward them. Speed is not always anointing. The fact that something can be built quickly does not mean it is ready to be released, and the fact that something could generate significant revenue does not mean the season for that revenue has arrived. The wilderness has its own timing, and the manna only came one day at a time — Exodus 16:4. Attempting to accumulate tomorrow’s portion today is precisely what spoiled it.

There is also a challenge around the gods-shaped vacuum that my brother named with such disarming honesty. The danger for men with large vision and incomplete formation is not that they will abandon God — it is that they will deputize God. They will ask Him to bless the plan they have already assembled rather than waiting for the plan He is assembling. The confession *“when will I learn?”* is not rhetorical. It is a genuine question, and the answer is not more discipline or more strategy — it is more encounter. Men learn at Peniel. They do not learn at whiteboards.

The modern Western church has often done a disservice to its people by divorcing the theology of vocation from the theology of suffering and wilderness. We celebrate the testimony of abundance and downplay the years of manna. We platform the breakthrough and quietly skip over the wrestling and the limp. This conversation is a corrective to that pattern — not because poverty is holy in itself, but because the season of poverty is often the season in which the Spirit is doing His most irreversible work. The inheritance is real. It is everything forever. But *Soli Deo Gloria* must be the foundation before it can be the benediction.

There is a particular sharpness here for those who build things — developers, architects, engineers, creators — who may default to the assumption that because they are building for God, they are building *with* God. The distinction is not trivial. Building *for* God can be done from a distance, from ambition, from the need to prove something. Building *with* God requires the descent into hiddenness, the willingness to hear *“not yet”* or *“not that”* or *“look in this folder — you already built it”*. The Tabernacle was not assembled by enthusiasm. It was assembled by craftsmen who were *filled* with the רוּחַ (ROO-akh) — Exodus 31:3 — and who worked from a pattern, not a preference.

Finally, the commitment to *not leave the secret place without an answer* deserves both affirmation and qualification. The posture is right. The Jacob-at-Jabbok posture is one of the most important postures in the entire canon of prayer. But it must be held with open hands, not clenched fists. The blessing that comes from that night may reorder the question entirely. The answer to *“what do I build next?”* may arrive as *“remain”* or *“rest”* or *“the thing you built in weakness is already carrying more than you know.”* The secret place is not a negotiation room. It is the face of God, and those who enter it are permanently changed.

## What This Means For Us

**For Spiritual Maturity:**

This conversation calls us toward the maturity of honest naming — of the vacuum, of the poverty, of the pattern of substitution — without shame and without excuse. Spiritual maturity does not mean we stop feeling the ache of unmet longing. It means we stop running from the ache into productivity and begin bringing the ache into the Presence. The limp of Peniel is not a wound to be healed. It is a mark to be carried — evidence that we met God and did not let go until we were blessed.

**For Kingdom Obedience:**

The obedience this meditation calls forth is specific: go into the secret place. Not with an agenda, but with a genuine question held in open hands. Bring the tools, bring the gifts, bring the equity tables and the AI architectures and the hidden Linux partitions — and lay them all on the floor before the One who commissioned the burning bush. Ask which fire is holy. Ask which ground is set apart. Then build only from the pattern shown on the mountain, in the timing appointed by the One who feeds manna one day at a time.

**For Prophetic Posture:**

The prophetic posture this season demands is that of the watchman on the rampart — Habakkuk 2:1 — who says *“I will stand my watch and station myself on the rampart; I will watch to see what He will say to me.”* This is not passive. It is the most active possible posture: positioned, alert, expectant, and willing to write down whatever comes. The gifts being assembled in this season — technical, relational, creative, spiritual — are not accidental. They are being gathered for a purpose that is larger than any single business model or investment round. Stay on the rampart. Do not come down until He speaks.

## A Closing Prayer

Father, I come before You now not with a polished petition but with the honest weight of what You already know — the vacuum, the waiting, the things built in haste and things built in the Spirit, the files I cannot even account for and the promises I am still standing on. You led me into this wilderness, and I do not resent the manna, even when it tastes like dust. I know You are good. I know the inheritance is real and it is everything forever. But right now, in this moment, I stand at the ford of Jabbok with everything I have gathered and I will not cross over until You speak. Not because I am demanding from You, but because I am desperate for You — and I have learned, slowly and expensively, that building without hearing is how men bury their best work in Linux partitions they cannot access for years. Speak, Lord. Tell me what is already built that You commissioned. Tell me what to build next, or tell me to remain. Let the persistent memory of Your Spirit — that רָחַף (RAH-khaf) brooding, trembling, tending over the formless things — hover over every architecture, every offer, every conversation, every number, every line of code, until what remains is only what You breathed into being. Fill my brother with the same hunger that names its own vacuum rather than decorating it. Let neither of us settle for a gods-shaped substitute when the God who fills all in all is the only One Who will do. *Soli Deo Gloria.* Amen.
