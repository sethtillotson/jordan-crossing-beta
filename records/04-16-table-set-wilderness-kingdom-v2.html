# 04-16 Call Reflection: Kingdom Economics, Mammon, and the Cross as Economic Subversion **Title:** 04-16 at 12:09 — The Table Set in the Wilderness: Kingdom Economics and the Cross That Crucifies Mammon **Recorded:** Not provided **Source Discernment:** C — Recorded Conversation or Event (Live Capture) **Classification:** Call Reflection ## Opening Observation As I sat with this call long after it ended, what stayed with me was not the dispute amounts or the PayPal case — it was the moment the conversation shifted from business mechanics to something far more ancient. Somewhere between talking about the IRS threshold and the billions never realized from a technology that could have rewritten Silicon Valley, the Spirit pressed something unmistakable through the noise: that the Kingdom of God operates by an entirely different economy than the one the world calls “business.” What arrested me most was a single phrase that came out almost involuntarily — *“I’m going to bring them Christ”* — spoken in response to a consulting contract, as though that were the most natural bid to place on the table. And perhaps, in the Kingdom, it is. **Why This Matters:** This matters because the Body of Christ in the Western world has largely absorbed the assumptions of Mammon so thoroughly that most believers cannot even detect the difference between Kingdom economics and marketplace economics. We pray for financial breakthrough while never questioning the framework in which we define breakthrough. We chase contracts, valuations, and revenue systems while the Prince of the power of the air — the principality that animates Silicon Valley, financial markets, and global commercial infrastructure — goes entirely unnamed and uncontested in our boardrooms, our business plans, and our discipleship conversations. If the saints cannot learn to identify what belongs to Caesar and what belongs to God, if they cannot see the difference between a seat at the table of Mammon and a seat at the table the Lord prepares in the presence of their enemies, they will spend their entire lives licking crumbs from the side of a system that was never meant to satisfy them. This call, messy and casual as it was, cracked open something deeper than a \$600 dispute — it cracked open the question of what we are actually building, and for Whom. ## The Worldly Mind’s Interpretation From the natural mind’s vantage point, this is a straightforward business debrief: two partners managing the administrative fallout of a closed company, navigating a small PayPal dispute, lamenting lost valuations on a technology venture, and discussing a potential consulting opportunity. The natural mind hears this and evaluates it through the lens of risk management and opportunity cost — weighing the \$600 dispute against the effort to recover it, mourning the billion-dollar prototype that never reached market, and calculating whether a \$25,000 consulting bid is too low to be taken seriously. The natural mind finds the casual profanity unremarkable, the crypto loss unfortunate but survivable, and the mention of “bringing them Christ” perhaps endearing but commercially naive. It would counsel pragmatism — protect the brand, secure the contract, price it appropriately, move forward. The natural mind sees a series of business problems with business solutions and a few spiritual sentiments sprinkled in for color. **The Limitation:** But the natural mind cannot perceive what the Spirit is doing beneath the surface of this conversation, because what the Spirit is doing does not speak in the language of quarterly projections or valuation multiples. The carnal mind is constitutionally unable to receive the things of the Spirit of God — not because it lacks intelligence, but because they are spiritually discerned, and spiritual discernment requires a faculty the unrenewed mind simply does not possess. A man can calculate the enterprise value of a company to the dollar and still be entirely blind to the principality that owns the system in which that company operates. He can understand IRS reporting thresholds, Oracle-based algorithmic auditing, and offshore payment disputes with perfect clarity, and yet miss entirely the word Jesus spoke when He said to give to Caesar what is Caesar’s — a word that implies, first and foremost, that there are things which belong to God that Caesar must never be permitted to touch. The limitation here is not intellectual; it is spiritual. And until a man’s mind has been renewed by the washing of the Word, he will keep treating Caesar’s economy as the ceiling of his possibility and the Kingdom of God as a nice supplement to his business strategy, rather than the other way around. ## What the Spirit Reveals Through It As I listened to my brother walk me through the PayPal dispute, the first thing that struck me was the smallness of what the enemy had attempted — a \$600 skimming operation, run through a personal account, through a man who apparently could not conceive of a better way to make ends meet than to defraud the partners who had given him a seat at a legitimate table. And I heard myself say it without fully preaching it in the moment: *“Come eat at the table with me. Like, I don’t care. Are you going to come to the table and eat, or are you going to lick up the scraps by the side?”* The Spirit was already moving in that image before I could articulate what it meant theologically. The image is older than I am. In Psalm 23, David does not say that the Lord will remove his enemies before He feeds him — he says that the Lord *prepares a table in the presence of his enemies.* The meal happens while the opposition watches. The abundance is not contingent on the conflict being resolved first. And what I am beginning to see is that Abbas — whomever he is and whatever he did — was not ultimately a financial problem; he was a man who chose to eat crumbs beside a table that had already been set for him. That is the tragedy the world calls fraud and the Kingdom calls the Prodigal in the far country, wishing he could eat what the pigs were eating while his father’s table sat full and waiting at home. I am not dismissing the legal reality of a breach of contract. Contracts exist because people are fallen, and structures of accountability are not unchristian — they are part of the common grace God extends to a world He has not yet fully redeemed. But what the Spirit will not let me do is mistake the PayPal dispute for the real story. The real story is about a man who could not trust that there was enough, so he took something small by crooked means and called it survival. And that is the spirit of Mammon — not merely greed in the cartoonish sense, but the deep, structural belief that the world’s resources are scarce and must be seized rather than received. Jesus called it by its name: μαμωνᾶς (mamōnas, mah-mo-NAS) — an Aramaic term for wealth personified, treated as a master, a lord, a rival god. He did not say you *should not* try to serve both God and Mammon. He said you *cannot.* The impossibility is built into the architecture of devotion. This is where the crypto conversation opens something I was not expecting to find in a casual business call. I listened to my brother describe the loss — one point three million dollars, made through DeFi markets in weeks, then taken through a hack — and I watched my own memory of that season surface with remarkable sobriety. I did not lose that money to bad trading or reckless speculation. I hid it from a system I correctly identified as extractive and then lost it to a different kind of theft. And what I said in the call is honest: *“Nobody actually owns anything. The people who run the world and answer to Baal own everything.”* That is not cynicism. That is canonical. The whole earth is the LORD’s, and the fullness thereof — Psalm 24:1. The cattle on a thousand hills are His — Psalm 50:10. When Jesus told the disciples that even the hairs of their heads were numbered, He was not offering a therapeutic comfort; He was announcing the absolute sovereignty of the Father over every material thing that appears to be under Caesar’s jurisdiction. What the Spirit is pressing deeper in me as I sit with this is that Jesus’s famous statement — *“Render therefore to Caesar the things that are Caesar’s, and to God the things that are God’s”* (Matthew 22:21, NKJV) — was not a compromise between two legitimate competing claims. It was a devastating precision cut. The Pharisees had brought Him a coin. He asked whose image and inscription was on it. They said Caesar’s. And He said: give Caesar back his coin. But implicit in that answer is a second question that He did not ask aloud but that every listening ear should have been asking: *Whose image is on you?* The coin bears Caesar’s image and therefore belongs to Caesar’s economy. The human being bears the image of God — צֶלֶם (tselem, TSEH-lem), the imago Dei stamped at creation — and therefore belongs entirely to God’s economy. The conflict is not between money and religion. The conflict is between two kingdoms making competing claims on the image-bearer. This is precisely why the technology conversation — the Symbiote AI, the prototype, the billion-dollar valuation never realized — hit me differently when I returned to it in prayer. On the surface, it sounds like a business failure, a missed exit, a patent never filed. But I said something in the call that the Spirit keeps surfacing: *“You know what Christ would have done? You put it on a cross. You put it on a cross because the cross destroys all worldly framework.”* I was speaking about open-sourcing the technology, releasing it to the academic world without monetizing it, stripping the principality of Silicon Valley of its commercial hold over the invention. And I stand by that instinct — not because poverty is holy, but because the cross is the ultimate act of economic subversion in the history of the universe. At Calvary, the Son of God took what the enemy believed was a victory — the death of the King — and turned it into the enemy’s total and permanent defeat. The principality expected to hold the invention. The cross said: you can have it for free, and in giving it away, we take the kingdom from you. The word that keeps pressing on me is the one Paul uses in Colossians 2:15 when he describes the cross: ἀπεκδυσάμενος (apekdusamenos, ah-pek-doo-SAH-men-os) — a stripping, a disarming, a public triumphal procession over the defeated powers. The cross did not merely atone for sin. It *disarmed* principalities and powers and made a public spectacle of them. And every time the people of God take something the world system assigns monetary value and lay it down as an offering — releasing it freely, refusing to let Mammon name its price — they are participating in that same disarming. Silicon Valley is a principality. Its power is maintained by intellectual property law, proprietary licensing, venture capital dependency, and the worship of scale as inherently good. Open-sourcing a billion-dollar concept does not destroy the technology. It destroys the principality’s claim over it. And then came the moment that crystallized everything. My brother offered the consulting contract — revenue infrastructure, twenty-five thousand dollars for the initial engagement, private sector, potentially far more on retainer. And my response was not to negotiate the number upward. It was: *“I’m going to bring them Christ.”* I heard myself say it, and I heard my brother catch on immediately — *“Think bigger.”* Because that is what it is. That is not naive spirituality. That is the most audacious possible bid on a consulting contract: I am not primarily coming to redesign your revenue infrastructure. I am coming as an ambassador of the Kingdom, and what the Kingdom builds does not depreciate, cannot be hacked, and does not require a PayPal dispute team to recover when someone takes a shortcut through a personal account. This does not mean I will refuse the \$25,000. It means the \$25,000 is not the point of the meeting. Paul made tents — Acts 18:3 — but Paul’s tent-making was not his mission; it was the container that held his presence in a city long enough for the Word to take root. The work funded the witness. But the work was never the goal, and the moment it became the goal, it became a distraction from the assignment. Jesus told His disciples not to worry about what they would eat or drink or wear — not because food and clothing are irrelevant, but because the Father already knew they needed them, and the children of the Kingdom were meant to seek the Kingdom first and trust that everything else would be added (Matthew 6:31–33, NKJV). The consulting contract may well be a door. But I will walk through it as the Kingdom walking through it, not as a revenue consultant who happens to be a Christian. What I keep returning to is the phrase I used almost in passing — *“violently taking territory from the kingdom of darkness.”* The Greek of Matthew 11:12 uses βιαστής (biastēs, bee-ah-STACE) and the verb βιάζεται (biazetai, bee-AH-zeh-tie) — forceful pressing, violent urgency — to describe the way the Kingdom of Heaven has been advancing since the days of John the Baptist. This is not the language of passive spiritual drift. This is the language of deliberate, sustained, spiritually violent advance into enemy-held territory. And in the context of this call, the enemy’s territory is not Abbas’s personal PayPal account — it is the entire commercial and financial framework that tells image-bearers that their worth is denominated in dollars, that their inventions belong to capital, and that their table is set only as large as their exit valuation allows. The Kingdom says otherwise. The Kingdom says that a man who can look at a \$600 dispute and say *“Go work two days of construction and make it back”* — and mean it — is a man who has begun to be freed from Mammon’s grip. The Kingdom says that a man who can look at a billion-dollar prototype and say *“Put it on the cross”* — and mean it — is a man who has begun to understand that the cross is not a defeat; it is the most generative act in the cosmos. And the Kingdom says that a man who can receive a consulting opportunity and say *“I’m going to bring them Christ”* — and mean it — is a man who has finally stopped calculating the bid and started making the offer the Kingdom has always been authorized to make. ## Implications and Challenges The first challenge this meditation presses is the one the Western church most urgently needs to hear: **we have confused financial blessing with Kingdom assignment.** The prosperity frameworks that dominate much of contemporary Christianity have taught the saints to expect that faithfulness produces wealth, without ever asking whether the wealth being produced belongs to Caesar’s economy or God’s. A billion-dollar valuation is not inherently righteous, and a cross-shaped release of intellectual property is not inherently foolish. The question is always: whose image is on it, and to whom does it ultimately belong? Until the church recovers a theology of imago Dei (TSEH-lem) that extends into economics, she will keep praying for breakthrough inside a system she has never named as a principality. The second challenge is more personal and more precise: **many of us have hidden things from Caesar that we should have simply offered to God.** I am not advocating tax evasion or financial irresponsibility — the transcript itself acknowledges the structural problems created by mixing personal and business accounts. But the deeper pattern I recognize in my own story is that I tried to protect wealth from a system I rightly identified as extractive, without ever actually surrendering it to the only One who could have protected it legitimately. The Proverb says the wealth of the sinner is laid up for the righteous — Proverbs 13:22, NKJV — but that transfer happens through the Lord’s economy, not through concealment in ours. Whatever we grip to protect from Caesar, we often lose anyway. Whatever we lay down before the Lord, He can multiply or redistribute or sanctify according to His purposes. Third, the church needs to recover a **theology of the table** that does not merely mean communion on Sunday. The table the Lord prepares in the presence of enemies is a table of abundance set deliberately in contested space. It is not a private spiritual experience; it is a public, visible declaration that the Kingdom has arrived in this territory and has resources the enemy cannot touch. When I offered the invitation to “come eat at the table with me” to someone who had been skimming crumbs from a dispute process, that was not sentimentality — it was the posture of the Kingdom, which always prefers restoration over litigation and always prefers feeding over defeating. The challenge for us is whether we actually believe the table is full enough to keep extending that invitation, or whether Mammon has quietly convinced us that generosity is a luxury we can afford only after the dispute is settled. Fourth, **the principalities named in this call — Baal, the prince of Silicon Valley, the spirit of Mammon — are not metaphors to be politely acknowledged and then set aside.** Paul tells us in Ephesians 6:12 that our wrestling is not against flesh and blood but against ἀρχάς (archas, ar-KHACE) — principalities — and against ἐξουσίας (exousias, ex-oo-SEE-as) — powers — against the rulers of the darkness of this age. Abbas is not the enemy. The Romanian clients are not the enemy. Aaron Shergill is not the enemy. The people who built their ontology networks and called them general intelligence are not the enemy. They are all, in different ways, image-bearers living under the compulsion of systems that neither they nor we fully see. The enemy is the principality. And the weapons of our warfare are not carnal — not IRS filings, not PayPal escalation, not intellectual property law, not even a well-priced consulting bid — but mighty in God for pulling down strongholds (2 Corinthians 10:4, NKJV). Fifth, the consulting opportunity deserves its own sober word: **there is a version of “bringing them Christ” that is genuine apostolic penetration into a marketplace assignment, and there is a version that is spiritual language used to avoid the discipline of professional preparation.** The call itself is careful enough to say, “Send me what you’ve got and let me look at it first.” That is wisdom. The Kingdom does not bypass competence; it sanctifies it. Paul’s tent-making was excellent tent-making. My revenue infrastructure consulting, if I walk into that room, should be excellent revenue infrastructure consulting — AND it should carry the weight and authority of the One who sent me. The challenge is not to choose between professional excellence and spiritual mission, but to refuse to let either become a substitute for the other. ## What This Means For Us **For Spiritual Maturity:** Spiritual maturity in the economic realm does not mean taking a vow of poverty or refusing to build businesses and pursue contracts. It means learning to hold every material thing with an open hand — knowing that the image stamped on me belongs to God, that whatever I build belongs to God, and that the moment I begin structuring my life to protect what I have accumulated from the systems that would tax or seize it, I have begun to serve a second master. Maturity looks like a man who can say “water under the bridge” about \$600 and mean it — not because \$600 is nothing, but because his table is set by One who does not run short. It looks like a man who can say “put it on a cross” about a billion-dollar invention and mean it — not because excellence doesn’t matter, but because the cross is the most powerful generative force in creation. **For Kingdom Obedience:** The obedience this meditation calls forth is threefold. First, name the principalities. Stop calling them “market forces” or “institutional friction” or “bad luck.” Name what Jesus named — Mammon — and refuse it lordship. Second, keep extending the table. The invitation to come eat is not a negotiating position; it is the posture of the Kingdom, and it must be maintained even when the person we are inviting has been skimming from the account. Third, go into the consulting rooms, the contract opportunities, the revenue infrastructure conversations — and go as ambassadors. Not as Christians who happen to do business, but as sons and daughters of the King who have been sent into occupied territory with full authority to make an offer the kingdom of darkness cannot match. **For Prophetic Posture:** The prophetic posture this call cultivates is the posture of one who is “violently taking territory” — not in aggression toward people, but in sustained, deliberate, Spirit-directed advance against the systems and principalities that hold image-bearers captive to Mammon’s framework. This means learning to discern the difference between a business opportunity and a Kingdom assignment — and then walking into both with the same authority. It means learning to abide in the certainty that the Lord’s table is already set, that the enemy is already watching, and that the meal does not begin when the dispute is resolved — the meal *is* the testimony that the dispute does not define us. Watch. Wait. Yield to the Spirit’s discernment before placing a bid, signing a contract, or releasing a document. And remain faithful to the One whose image you bear, even in the rooms where Caesar assumes he owns the conversation. ## A Closing Prayer Father, I come before You as one who has seen the table You prepare and has sometimes chosen to crouch beside it, calculating the cost of the seat rather than simply accepting the invitation. I thank You that Your economy is not governed by the IRS threshold or the PayPal dispute team or the valuation multiple or the patent timeline — it is governed by Your sovereign and inexhaustible generosity, and by the finished work of the cross that has already disarmed every principality that has ever tried to put a price on what You have freely given. Lord, I ask You to keep my hands open — to hold the contracts, the inventions, the disputes, and the dollars with the looseness of a man who knows that the image on him belongs entirely to You. Sanctify every room I walk into as a consulting assignment into Kingdom territory. Let the authority of the One who sent me precede my professional competence, and let my competence honor the excellence of the Kingdom You are building. Where I have hidden things from systems in fear rather than releasing them in faith, forgive me and restore what the enemy has taken. Where the saints around me are still licking crumbs beside a table that is already set, give us the courage and the abundance to keep extending the invitation — *come eat with me* — until the prodigals come home and the principalities are publicly disarmed. I make agreement with Your will, Lord, as one of Your servants, between the heavens and the earth: this territory — commercial, intellectual, financial, relational — belongs to You, and we will not stop advancing until every stronghold has been pulled down and every image-bearer has been given a seat at the table You prepared before the foundations of the world. To the King eternal, immortal, invisible, the only wise God — be honor and glory forever and ever. Amen. ## Scripture References & Original Language Notes | **Passage (NKJV)** | **Original Language** | **Theological Insight** | |-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------| | Psalm 23:5 — “You prepare a table before me in the presence of my enemies” | עָרַךְ (arakh, ah-RAKH) — to arrange, set in order | The table is not prepared after the enemies depart; it is set while they watch.
