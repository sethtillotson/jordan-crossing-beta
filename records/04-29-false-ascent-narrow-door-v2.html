# 04-29 Call Reflection: Psychedelics, Counterfeit Transcendence, and Discernment in Christ **Title:** 04-29 at 18:27 — The False Ascent and the Narrow Door **Recorded:** 2026-04-29 18:27:47 **Source Discernment:** C — Recorded Conversation or Event (Live Capture) **Classification:** Call Reflection ## Opening Observation As I sit with this conversation, I am struck by the spiritual hunger underneath it. On the surface, it was a late-night exchange about psychedelics, altered states, DMT, mushrooms, tunnels, entities, and “transcendent experiences,” but beneath all of that I hear the ache of human beings trying to pierce the veil without surrendering to the One who tore the veil. I am seeing again how easily counterfeit transcendence can imitate mystery while bypassing holiness, repentance, and covenant. **Why This Matters:** This matters because our generation is not irreligious; it is deeply spiritual, but often spiritually unsubmitted. Many are not asking whether unseen realms exist anymore; they are asking how to access them. The question is no longer merely, “Is there more than the material world?” but “Can I enter the more on my own terms?” And this is where discipleship becomes urgent. Scripture does not deny the existence of spiritual realms, visions, beings, signs, wonders, heavenly encounters, or unseen realities. It testifies to them repeatedly. But Scripture also makes a holy distinction between revelation received in covenant obedience and forbidden access pursued through intoxication, sorcery, divination, or manipulation. The Body of Christ must learn how to speak to this hunger with sobriety and compassion, not with mockery, fear, or shallow dismissal. People are reaching for transcendence because they were made for God. But the Father does not call His children to chemical doors, astral projections, or counterfeit heavens; He calls us through the Son, by the Spirit, into communion, truth, holiness, and life. ## The Worldly Mind’s Interpretation The worldly mind would likely interpret this conversation through psychology, neurochemistry, and shared cultural language around psychedelics. It might say that DMT causes intense visual hallucinations, ego dissolution, tunnel imagery, archetypal encounters, and altered perceptions of space, time, and self. It might explain the “entities” as projections of the subconscious, the fractal patterns as brain activity under chemical influence, and the sense of transcendence as the nervous system interpreting overwhelming stimulation as spiritual encounter. It might even see the careful planning of trips, the hiding of substances, and the need for mutual agreement as an attempt to create safety, ritual, consent, or meaning around experiences that felt too powerful to approach casually. **The Limitation:** But natural reasoning cannot fully account for the spiritual danger of seeking revelation apart from submission to God. It can describe the brain under the influence, but it cannot discern whether a door has been opened that should have remained shut. It can analyze patterns, images, and emotional effects, but it cannot judge spirits by the confession of Christ, nor can it distinguish true glory from deceptive light. The natural mind may recognize that something profound is happening, but without the fear of the Lord, it cannot properly ask whether that profundity is holy. Not every unveiled thing is from God. Not every spiritual experience is revelation. Not every “transcendent” encounter is ascent. Some heights are actually falls, and some tunnels are not passages into truth but invitations into bondage. ## What the Spirit Reveals Through It What arrests me first is the language of ascent. The conversation moved through images of the body passing out while consciousness continues, fractal tunnels, dimensions, hyperspace, sky dragons, entities speaking, and experiences described as being “pretty much God.” I do not want to flatten that into mere nonsense, because Scripture itself presents reality as layered with visible and invisible dimensions. Elisha prayed, “LORD, I pray, open his eyes that he may see” (2 Kings 6:17, NKJV), and the servant saw the mountain full of horses and chariots of fire. Ezekiel saw wheels within wheels. John was “in the Spirit” on the Lord’s Day and was shown heavenly realities. Paul spoke of being caught up to the third heaven, though he refused to boast in it as a spiritual credential. The issue is not whether unseen realities exist. The issue is who opens the eyes, who initiates the encounter, and under whose lordship the revelation comes. I am seeing that Scripture never treats access to the unseen as neutral. There are holy unveilings and forbidden intrusions. The Greek word φαρμακεία (pharmakeia, far-mah-KAY-ah) — sorcery / drug-associated occult practice — appears among the works of the flesh in Galatians 5:20 and in Revelation’s warnings against the nations being deceived. That word does not mean every medicine is evil, nor should it be weaponized carelessly against legitimate healing. But it does name a spiritual pattern where substances, enchantments, and occult practice mingle as a means of power, manipulation, or illicit access. In the ancient world, the line between drug use, spellcraft, and spiritual control was often intertwined. That matters here because the conversation was not merely about having strange visuals; it was about intentionally engineering transcendent experiences. Something in me trembles at the phrase “we premeditated our trips to make sure we had transcendent experiences.” That sentence exposes a central counterfeit: manufactured transcendence. In Scripture, the holy is never something man manufactures. Fire falls from heaven on God’s terms. The cloud fills the temple by God’s presence. The Spirit descends as gift, not technique. Even when priests prepare, prophets wait, apostles pray, and saints fast, they do not control the glory. They posture themselves in obedience. They do not seize the veil; they are invited through Christ. The counterfeit says, “If I combine the right substance, setting, dosage, timing, and ritual, I can enter.” The Kingdom says, “No one comes to the Father except through Me” (John 14:6, NKJV). The conversation also carried a strange echo of biblical language: “when two or three come together as witnesses,” “casting lots,” “divine intervention,” “confirmation.” This is where I feel the fear of the Lord. Holy language can be borrowed to sanctify unholy practice. The fact that two people agree does not make a thing righteous. Agreement has power, but power must be submitted to truth. Jesus said, “For where two or three are gathered together in My name, I am there in the midst of them” (Matthew 18:20, NKJV). The center is not the number; the center is His name. Likewise, casting lots in Scripture was not a game of self-willed desire dressed up as providence. It was used in reverence before God, and even that practice fades in the New Covenant as the Spirit is poured out to lead the people of God. Agreement without holiness can become conspiracy. Confirmation without surrender can become confirmation bias with spiritual vocabulary. I notice, too, that the hidden drugs became a kind of covenant object. Critical pieces of information were divided between two people so the substances could only be recovered through mutual agreement. On one level, that sounds like an attempt to restrain the flesh. On another level, it reveals how deeply ritualized the bondage had become. The drugs were not simply discarded; they were hidden in a way that preserved possibility. The door was not closed; it was made harder to open. This is one of the great subtleties of sin: sometimes we do not repent by destroying the altar; we only move it into a harder-to-reach room. But when Josiah tore down the high places, he did not label them “for later discernment.” He defiled them, broke them, burned them, and removed their priests. True repentance does not merely make sin less convenient. It renounces the covenant. And yet I also hear mercy. The conversation included the line that the drugs were never found again and that it became the last time psychedelics were used. I do not want to overstate what was not fully said, but I do see a mercy pattern there. Sometimes God lets the thing become inaccessible before our hearts are fully mature enough to hate it. Sometimes He interrupts the cycle before our theology can explain the deliverance. Israel did not fully understand the wilderness when they first left Egypt, but the blood was already on the doorposts, the sea had already opened, and Pharaoh’s army was already drowning behind them. Deliverance often begins as escape before it becomes understanding. The deeper issue is hunger. People seek DMT, mushrooms, and altered states because they want contact with something beyond ordinary life. They want awe. They want unveiled reality. They want to know if consciousness is more than flesh. They want to meet intelligence beyond themselves. They want healing, meaning, initiation, visitation, and sometimes power. But the hunger is not evil in itself; it is displaced worship. Ecclesiastes says God “has put eternity in their hearts” (Ecclesiastes 3:11, NKJV). The tragedy is that eternity in the heart can become torment when it is not brought home to the Eternal One. The soul made for God will either worship Him or wander through substitutes. This is where I am pressed to remember that Christianity is not the denial of mystical reality; it is the purification and fulfillment of it in Christ. Biblical faith is more supernatural than the psychedelic seeker imagines, but it is also more holy than the seeker often desires. We do not need to pretend the Christian life is flat, safe, and merely moral. We are indwelt by the Holy Spirit. We are seated with Christ in the heavenly places. We have access by one Spirit to the Father. Angels are ministering spirits sent forth for those who will inherit salvation. The gifts of the Spirit are real. Dreams, visions, prophecy, discernment, healings, deliverance, and divine encounters remain under the lordship of Jesus. But none of this is accessed by intoxicated self-initiation. It is received through covenant union with Christ. The Greek word μυστήριον (mystērion, moos-TAY-ree-on) — sacred mystery once hidden, now revealed by God — helps me here. The New Testament does not present mystery as something the spiritually adventurous break into by technique. It presents mystery as something God reveals in Christ. Paul speaks of “the mystery which has been hidden from ages and from generations, but now has been revealed to His saints” (Colossians 1:26, NKJV). The center of the mystery is not a tunnel, an entity, a dragon, a dimension, or an experience of disembodied consciousness. The mystery is “Christ in you, the hope of glory” (Colossians 1:27, NKJV). That is the true inner unveiling. Not escape from the body into a counterfeit heaven, but the risen Christ dwelling in redeemed bodies by the Holy Spirit. There is also a counterfeit death and resurrection pattern in these experiences. The body “passes out,” consciousness “keeps going,” and the person comes back with a story. But biblical resurrection is not an altered-state simulation. Jesus did not merely have a consciousness journey; He died in the flesh, was buried, and rose bodily in glory. The Christian hope is not to abandon creation but to see creation redeemed. This is why drug-induced transcendence can subtly train the soul to despise ordinary embodied faithfulness. But the Lord meets us in bread, water, oil, tears, hands laid on the sick, feet washed, bodies presented as living sacrifices, and saints gathered in love. The Kingdom does not bypass the body; it redeems it. As I think about the “tunnel” imagery, I cannot help but contrast it with the biblical “way.” Jesus does not say, “I will show you a passage into hidden realms.” He says, “I am the way, the truth, and the life” (John 14:6, NKJV). The way is a Person. The door is a Person. The ladder between heaven and earth is fulfilled in the Son of Man, upon whom angels ascend and descend. Jacob saw a ladder in a dream and called the place Bethel, the house of God. Jesus later reveals Himself as the true meeting place of heaven and earth. So the question is not whether there is a passage; the question is whether I am entering through Christ or climbing up another way. The Hebrew word קָדוֹשׁ (qadosh, kah-DOHSH) — holy / set apart — clarifies the difference. God is not merely intense, luminous, strange, or overwhelming. He is holy. Many experiences can feel vast. Many spirits can feel ancient. Many visions can feel intelligent. But holiness has a distinct fragrance: it exalts Jesus Christ, produces repentance, bears the fruit of the Spirit, deepens love, humbles the soul, honors Scripture, and leads to obedience. The Holy Spirit does not merely make me feel expanded; He makes me conformed to the image of the Son. If an encounter leaves me fascinated but not surrendered, amazed but not sanctified, initiated but not obedient, I must test it. This conversation also makes me grieve for how often the Church has failed to present the true supernatural life in Christ. When believers reduce Christianity to attendance, ethics, cultural identity, or intellectual agreement, spiritually hungry people look elsewhere for fire. But the answer is not to imitate the counterfeit. The answer is to return to the upper room, the altar, the Word, the table, the secret place, the fellowship of the saints, and the fear of the Lord. We need a Church that can say both, “Yes, the unseen realm is real,” and, “No, you may not enter it lawlessly.” We need fathers and mothers who know how to shepherd people out of occult curiosity and into Spirit-filled obedience. I am also sobered by the mercy embedded in the ending of the call: “God bless.” Even after discussing distorted spiritual pursuits, intoxication, and past practices, the conversation ended with blessing. That does not make everything clean, but it does reveal a seed of orientation toward God. The Lord often begins with a small confession before He brings full consecration. A person may still be tangled, unclear, immature, or compromised, yet the name of God is not absent from their mouth. The pastoral question becomes: will that blessing become surrender? Will the language of God become obedience to God? Will spiritual curiosity become discipleship under Jesus? As I sit with all of this, I am not left with condemnation but with urgency. The veil has already been torn. The blood of Jesus has opened “a new and living way” through the veil, that is, His flesh (Hebrews 10:20, NKJV). That means I do not need to force access. I do not need to chemically rupture perception. I do not need to chase entities, tunnels, or forbidden visions. The Father has given me the Son, and the Son has poured out the Spirit. The invitation is not to escape into altered consciousness but to awaken into union with Christ. The true miracle is not that my consciousness can travel somewhere strange; it is that the Holy God would make His dwelling in me. ## Implications and Challenges Grace must lead this reflection, because many who have used psychedelics were not simply chasing rebellion; they were chasing healing, awe, relief, or proof that reality is more than pain. We must not speak as though every wounded seeker is knowingly worshiping darkness. But grace also tells the truth: spiritual hunger does not justify forbidden doors. Compassion does not require naivety. This challenges the modern Western habit of treating all “spiritual experiences” as equally valid if they feel meaningful. Scripture commands discernment. A profound experience can still be deceptive. An encounter can feel loving and still lead away from Christ. A vision can feel ancient and still be unclean. The test is not intensity but lordship, fruit, confession, holiness, and fidelity to the Word. This also challenges shallow Christian supernaturalism. If we only warn people against counterfeit experiences but never teach them the real inheritance of life in the Spirit, we leave them spiritually underfed. The answer to lawless mysticism is not dead religion. The answer is holy fire, biblical discernment, and intimate communion with the living God. It confronts the temptation to borrow biblical language to baptize fleshly desire. “Two or three witnesses,” “confirmation,” “casting lots,” and “divine timing” can all become dangerous phrases when detached from surrender. God’s language must not be used as decoration on self-will. The name of the Lord is holy. It also calls for real repentance, not merely risk management. Hiding access to sin is not the same as destroying agreement with sin. Making bondage harder to reach may be a mercy step, but the mature work is renunciation. The altar must come down, not merely be relocated. And finally, this reflection invites hope. The same person who once chased false transcendence can become a witness of true communion. The same hunger that wandered into danger can be purified into prayer. The same longing for hidden reality can be fulfilled in Christ, in whom are hidden all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge. ## What This Means For Us **For Spiritual Maturity:** We must learn to distinguish between hunger and holiness, between awe and truth, between spiritual experience and Spirit-led revelation. Maturity means we do not deny the unseen realm, but we also do not approach it lawlessly. We grow by submitting every desire for encounter to Jesus Christ, the Word made flesh. **For Kingdom Obedience:** This calls us to renounce counterfeit doors and receive the access already given in the New Covenant. We do not need manufactured transcendence. We need prayer, repentance, Scripture, fellowship, worship, fasting, obedience, and the filling of the Holy Spirit. The narrow door is not less supernatural; it is the only holy way in. **For Prophetic Posture:** The saints must become sober, discerning, and compassionate witnesses in a spiritually intoxicated age. We must test the spirits, abide in Christ, refuse sensationalism, and speak with clarity to those who have encountered real darkness under the name of enlightenment. Prophetic posture is not fascination with hidden things; it is faithfulness to what God reveals. ## A Closing Prayer Father, in the name of Jesus Christ, I thank You that the veil has been torn by the flesh of Your Son and that we do not have to claw our way into the heavens by counterfeit doors. I come before You as Your servant, as a co-heir with Christ, as one seated with Him by grace and yet bowed low in holy fear. Cleanse us from every agreement with sorcery, intoxicated spirituality, false transcendence, and curiosity that is not submitted to Your lordship. Make us a people of the open heaven that rests upon Jesus, not a people chasing tunnels, entities, or forbidden fire. I agree with Your will between heaven and earth: let the secrets You entrust to Your servants produce repentance, holiness, testimony, and love. Deliver the hungry from deception, heal the wounded who sought relief in dangerous places, and raise up Your Church as a dwelling place of true fire, true wisdom, and true communion. May Jesus Christ be exalted as the Way, the Truth, and the Life, and may every false light bow before the glory of His face.
