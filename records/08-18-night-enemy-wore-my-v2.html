# 08-18 Call Reflection: Spiritual Warfare and the Refining of Pride **Title:** 08-18 at 15:08 — The Night the Enemy Wore My Brother’s Voice, and the Seat I Sat On for Ninety Seconds **Recorded:** 2026-08-18 15:08 **Source Discernment:** C — Recorded Conversation or Event (Live Capture) **Classification:** Call Reflection ## Opening Observation I am still shaking as I speak to my brother Samuel across the phone line, and I want to be precise about why. It is not the growling that unsettles me, not the threats against Samuel’s throat or my son, not the three black shapes I was begged to confirm, not the voice that would not flee the name of Jesus but instead interrogated me — “Who is Jesus Christ?” What has me trembling is the mercy of Christ. Over one long stretch that began as an ordinary call between brothers and became eighty-five minutes of contested ground, something spoke through Samuel’s drunkenness that knew things it should not have known, argued my own unpublished Christology back at me, and pressed on the exact seam between my confessed humility and my actual pride. And in the sober morning after, sitting with him as he described feeling thirty kilograms lighter, oddly calm, strangely at peace, I began to see that the Lord had done a great work — not despite the assault, but through it. The deepest trap was never the one they set. It was the one already in me. **Why This Matters:** This matters because the discipleship of the saints is not lived only in well-lit sanctuaries and orderly Bible studies; it is lived in the midnight hours when a brother is drowning, when the flesh rises unbidden, and when the accuser probes for the one unhealed seam still hidden in us. Most of the saints are trained to recognize temptation as invitation to obvious sin, and are wholly unprepared for the temptation that comes dressed as correct theology spoken from a stolen authority. The enemy in those hours did not ask me to deny Christ; he asked me to *defend myself*, to answer as myself, to speak true words from my own confidence rather than from my knees. It matters because a brother woke stable and at peace, and I must discern whether that peace is the Father’s mercy or a lull I dare not trust naively. It matters for humility, because the mercy that kept me from seeing with my own eyes is the same mercy that keeps any of us from pride or terror. And it matters because the Body of Christ needs sober witnesses to these realities, neither sensationalizing the demonic nor pretending it does not press against us — witnesses who understand that being *doctrinally correct* is not the same as *standing in the authority of the One who is Truth*. ## The Worldly Mind’s Interpretation The natural mind has ready explanations for everything in this account. A man drank a bottle of champagne, a bottle of Jack Daniel’s, three beers, and two ciders after a painful confrontation with a woman named Emily, and predictably lost all memory of the night — a textbook alcoholic blackout, the brain simply failing to encode memory under that much toxin. His cruelty toward his wife, the collapse of a family dinner into estrangement, the impending divorce papers — all of it reads as the tragic, cyclical wreckage of untreated addiction and marital breakdown. His speech became strange, repetitive, self-contradictory, and emotionally raw because that is what intoxication and heartbreak do. The “demon” is the mind’s way of externalizing shame, a dissociative story so that “I said that” becomes the more tolerable “something else said that through me.” And on my side of the line, a man working twelve-hour night shifts, awake until six in the morning, exhausted and depleted, experiences an intense episode: audio that “cuts out,” memories that do not match the recording, a stranger’s text arriving at a charged moment. Sleep deprivation is known to distort perception and blur the line between inner voice and external presence. The 289 area code is simply Ontario; the text likely a wrong number. Even the shaking afterward reads as the nervous system discharging after a frightening night. This reading is not cruel; it is coherent, and it would satisfy most therapists and many pastors. **The Limitation:** Yet the natural reading, for all its tidy competence, cannot account for the *content* of what pressed against me across that line. It cannot explain how a man who does not know theology as I know it argued my own written Christology back at me — Christology from meditations he plainly told me he never read. It cannot explain a voice that constructed interrogator’s frames — “You forget. You think I want to forgive?” — sentences engineered to bait a defense. It cannot weigh legal claims in the courts of heaven, cannot perceive why specific renunciation was required, cannot see why a man would repent over ninety seconds of borrowed glory that no clinical category even names as harm. The flesh-mind must reduce, must flatten, must find the smallest possible explanation and stop there, precisely because it has no category for a premeditated assault that studies a man’s soul for its seams. It can measure fatigue but not the weight of a mediator’s seat wrongly occupied. Discernment is not credulity; I refuse to sensationalize. But something shone in that room during worship, and something recoiled from it, and no theory of tired neurons explains a presence that grows sick at holiness. The unspiritual man cannot receive these things — not because he is stupid, but because they are discerned another way entirely. ## What the Spirit Reveals Through It I keep returning to the sound of Samuel on the phone, five ordinary minutes into a call between brothers, before it went strange. He had cooked for hours, wanted to celebrate his good news, and watched the dinner fracture before a drop was even poured. So he went to the pub and drank until he was gone, and somewhere in that hollowing-out, something moved in. What arrests me is not the drinking itself but the door — the resentment, the “well, fuck it,” the deliberate turning away from the table he had set in love toward the bottle that promised comfort. I have watched him shut down and drink two days running now, and I named it to myself as trouble at home, and let it pass. There is an ache in that. A man builds a feast, no one comes, and the emptiness becomes an invitation to something that had been waiting. And what presses on me, still, is that the assault did not stay on him. It reached through the line and came for me. It found pride where I had written that I am “just a vessel.” It hit my ego. It made me angry, and here is the terrible mercy of it — I did not feel angry in the moment. Only when I listened back to my own recorded voice did I hear it: that was not me speaking, or not only me. The enemy had located the exact seam between my confessed humility and my actual pride, and pressed there until something false came out of my mouth. I had thought myself past that. The night proved I was not. The first Scripture that opened over me was Peter warming himself at the fire, denying his Lord three times before the cock crowed. I lived a version of that. I slipped, corrected myself, slipped again, corrected again — more than three times, though three restorations stand out. What undoes me is that the Lord let it happen the way He let Peter’s happen, not to destroy me but to show me the dross still in me. “Simon, Simon! Indeed, Satan has asked for you, that he may sift you as wheat” (Luke 22:31). I was sifted. And like Peter, I did not know how much chaff was in me until the sieve began to shake. The Greek beneath that sifting is σινιάζω (siniazō, sin-ee-AD-zo) — to sift, to shake violently in a sieve so that everything not firmly rooted is thrown loose. That is exactly what the night was. It was not random chaos; it was a shaking with a purpose, a violent agitation that separated what was truly mine in Christ from what was still merely my flesh dressed in spiritual language. The enemy meant it for my destruction. But the sieve is held in the hand of the One who prayed that my faith would not fail, and the shaking only cast out what I did not need to keep. I think of dross, because that is the word that came to me — “I will turn My hand against you, and thoroughly purge away your dross, and take away all your alloy” (Isaiah 1:25). The Hebrew סִיג (sig, SEEG) is that scum of base metal that must be skimmed before silver is pure. And it is no small thing that I am now a certified welder, a man who works metal with fire for his trade — and the enemy taunted me with the welding, repeating it, “I hear you come from a welding job, Seth, is it true?” What it meant as mockery, the Spirit turned into a parable: I am the metal in the furnace now, not merely the one who wields the flame. Here the canonical pattern deepens, because Scripture never presents deliverance as clean and instantaneous by human command alone. The sons of Sceva learned this when they invoked a name they had no covenant standing to wield, and the evil spirit answered, “Jesus I know, and Paul I know; but who are you?” (Acts 19:15). When my voice was met with “Jesus Christ? Who is Jesus Christ?” I hear the same terrible question turned toward me — not a denial of Christ’s authority, but a probe into whether I was truly *in* Him or merely near Him. Something in me had been trusting the syllables and not fully the Person. The name itself carries weight the English flattens: Ἰησοῦς (Iēsous, ee-ay-SOOSS) is the Greek rendering of יְשׁוּעַ (Yeshua, yeh-SHOO-ah) — the LORD saves. Philippians 2:10 says that at the name of Ἰησοῦς (ee-ay-SOOSS) every knee shall bow — but the bowing is compelled by the exalted Person, not by phonetics detached from Him. The enemy that night was pressing the difference between a name used as incantation and a name confessed as Lord. And here the whole framing of the enemy’s kingdom comes into focus, because I had been shown that the kingdom of darkness creates nothing. It only counterfeits, corrupts, inverts. Satan’s original claim — “I will ascend, I will be like the Most High” (Isaiah 14:14) — is not creativity but envy dressed as ambition. This is why the exchange had the shape of a court. The accuser, ὁ κατήγωρ (ho katēgōr, ho kat-AY-gore) — the accuser of the brethren (Revelation 12:10) — works by legal counterfeit, pressing charges, seeking standing. When I commanded the angels of heaven to arrest the spirit, the first time it did not go — “he tried to arrest me, but I am here with my brother; my brother will defend me.” There was standing being contested, ground that had been given through vows and words and the thing Samuel spoke over his wife. Deliverance was not a formula I could execute; it was a war over legal claim, and the claim had to be renounced before the ground could be taken. The word that unlocks the whole night for me is τετέλεσται (tetelestai, teh-TEL-es-tai) — “it is finished,” the sixth word from the cross in John 19:30. It is perfect passive: an action completed in the past whose effects stand permanently accomplished into the present and forever. The cross of the Lord Jesus Christ is not a project awaiting my contribution; it is a finished, sealed, standing-completed reality. And this is *precisely* the doctrine the voice attacked. Through Samuel’s mouth came the words: “If he’s done it all, why am I still doing it?” and then, escalating, “He tells me, brother, it’s my job to complete.” A father-voice claiming the finished work still awaits a human hand — this is the direct contradiction of τετέλεσται (teh-TEL-es-tai). Here the canon gives me the one measuring line I need: “Every spirit that confesses that Jesus Christ has come in the flesh is of God, and every spirit that does not confess that Jesus Christ has come in the flesh is not of God” (1 John 4:2–3). A spirit that insists the incarnate, crucified Christ did *not* finish His work is a spirit that does not confess Him rightly. I had the sign at minute twelve of an eighty-five minute call. I had it in my hand. And what I did with that sign is the whole grief of the night. This is the theological tension the night forced open in me. I had begun to see myself as the mediator, the one through whom revelation flows, the scribe of stone tablets and meditations. And the enemy found precisely that inflation and pressed it, tempting me to become “the idol of mediation” rather than a servant pointing to the mercy seat. The whole assault was, in a sense, an attempt to get me to sit where only Christ may sit. “For there is one God and one Mediator between God and men, the Man Christ Jesus” (1 Timothy 2:5). The word is μεσίτης (mesitēs, meh-SEE-tace) — a go-between, the one who stands in the gap. There is a single μεσίτης, and His name is Jesus. And I did not do the thing the sign at minute twelve demanded — I did not fall silent and worship; instead I answered, and I answered *as myself*, from my confidence, not from my knees. The sentence I will sit with for a long time is the one I spoke: “I am telling you as one who loves you more than anyone else in your life right now, that is not your father in heaven.” Every word of it was true. Samuel himself, the next morning, said that line nailed it. And yet — that was *my* sentence, deduced and thrown into the conversation, not a word I had received from the Father to be dispatched. There I was, doing a version of the very thing the darkness was doing: standing on true doctrine in an authority that was not given to me in that moment. The Kingdom mystery I could not have grasped by reasoning is this: the questions, and the framing. This spirit interrogated me. It asked me questions, made statements about what I supposedly believed about my brother, set me up to defend myself — “No, Samuel, I don’t believe that about you” — and in that reflex of self-defense, I gave it words to work with. “Has God indeed said…?” (Genesis 3:1). The serpent does not command; it inquires. It baits the mouth into speech, because “by your words you will be justified, and by your words you will be condemned” (Matthew 12:37). And every time the attention curved back to the vessel — how highly ranked the spirit was, whether it was truly Lucifer, how significant I must be to warrant such an assault — the trap was tightening. The moment I say “I have really done nothing, and this is not about me,” the enemy’s leverage collapses, because his entire claim depends on my believing the story is about my greatness or my failure. My greatest mistake was not shutting my mouth at minute twelve. “Set a guard, O LORD, over my mouth; keep watch over the door of my lips” (Psalm 141:3). And there was a later night, a separate assault at six in the morning after twelve-hour shifts, where the same seam was pressed to its sharpest point. My brother, under pressure, kept pressing me — *how did you know that? How did you know that?* — and I, sensing the atmosphere of that house even without being there, gave him an answer. I pointed to the way the deceiving voice fell silent after I corrected it and said, in effect, *there is your proof.* I named the authority as Jesus’. I even called mine borrowed. And still, listening back, I hear what I actually did: I handed a shaken man a sign to satisfy his demand for one. For ninety seconds I sat where I had no business sitting. I became the mediator, the visible proof, the thing his faith rested on. “An evil and adulterous generation seeks after a sign, and no sign will be given to it except the sign of the prophet Jonah” (Matthew 12:39). A true σημεῖον (sēmeion, say-MAY-on) — a sign pointing beyond itself — never terminates on the one who performs it; it always directs the eye to God. When I said *that’s your proof of my borrowed authority*, I made myself the terminus of the sign. The pointing finger became the thing pointed at. The canon traces this danger from beginning to end. At Lystra, when the crowds cried “The gods have come down to us in the likeness of men,” Paul and Barnabas tore their clothes and ran in among them shouting, “We also are men with the same nature as you” (Acts 14:11-15). Herod, by contrast, received the crowd’s cry — “The voice of a god and not of a man!” — and “because he did not give glory to God, an angel of the Lord struck him” (Acts 12:22-23). Two responses to being made an idol: one man tears his garments, the other is eaten by worms. I sat on that seat for ninety seconds, and by His glorious mercy I was given the garment-tearing, not the worms. And the demons that night wanted my *agreement* — *Do you see the three black demons? Do you see the coat hanger? Tell me you see them.* “By the mouth of two or three witnesses every word shall be established” (Deuteronomy 19:15). They sought a second witness so their reality could be established. My eyes were wide open and I said, *no, not with the physical eyes.* What I did discern in the room was not a demon at all — a clear presence, like a puff that was not smoke, which I would not name evil. They pressed me to call it what it was not, to “call evil good, and good evil” (Isaiah 5:20). This is the oldest inversion, and refusing to slander what may have been a guardian or a theophany was as vital as refusing to bless the darkness. The personal encounter arrives when I finally do what I should have done at minute twelve. I stop arguing. I stop taunting them back, which was its own snare — engaging the mockery, returning the taunt, letting them pull me into their arena, when even the archangel Michael would not bring a railing accusation (Jude 9). I fall silent and I begin to worship — not to provoke, but to give thanks: *your glory here, not mine; you are the ultimate authority; you have completed the works.* And the growling rises to a fury and screams for me to *stop making the volume loud*, because it cannot say it is afraid of the Spirit of God. So I breathe out — *feel the breath of God* — and the line goes dead. It was never about volume. The breath of the One who lives in me, sent their way, ended it. What the Spirit finally pressed into me, after eighty-five minutes of renouncing and the long dawn of writing it all down, was the meaning of the whole assault: every threat, every plea, every demand that I *see*, every mockery of my son’s name and my Lord — all of it was the enemy drawing up in me what the refiner was already waiting to burn: pride, fear, the appetite to seat myself where Christ belongs, and that ancient seam of *knowledge earned* against *gift received*. This is the Gnostic corruption — to take revelation the Father gives and say *this is mine now, I earned this.* But “what do you have that you did not receive?” (1 Corinthians 4:7). It is not knowledge earned. It is not mine. It is a gift of revelation from the Father, and the moment I grip it as possession, I have already begun to sit on the seat again. And so the communal weight settles on me, and it is the only unselfish reason I told Samuel anything at all: through what happened, the Lord did a great work in *him*. He woke early, stable, calm, at peace, feeling thirty kilograms lighter, exactly as I prayed through the final intercession. The atmosphere in that house changed — calmer now, a little brighter. What grieves Samuel most is not Emily, not the drink, but that he set a bad example of being a Christian — a godly sorrow that produces repentance (2 Corinthians 7:10), and his vengeance toward Emily has turned to something that feels bad *for* her rather than against her. That is not the flesh’s outlet; that is the Father’s hand. Samuel told me plainly, “Those things said would never come from my mouth” — and he is right, and I never once believed it was him. This is what I must carry for the Body: we do not disciple one another only in strength; we hold one another in the hours when something else is speaking through a beloved brother, and we refuse to identify the person with the possession. I love him. He is not the enemy that used his voice. The war was real, but so is his sonship. There is a final thing I dare not fully interpret — the spirit spoke of “my brother,” of “brothers,” switching between singular and plural, and a strange text lit up my phone mid-worship, *did the truth finally come out?* Every instinct wanted to decode it, and the impression that dropped was simply, *don’t worry about it.* “The secret things belong to the LORD our God, but those things which are revealed belong to us” (Deuteronomy 29:29). I will hold what was revealed and leave the rest sealed. ## Implications and Challenges **Theological correctness is not spiritual authority.** I spent an eighty-five minute call speaking true things — the finished work of the cross, the rest of the Father, that the Father does not give fear or confusion — and nearly all of it was accurate, and nearly all of it was spoken from the wrong place, from my own confidence rather than from my knees. The modern Western church trains us relentlessly to be *right*: to have the verse, the counter-argument, the doctrinal position. But this night reveals that a man can be doctrinally armed and spiritually disarmed at once. The kingdom of darkness is doctrinally correct too; its truths are our truths twisted, its doctrine often accurate — what it lacks is the right to stand in the authority of the only One who has authority. If our confidence is in our accuracy rather than in the moment-by-moment giving of the Father, we are leaning on nothing that can hold. **We have almost no category for silence as warfare.** My whole formation says that when error appears, I must answer it, and so I did not go silent at minute twelve when the sign was already in my hand. But some spiritual encounters are won by the shut mouth, not the eloquent one (Psalm 141:3). Every question the thing asked was bait for my speech, and my corrections did not simply cancel my errors, because these entities seize the spoken word and work with it regardless of the retraction. The one sentence I spoke *from* received authority rather than from myself — “Leave him alone; he’s coming sober for the rest of the night” — silenced the room. Grace to the activist temperament of much modern Christianity: sometimes the most powerful thing a servant can do before a twisting spirit is refuse the frame, refuse to defend himself, and turn his face toward the living God in worship. **Making an idol of the deliverer is a live and subtle danger.** My most sobering exposure was not the demon but the pride it found in me — the inflation of “vessel” into something nearer to mediator, and the ninety literal seconds I supplied a sign and became the terminus of my brother’s faith (1 Timothy 2:5; Matthew 12:39). This is a live danger for anyone gifted in intercession, prophecy, or teaching, and the American church especially rewards the platformed and the anointed until they begin to believe they are the source rather than the servant. The enemy did not need to make me deny Christ; it only needed to make me central. When people press us for proof, the faithful answer is often to withhold ourselves as the sign and point relentlessly to the crucified and risen Christ — to tear our garments at Lystra rather than receive the praise at Caesarea. **Testing the spirits means listening for the confession they will not make.** The voice that begged me to test it — “I am hearing the Spirit, brother. I love you. Test me. You cannot silence me.” — never once said *I am from the Lord*; it said only *I am not of evil origin*, which is technically true of everything created, for evil has no origin of its own (1 John 4:1-3). We are in danger when we let a spirit set the terms of its own examination, or when we mistake affectionate language and pleas of love for the fruit of the Spirit. The single non-negotiable is the confession of Jesus Christ come in the flesh, from the Lord, as Lord — and the deceiving voices, whether attacking τετέλεσται (teh-TEL-es-tai) or pressing me to slander a possible theophany (Isaiah 5:20), circled that confession without ever landing on it. Grace to the earnest: better to say *I do not see that* than to establish a false reality by our agreement. **We must resist both the reduction of the demonic to psychology and the fascination with the ranks of darkness.** Much of the modern Western church has quietly agreed to treat every account like this as trauma or superstition — categories that let us keep our respectability — while other corners are intoxicated with hierarchy, cataloguing which principality, whether it was Lucifer himself. I do not despise psychology; the blackout is real, the addiction is real, and Samuel needs sober help as much as deliverance. But a spirit argued my unpublished Christology back at me and named my welding job, and no naturalist box holds that. And when I asked for names, I repented, because the appetite to know the mechanics of the demonic is itself a snare that pulls the eyes off Christ — my forgetting the names is the Lord’s mercy. Grace says: get the help. Truth says: there was a war in that room. And the way through was worship, not intelligence-gathering. **The mercy is hidden inside the fire, and even our craving to interpret it can be idolatry in spiritual dress.** It would be easy to read these nights as pure defeat, and easy for a brother to be crushed by shame over what was said through him — but the fruit contradicts despair: a man woke thirty kilograms lighter, calm, at peace, with claims renounced over households, workplaces, and lineages. “My son, do not despise the chastening of the Lord” — παιδεία (paideia, pie-DAY-ah), the fatherly training of a son — “for whom the Lord loves He chastens” (Hebrews 12:5-6). The Father sometimes appoints the trial He does not author the evil of, purging dross He fully intends to remove. And when the strange text arrived and I burned to decode it, the Spirit said only *don’t worry about it* — for the safest posture is not the one that sees and decodes the most, but the one that worships and lets the Father keep His own secrets (1 Corinthians 4:7; Deuteronomy 29:29). To the reader carrying the wreckage of a night like this: do not despise the chastening. The furnace that frightened you may be the very hand of a Father refining a son He will not lose. ## What This Means For Us **For Spiritual Maturity:** Maturity is not the arrival at a place where I can no longer be deceived; it is the humility to listen back to my own voice and admit that the anger, the pride, and the defensive words were dross I did not know I still carried. The most dangerous moment of a long ordeal was rarely the loudest one — I survived eighty-five minutes of screaming and threats, but the trespass that shook me was ninety silent seconds of borrowed glory that no one else would have noticed. To grow in Christ is to develop an ear for the quiet usurpations: the moment a brother’s faith begins to rest on us, the moment revelation curdles into possession, the moment right words issue from a compromised seat. Real maturity kept my face turning back toward worship even after four failures, because the measure of a son is not that he never slips, but that grace keeps returning him to the mercy seat — and that he prizes the refiner’s exposure of hidden pride above the drama of spiritual warfare. **For Kingdom Obedience:** Obedience here took the concrete shape of renunciation and restraint — renouncing every claim, vow, oath, and word that gave the enemy legal ground over Samuel, over Emily, over Donovan and his mother and his sisters, over my household and ours, over our workplaces and the very atmospheres of our homes, and restraining myself from disclosing what the Lord has not released. It demands the discipline of prayer and fasting as genuine standing, not ritual — “this kind does not go out except by prayer and fasting” (Mark 9:29) — quelling the flesh so that I am able to fall silent under pressure rather than answer as myself. It calls the Body to test every spirit against the confession that Jesus Christ has come in the flesh and has *finished* the work, τετέλεσται (teh-TEL-es-tai), refusing any voice, however pastoral its register, that claims the cross awaits our completion. And it calls me to keep loving the man while refusing the spirit that used him, and to walk with my brother into sober daylight help, not leaving deliverance as a dramatic memory while the open door of resentment and drink stands unaddressed. **For Prophetic Posture:** The prophetic posture I am being trained into is one of empty hands and a guarded seat — holding revelation without gripping it, stewarding the gift while confessing *what do I have that I did not receive*, and holding the sealed thing sealed because the secret things belong to the Lord. I am learning to abide in worship before I intercede, to let the Spirit turn my face before I ever open my mouth against the darkness, because the war is not won by my authority but by the Father’s breath. To watch and wait means discerning whether Samuel’s morning peace is mercy or lull without seizing false certainty in either direction, recognizing the interrogator’s frame engineered to bait my defense, and refusing to enter the enemy’s arena of taunts. The true seer is not the one who names the darkness most precisely but the one who abides most closely in the light, who honors the body’s need for rest as a spiritual duty — Samuel’s blunt “you do need to sleep” was as much a spiritual word as any prayer — and who testifies only to what the Spirit and the Word confirm, never sensationalizing the battle or making himself its hero. ## A Closing Prayer Father, I come to Your mercy seat still trembling, but the trembling is worship now and not fear, for it was Your mercy and not the enemy’s rage that undid me. I thank You that You kept me through nights I did not understand and could not have rehearsed, that You did not strike me down when I sat for ninety seconds on the seat that belongs to Your Son alone, but let me see it, and named it, and gave me the tearing of garments instead of the worms. I confess the pride You exposed, the anger that rose in me unnoticed, the sign I gave that I was never commissioned to give, the words I should never have spoken, and the four times I slipped before the sifting finally broke; and I receive Your forgiveness as a son, not a slave. Thank You that You are the only Mediator, the one μεσίτης (meh-SEE-tace), that I am not, that I never was, and that You will not share Your seat with my inflated sense of calling — purge from me every last grain of that dross, O Refiner, until only Christ is reflected in me. I bless You that the assault meant for destruction became a fatherly training, and that You raised my brother into a stable and peaceful morning exactly as I prayed. I stand as one of Your servants the prophets, a co-heir under the New Covenant, in agreement with Your revealed will, and I hold before You every claim renounced over Samuel, over Emily, over Donovan and his mother and his sisters, over my household and ours, over our workplaces and the very atmospheres of our homes — let no reclaimed ground be lost, and let every door opened through resentment and drink be shut by Your Spirit and healed at its root. Keep me silent where You have not released me to speak, guard my rest, guard my tongue, and let me steward the sealed things faithfully, disclosing only what serves my brother’s healing and Your glory. I renounce every appetite in me to know the powers by name or to decode what You have told me not to worry about, and I choose instead to know You, to worship You, to abide in You until the war is only ever fought from that place. To You, the God who saves, the Lord of the armies of heaven, be all glory, dominion, and praise, between the heavens and the earth, in the name of Jesus Christ, come in the flesh, crucified, risen, and reigning.
