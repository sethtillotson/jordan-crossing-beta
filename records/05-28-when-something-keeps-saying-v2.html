# 05-28 Call Reflection: Spiritual Discernment — Divine Positioning and Obedience Before Clarity **Title:** 05-28 at 09:35 — When Something Keeps Saying Go **Recorded:** 2026-05-28 09:35 **Source Discernment:** C — Recorded Conversation or Event (Live Capture) **Classification:** Call Reflection ## Opening Observation I listened to a brief but weighty exchange this morning, and something in it arrested me long after the call ended. My brother stepped outside — away from the ordinary rhythm of his workday — to share something tender and unresolved: he has a date, and he cannot fully explain why he said yes. What stopped me was not the circumstance itself, but the posture underneath it — the honest admission that something is *pushing* him toward this, and he does not yet know what for. That kind of holy uncertainty is worth sitting with. **Why This Matters:** There is a kind of spiritual sensitivity that Western Christianity has largely trained out of its people — the capacity to notice a prompting before it can be fully explained, to move toward something without yet possessing a theology for it, and to hold the tension of *I know God is in this, I just don’t know why* without collapsing that tension prematurely into either fear or false certainty. My brother named something real this morning: the awareness that a person or moment has been placed intentionally into your path. That awareness itself is a form of discernment. The fact that he paused long enough to feel it, name it, and bring it to a brother rather than dismiss it — that is not small. The saints need to recover this kind of attentive, unhurried listening to the Spirit’s movement in the ordinary geography of daily life. ## The Worldly Mind’s Interpretation From the vantage point of purely natural reasoning, this is simply a man who agreed to go on a date with a coworker he finds appealing, felt some social ambivalence about it, and is now processing that ambivalence by framing it spiritually — perhaps as a way of resolving the internal conflict between hesitation and desire. The natural mind would likely counsel him to stop overthinking it, enjoy the company, and let the situation develop however it develops. Psychology might identify the “push” he describes as cognitive dissonance resolving itself through the path of least resistance, or as confirmation bias — finding divine sanction for something he already wanted to do. Culture would simply say: you feel it, so go, and see what happens. **The Limitation:** But that reading flattens something the natural mind cannot perceive on its own. It treats the interior prompting as noise to be rationalized rather than signal to be discerned. The soul has registers that purely analytical frameworks cannot access, and what my brother described — that unmistakable sense of *something placed very intentionally* — is a category of knowing that neither psychology nor social reasoning was built to honor. The natural mind processes the external facts: a coworker, a date, a feeling. The Spirit reads the architecture beneath those facts: the timing, the softening, the open door, and the question of *what am I being positioned for?* The difference between those two readings is not intelligence — it is the anointing that teaches all things, the inner witness that the natural man simply does not carry. ## What the Spirit Reveals Through It As I sat with what my brother shared, the first thing I noticed was how genuinely humble his framing was. He did not announce a divine mandate. He did not dress the prompting in more certainty than it carried. He said plainly: *I’m not sure if I should go to evangelize, or if I should really just be there. Something is pushing me. I just don’t know why.* That honest holding of tension is itself a mark of spiritual maturity. The immature soul either dismisses promptings that cannot be immediately explained or overclaims them into elaborate prophetic structures. My brother did neither. He held the prompting openly, brought it into relationship, and asked for prayer-shaped companionship in sitting with it. This recalls something in the movement of the Spirit throughout Scripture that I find consistently overlooked in modern discipleship — the frequency with which God positions His servants before He fully explains the assignment. Philip was sent by the Spirit toward a desert road without being told who he would meet there — only told to *go near and overtake* the chariot when he arrived (Acts 8:26–29). The prompting preceded the clarity. Cornelius was told in a vision to send for Peter before either man fully understood what the meeting would mean (Acts 10:1–6). Ananias was sent to Saul of Tarsus — a name he had every natural reason to fear — with nothing more than a name, a street, and a house number (Acts 9:11). In every one of these moments, the servants of God moved toward something without yet possessing the full theology of it. Obedience was required before understanding was supplied. I am seeing something here about the nature of divine positioning that deserves to be named carefully. When the Spirit places a person into proximity with another — whether through a workplace, a neighborhood, a family gathering, or a social invitation — it does not always announce its purpose loudly. Sometimes the placement itself is the message, and the purpose only becomes legible through faithful presence. My brother’s instinct that this woman has been placed intentionally into his life may be entirely correct, and yet the reason may be something he cannot yet categorize as evangelism *or* relationship *or* anything else in the existing vocabulary of his spiritual life. Sometimes God is doing a third thing that neither of those categories adequately names. The Greek word that keeps pressing on me here is καιρός (kairos, KY-ros) — the appointed time, the right moment, the season charged with divine weight, as distinct from χρόνος (chronos, KRON-os), which is merely clock-time, the neutral passage of hours. When my brother said *the vibes were good today*, and then immediately followed that with *something’s telling me to go* — I hear two registers speaking at once. The surface register is social and affective: good energy, mutual interest, easy agreement. But underneath that, he sensed a καιρός (KY-ros) quality to the moment — something that would not simply be available again if he let it pass. Scripture is full of these charged moments where ordinary circumstances become the vessel for divine intention, and the difference between the person who walks in fruitfulness and the one who misses the moment is often just this: one has learned to feel the weight of καιρός (KY-ros) and honor it, even without full explanation. What strikes me even more is what he *did not* do. He did not spiritualize himself out of the situation. He did not say, *I cannot go on a date with a non-believer* and retreat into a posture of religious separation. He also did not strip the moment of spiritual significance and just treat it as a social outing with no further dimension. He held both registers simultaneously — the human and the sacred — and asked the right question: *what am I being sent for?* That question itself is a form of consecration. It is the posture of a servant who has learned that ordinary life is not spiritually neutral, that the geography of his daily existence is not accidental, and that the Spirit does not only speak in church buildings or prayer closets. The book of Proverbs describes something I want to name here: *A man’s heart plans his way, but the LORD directs his steps* (Proverbs 16:9). There is a Hebrew word underneath *directs* — כּוּן (kun, KOON) — which carries the sense of establishing, making firm, setting in place. The Lord does not merely suggest a correction to the path we have planned; He *establishes* the step. There is sovereignty and intentionality in the word. My brother made a plan this morning — a social plan, a relational plan — and somewhere underneath that plan, the One who establishes steps was already at work. The question my brother is wisely asking is not *should I have made this plan*, but *Lord, what are You establishing through it?* I also want to name the beauty of the fact that he brought this into relationship before he had to go back inside. He did not wait until he had it fully worked out. He called a brother, named the tension, asked for prayer-shaped space, and said *sit with it and let me know what you think later on*. This is the Body of Christ functioning as it was designed to function — not as an audience for polished testimonies, but as a company of witnesses who carry one another’s unresolved things, who pray into the uncertainty with a brother, who hold the weight of the not-yet-known alongside each other. The New Covenant does not ask us to be certain before we bring something to the Body. It asks us to *bear one another’s burdens* (Galatians 6:2) — and sometimes the burden is simply an open question that needs to be carried in good company. There is also something I want to sit with regarding his two stated possibilities: *should I go to evangelize, or should I really just be there?* I understand why he framed it that way — the evangelical tradition he likely carries has taught him to think in terms of witness and proclamation whenever proximity to a non-believer is involved. But I wonder if the Spirit is inviting him past that binary. Evangelism conceived as a transaction — I go, I share the gospel, I close the loop — is actually a diminished category compared to what Jesus modeled. Jesus went to weddings. He ate with tax collectors. He walked long roads with people before He asked them the question that mattered most. He was simply and fully *present*, and His presence itself was an announcement of the Kingdom. My brother’s instinct that he might be called to *really be there* — to be genuinely present, not strategically positioned — may be the more mature spiritual instinct of the two. This also touches something about purity of motive that I do not want to pass over too quickly. There is a real difference between going to a social encounter as a vessel of the Spirit’s genuine love for the other person, and going as someone with a hidden agenda dressed in relational clothing. The Spirit does not recruit us to be covert operators for the Kingdom. He forms us into people whose presence carries something real and whose care for others is not contingent on those others responding the way we hope. If my brother goes to this date genuinely open — genuinely willing to be changed by the encounter, genuinely present to the person rather than performing a role — then whatever the Spirit intends can move through him freely. If he goes with a script, the very thing that makes him a vessel will be quietly obstructed. Finally, I am struck by the way the call ended — with *shalom*. In a brief, practical, almost logistical exchange between two brothers, the ancient Hebrew greeting of wholeness, completeness, and divine peace was the parting word. שָׁלוֹם (shalom, shah-LOME) is not merely a farewell. It is a declaration — a pronouncement of the condition the Lord intends for His people. That it emerged naturally at the end of an ordinary Tuesday morning call, between two men talking about a date and a workday, tells me something about the spiritual culture these brothers inhabit. The sacred and the ordinary are not compartmentalized in their friendship. They carry שָׁלוֹם (shah-LOME) into the workweek, into the uncertainty, into the situations they cannot yet explain. That is not a small thing. That is the Kingdom at ground level. ## Implications and Challenges The first challenge this meditation surfaces is the tendency of modern Christianity to require full theological clarity before taking a step. We have built a culture of over-explanation that often mistakes hesitation for holiness and paralysis for discernment. My brother’s posture — *something is pushing me, I don’t know why, I’m going* — is actually closer to the biblical pattern of faith-in-motion than the endless committee-style deliberation we have normalized. Abraham went out, not knowing where he was going (Hebrews 11:8). The willingness to move toward what God is establishing, before the full reason is clear, is not recklessness. It is covenant trust. Second, the evangelical instinct to frame every proximity to a non-believer as primarily an evangelism opportunity, while not wrong in its desire, can quietly undermine the quality of presence that actually makes us effective witnesses. People are not outreach targets. They are image-bearers of God (Genesis 1:27) who deserve to be genuinely known and genuinely cared for. When we enter relationships with hidden agendas — even holy ones — something in the other person eventually perceives the inauthenticity. The Spirit’s invitation is often into *genuine* presence, from which witness flows naturally rather than strategically. Third, the instinct to call a trusted brother in the middle of an ordinary morning — before clarity, before the situation resolved itself, before there was anything impressive to report — reflects a relational discipline that the individualism of Western Christianity actively works against. We wait until we have a testimony before we bring something to the Body. But the testimony is often *born* in the shared carrying of the unresolved. The Church must recover the practice of bringing the half-formed, the uncertain, and the quietly pressing to one another — not for answers, but for company in the waiting. Fourth, the use of the word *shalom* in an ordinary goodbye deserves more weight than we typically give it. Language shapes spiritual reality. When the people of God bless one another with the vocabulary of the covenant — whether it is *shalom*, or *grace and peace*, or *the Lord be with you* — they are doing something more than observing social courtesy. They are making a declaration. They are agreeing with heaven’s intention for one another. The recovery of covenant language in ordinary speech is not archaism; it is a form of priestly ministry that the Body desperately needs to reclaim. Fifth, and perhaps most quietly, this exchange challenges the notion that spiritual significance only lives in dramatic moments. This was a five-minute call from a parking lot. Two brothers, a workday, a date, a goodbye. And yet the Spirit was moving in it — positioning, prompting, pressing, and blessing. If we only look for God in the large and the loud, we will miss the quiet architecture He is building in the ordinary Tuesday mornings of our lives. ## What This Means For Us **For Spiritual Maturity:** This calls us to develop what might be named a *theology of placement* — the practiced awareness that the people God brings into proximity with us are not accidental, and that our first question when someone enters our life is not *what do I do with this person* but *Lord, what are You doing here?* Spiritual maturity includes the capacity to hold that question without forcing it into premature resolution, and to remain present to the person while the answer unfolds. **For Kingdom Obedience:** Kingdom obedience here looks like going — not because everything is clear, but because the prompting is real and the Lord establishes steps. It looks like entering the situation with an open hand rather than a closed agenda, genuinely available to whatever the Spirit intends. And it looks like returning to a trusted brother afterward to continue the discernment together, rather than processing it alone. **For Prophetic Posture:** The prophetic posture this meditation trains us toward is one of attentive presence — the capacity to feel the καιρός (KY-ros) weight of ordinary moments and honor it, to recognize placement before we understand purpose, and to trust the One who directs our steps even when our own planning only sees the surface. We remain watchful not by withdrawing from life but by bringing the Spirit’s awareness into the full texture of it — including Tuesday morning phone calls, workplace friendships, and spontaneous yeses to something we cannot yet explain. ## A Closing Prayer Father, I come before You with gratitude for the quiet morning movements of Your Spirit — for brothers who step outside their ordinary day to share the tender, unresolved things, and for the grace to hold those things together rather than alone. I thank You that You are the One who establishes steps, that Your positioning is never accidental, and that the ordinary geography of daily life is never beneath Your sovereign attention. I ask that You guard my brother in this moment — that You give him the gift of genuine presence, pure motive, and a heart that is truly open to whatever You are doing, whether that is a word spoken, a friendship kindled, or simply the faithful witness of a life that carries שָׁלוֹם (shah-LOME) into the places others have not yet tasted it. Make us, as Your people, a company who can feel the weight of καιρός (KY-ros) without demanding that it explain itself before we move. Teach us to go when You say go, to be present when You say be present, and to trust that the understanding will come in its own appointed time. Let our farewells carry blessing. Let our ordinary conversations be charged with Your Kingdom reality. And let the Body learn again the ancient practice of carrying one another’s uncertainties as well as one another’s testimonies — for You are glorified in both. In the name of Jesus, the one who goes to the feast and to the road and to the desert place and to the table — and who is fully present in all of them — amen. ## Scripture References & Original Language Notes | **Passage (NKJV)** | **Original Language** | **Theological Insight** | |-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------| | Acts 8:26–29 — “Arise and go toward the south along the road which goes down from Jerusalem to Gaza… Go near and overtake this chariot.” | πνεῦμα (pneuma, NYOO-mah) — Spirit / breath | The Spirit gives directional instruction before the purpose is explained;
