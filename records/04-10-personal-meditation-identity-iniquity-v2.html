# 04-10 Personal Meditation: Identity, Iniquity, and the Image of God — A Prophetic Response to the LGBTQ+ Identity Debate **Source Discernment:** A — Personal Spoken-Word Revelation (with brief reference to a community note encountered online and a passing mention of a remnant sister's perspective) **Personal Meditation: The Mark of What You Call Yourself — Identity, Iniquity, and the Consuming Fire of God** --- ## ⚡ Opening Observation Something arrested me today while scrolling through Threads — a community note, of all things, cut through the noise and landed with the weight of revelation. The note made a distinction that the culture itself is beginning to sense: that *sexual orientation* and *identity* are not the same category, and that collapsing them together has created confusion even within the very movement that merged them. I felt the Spirit press on that word — *identity* — and I could not let it go, because I believe that word is the hinge on which an entire Kingdom truth is swinging right now. **Why This Matters:** The question of identity is not a peripheral cultural debate. It is the oldest war in creation — the war over what a human being is, what they are called, and who has the authority to name them. When the enemy approached Eve, he did not merely offer her fruit; he offered her a *redefinition of self* — "you will be like God." When the prodigal son "came to himself," the Greek text says he returned to his *being*, his essential nature as a son. When Jesus stood before Pilate and before the crowd, the question was not merely what He had done — it was who He was. Identity is the battleground of the ages, and the present cultural moment has made that warfare visible in a way that the Body of Christ cannot afford to ignore or caricature. --- ## 🌍 The Worldly Mind's Interpretation From the vantage point of purely natural reasoning, the cultural phenomenon unfolding around LGBTQ+ terminology looks like a community in the process of self-differentiation — a social group discovering that it has grown large enough, and diverse enough, to contain internal tensions and competing interests. The secular observer might note that the original gay rights movement was built on a narrower legal and social claim — the right to love whom one loves — and that the addition of gender identity categories has introduced philosophical frameworks (such as gender theory and social constructionism) that operate on a fundamentally different logic than sexual orientation. Commentators across the political spectrum, including many within the LGB community itself, have begun to voice this tension publicly. From a sociological standpoint, it makes sense: movements fracture when their foundational claims broaden faster than their coalitional coherence can bear. This is, in the natural mind's reading, simply the ordinary entropy of activist politics. **The Limitation:** What the natural mind perceives as sociological drift, the Spirit reveals as something far more ancient and far more dangerous. Cultural analysis can describe the fracture — it cannot name the fracture's root. Human reasoning can trace the trajectory of a movement; it cannot discern the *spiritual grammar* underneath it. There is a dimension to this moment that no political science, no psychology of identity formation, and no cultural commentary can reach — because it requires standing inside the revelation of Scripture, inside the knowledge of what a human being actually *is* before God, to see what is really happening. The natural mind reads "identity politics" and sees a sociological problem. The Spirit reads it and sees a theological one — the ancient counterfeit of image-bearing, the substitution of self-constructed personhood for God-given sonship. --- ## ✝️ What the Spirit Reveals: A Meditation I am sitting with that community note, and I find myself strangely grateful for it — not because it represents righteousness, but because even the secular mind is beginning to sense a distinction that Scripture established long before the debate existed. The note separates *orientation* — an inclination, a pull, a pattern of desire — from *identity* — a chosen garment, a declared self, a label affixed to the soul as a defining mark. And something in me recognizes that this distinction, however partially the world sees it, is theologically crucial. Scripture does not ignore the reality of disordered desire. Paul, writing to the Romans, describes in devastating detail the cascade of exchanges that follow when humanity refuses the knowledge of God — exchanging the glory of the incorruptible God for images, exchanging natural use for what is against nature, exchanging the truth of God for the lie (Romans 1:23–25). But notice what Paul is describing: he is describing *exchange*. Something given up. Something substituted. The language is not of creation but of *replacement* — and that replacement begins not in the body but in the mind, in the object of worship, in what or whom one declares to be ultimate. The body follows the altar. This is where the mark of the beast becomes startlingly relevant to this moment, and I do not say that lightly or sensationally. The number χξς — χξς (six hundred sixty-six) — has long been understood as the number of *humanity fallen short of divine completion*. Seven is the number of divine fullness; six is humanity straining toward that fullness through its own effort, its own declaration, its own name-giving. The mark on the hand and the forehead in Revelation 13 is not, I believe, merely a future barcode — it is the fullest expression of a pattern that has always existed: humanity marking itself with what it *does* (the hand — works, deeds, labor, production) and what it *thinks* (the forehead — mind, identity, allegiance, self-conception). What you call yourself is a mark. What you build with your life in the name of what you call yourself is a mark. And this is what arrests me: when a person moves from "I experience same-sex attraction" to "I *am* gay" — from desire to identity, from inclination to declaration, from something felt to something worn as a garment — something spiritually significant has happened. Not because one category is sinful and the other is not; all sexual expression outside of covenantal marriage is sexually immoral according to the witness of Scripture (1 Corinthians 6:18, Hebrews 13:4). But the move from experience to identity is the move from *having a wound* to *building a house in the wound*. It is the move from "I am tempted" to "I am defined by my temptation." And that second move — that identity move — is precisely what the enemy has always wanted. The Hebrew concept of צֶלֶם (tselem, TSEH-lem) — image, likeness, representation — is the word used in Genesis 1:26–27 when God declares that humanity is made in His image. To be a human being, in the theological anthropology of Scripture, is to be an *image-bearer* — a living representation of the invisible God, designed to reflect His nature, His glory, His dominion into the created order. Identity, in the biblical framework, is not something you construct; it is something you *receive* from the One whose image you bear. When Israel forgot who they were — when they built golden calves and chased foreign gods — they were not merely sinning morally; they were committing ontological treason. They were exchanging the glory of their image for the image of something lesser. The prodigal son in Luke 15 is perhaps the clearest narrative picture of what identity collapse looks like and what identity restoration requires. He went to the far country, and in that far country he spent everything — and then he "came to himself." That phrase in the Greek is εἰς ἑαυτὸν δὲ ἐλθὼν (eis heauton de elthōn) — *having come into himself*. The return to the Father begins with a return to *self* — not the constructed self of the far country, not the identity built in the pig pen, but the essential self that was always a son. The Father's response is staggering: he does not ask for a résumé of repentance before clothing him. He runs, he falls on his neck, he calls for the *best robe* — and that robe covers the far-country identity entirely. I think about the remnant sister who was speaking about the gay and lesbian community with genuine empathy, and something in me resonates with that posture deeply. Because the people caught in these identity constructions are not primarily enemies to be defeated — they are image-bearers who have been deceived into building houses in the wrong place, wearing garments that were never woven for them, answering to names that were never spoken over them by the Father. The Lord of our salvation — and I say this with full theological sobriety — *will cast our iniquities into the sea* (Micah 7:19). He chooses to remember sins no more (Jeremiah 31:34). The sexual immorality is real; the grace is also real; and the two truths must be held together without collapsing either one. But here is where I must press harder, because the Spirit will not let me stop at comfort. There is a category of things that must be *violently torn down* — and I use that phrase deliberately, borrowing from the language of Jeremiah's commission: "to root out and to pull down, to destroy and to throw down, to build and to plant" (Jeremiah 1:10). The things that must be torn down are not the people. They are the *exaltations* — the systems of thought, the identity architectures, the pride labels and pride flags and pride ideologies that have been lifted up above the knowledge of God (2 Corinthians 10:5). When an identity system says, "This is who you are, and nothing — not God, not Scripture, not community, not love — has the authority to tell you otherwise," that system has become a high place. And the prophets of God have always been commissioned to tear down high places. The deeper revelation I am carrying is this: the mark of the beast is not ultimately about a chip or a number tattooed on the wrist. It is about a *comprehensive identity system* — a way of marking yourself, marking your works, marking your allegiances — that does not originate in the living God. Six hundred sixty-six is humanity in full self-sovereignty: defining itself, marking itself, worshipping itself. And every system — religious or secular, traditional or progressive — that displaces "I am a child of God" with any other ultimate identity is participating in the spirit of that mark, whether or not it carries its name. The rainbow flag is visible. The suburban comfort-Christianity that never names anyone as a child of God is invisible. Both are identity displacements. Both will be burned in the fire that tests every man's work (1 Corinthians 3:13). What gives me hope — what I keep returning to — is that the fire of God is not punitive in its primary purpose; it is *purifying*. The gold survives. The child of God survives. Even if the garments of false identity are consumed, the person wearing them is not consumed. This is the mercy underneath the judgment: God is after the image, not the counterfeit clothing. He is after the son, not the pig-pen smell. He is after the daughter, not the far-country accent. And so the work of the Spirit in this hour is not to condemn people out of false identities but to *call* them — by name, by the name the Father has always known — back into the צֶלֶם (TSEH-lem), back into the image, back into the sonship that was always theirs. I am aware that my wife is calling, and life does not pause for revelation — which is itself a grace. But I need to say this before I set it down: the distinction between orientation and identity is not just a cultural footnote. It is a prophetic opening. The culture itself is beginning to name something that Scripture named first: that *what you do* is not identical to *who you are*, and that building your identity on desire — any desire — is an unstable foundation. The Stone that the builders rejected (Psalm 118:22, Luke 20:17) is the only foundation on which identity does not shift. And the invitation of this moment, for every image-bearer walking in confusion about who they are, is to lay down every constructed garment and receive the robe the Father has already prepared. --- ## 🤔 Implications and Challenges The first challenge this meditation presents is to the part of the Western Church that has responded to LGBTQ+ culture primarily with *disgust rather than discernment*. Disgust is a response of the flesh; discernment is a gift of the Spirit. If we cannot distinguish between the person who is suffering under the weight of same-sex attraction and the ideological system that tells that person their suffering is actually their identity, we will wound the people we were sent to heal. Grace must be as precise as truth, and the precision of grace requires discernment — not sentiment, but Spirit-illuminated seeing. The second challenge is to the part of the Church that has responded with *uncritical affirmation*. Affirming a person's constructed identity in order to communicate love is not love — it is the soft version of the same error the culture makes. It tells someone that their wound is their home, that the pig pen is as good as the Father's house. True love — ἀγάπη (agapē, ah-GAH-pay), covenantal love that wills the highest good of the beloved — sometimes refuses to affirm what it sees because it can see something better. The Father did not affirm the prodigal's pig-pen identity. He ran toward him with a robe. The third challenge is to those of us who use the language of spiritual warfare without ever extending our hands toward the people caught in the warfare. Tearing down high places is real and necessary — but Jeremiah was also commissioned to build and to plant. Every high place we tear down in the Spirit must be replaced with something constructed in ἀγάπη (ah-GAH-pay) — with genuine relationship, genuine welcome, genuine invitation into a community where identities are formed by the Spirit of adoption rather than the spirit of the age. The fourth challenge — and perhaps the most sobering — is the one that applies to all of us, not only to those whose identity struggles are culturally visible. The mark of self-sovereignty, the mark of building identity on anything other than sonship in God, is not limited to the rainbow flag. It lives in every heart that says "I am what I have accomplished," "I am what I have survived," "I am what my denomination declares me to be," "I am what my wound has made me." The fire tests every man's work. The question is not whether any of us have built with wood, hay, and stubble — the question is whether we will let the fire do its purifying work before the final Day, or after. The fifth challenge is the invitation to name people correctly — to speak *tselem* over those who have forgotten they bear it. This is a prophetic act. When you look at a person wrapped in a constructed identity and say — gently, firmly, with the authority of the New Covenant — "You are an image-bearer of the living God, and your name has already been written in the Lamb's Book of Life if you will receive it," you are doing something the culture cannot do, the political movement cannot do, and the therapeutic model cannot do. You are speaking a Name above every name over a life that has settled for lesser names. --- ## 🚀 What This Means For Us **For Spiritual Maturity:** Spiritual maturity in this hour requires the discipline of *seeing people before we see their labels* — and it also requires the courage to refuse to let labels have the final word. We grow in maturity when we can hold sexual immorality and imago Dei in the same sentence without collapsing one into the other. The mature saint does not ignore sin; the mature saint also does not let sin become the loudest noun in anyone's sentence. God's loudest noun over humanity is still *son* and *daughter*. **For Kingdom Obedience:** Kingdom obedience calls us to tear down every identity exaltation that has been raised above the knowledge of God — starting in our own hearts, then in our households, then in our communities. This is not a culture war strategy; it is a priestly function. We cast down imaginations (2 Corinthians 10:5). We do not do this with contempt; we do it with the authority of those who have already been freed from their own false identities by the same grace we are extending. **For Prophetic Posture:** The prophetic posture for this moment is to abide long enough in the knowledge of who God says we are — and who He says every image-bearer is — that we become fluent in the Father's language about people. The prophet does not merely denounce what is false. The prophet *declares* what is true. In this hour, the truest prophetic word we can carry is not "your identity is wrong" but "you have a Father who already knows your real name, and He is running toward you right now." --- ## 🔥 A Closing Prayer Father, I come before You as Your son, as one who carries Your Word in this moment as a steward and not an owner, standing between the heavens and the earth in the authority of the New Covenant You have established in the blood of Jesus Christ. I thank You that You have not left humanity without a witness — that even in the cultural confusion of this hour, You are pressing truth through unexpected cracks, bringing even secular minds to the edge of a distinction that Your Word named first. I agree with Your will in this: that every false identity be gently, mercifully, powerfully dismantled — not by our cleverness or our culture war, but by the patient, relentless, pursuing love of a Father who keeps the robe ready and watches the road. I ask You, by Your Spirit, to give Your people eyes to see image-bearers where the world sees labels, and the courage to speak the Name of the Father over every life that has settled for a lesser name. Let the fire of Your holiness purify what needs to be purified — beginning in us — and let what remains be the gold of genuine sonship, the unshakeable identity that neither pride flags nor religious performance nor any other human marking can give or take away. You are the One who names. You are the One who robes. You are the One who runs. Let us run with You. Amen. --- ## 📝 Scripture References & Original Language Notes | Passage (NKJV) | Original Language | Theological Insight | | --- | --- | --- | | Genesis 1:26–27 | צֶלֶם (tselem, TSEH-lem) — image, representation | Humanity's foundational identity is received, not constructed;
