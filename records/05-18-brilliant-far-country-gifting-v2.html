# 05-18 Call Reflection: Gifting, Identity, and the Architecture of a Redeemed Mind **Title:** 05-18 at 21:57 — Brilliant in the Far Country: Gifting, Identity, and the Architecture of a Redeemed Mind **Recorded:** 2026-05-18 21:57:08 **Source Discernment:** C — Recorded Conversation or Event (Live Capture) **Classification:** Call Reflection ## Opening Observation As I sat with this conversation — laughing with my brother Samuel, remembering, building, and dreaming alongside him — something underneath the surface of the banter arrested me in a way I cannot easily shake. There is a thread woven through this entire exchange that goes deeper than shared nostalgia or entrepreneurial excitement: it is the thread of minds that were made to think differently, pressed into environments that expected conformity, and forced to either surrender or innovate. What I am seeing as I sit with this now is not simply two men swapping stories — I am watching a conversation between two people whose intelligence was, at various points in their lives, an offense to the rooms they entered, and who had to learn, in their own ways, how to hold weight without breaking. **Why This Matters:** This matters because the Body of Christ is full of brothers and sisters who carry uncommon gifting — minds that bend around corners, spirits that perceive what others pass over — and the Church has often done with them exactly what those classrooms did: expected the standard cut and been confounded when someone showed up shaped entirely differently. If we cannot hold two truths simultaneously — that many of God’s called were genuinely formidable before their conversion, *and* that their gifting was being squandered on things that could not hold it — we will fail to preach the full gospel to the Samuel-shaped people still out in the far country, and we will fail to steward what God has already redeemed in those who have come home. The conversation I had with my brother is, beneath its rough and unfiltered surface, a testimony about grace hidden in the engineering of a life — and what happens when a man begins to recognize that the same mind that held weight in a physics experiment, or silenced a classroom with knowledge no one expected him to have, was never random. It was always preparation. ## The Worldly Mind’s Interpretation From the natural vantage point, what transpired in this conversation is entirely intelligible without reference to God. Two intelligent, unconventional men — one with autism spectrum tendencies, one a rough-edged transplant who learned to weaponize his intelligence in hostile social environments — are bonding over shared experiences of being underestimated, proving people wrong, and carving out identity through wit, force, or both. The world would file this under resilience psychology and the narrative of the outsider who overcomes. It would celebrate the strategic thinking about investor class dynamics, the entrepreneurial instinct emerging in Samuel’s fraud investigation business model, the defiance of insufficient authority structures, and the shared pride of being the clever ones who saw through the system early. A secular analyst would call both of us gifted nonconformists who learned to work systems rather than be worked by them — and they would not be entirely wrong. The natural framework would also account for the emotional arc my brother described: the sting of seeing someone else’s sunny afternoon while sitting in grey England, the anger that preceded acceptance, the eventual reframe from comparison to appreciation, filing it all under cognitive restructuring and emotional intelligence. That framing would be affirming, even celebratory, and it would leave both of us exactly where we were. **The Limitation:** What the natural mind cannot perceive is that the gifting underneath these stories does not originate in neurology or environment alone, and that the emotional moments woven through the conversation carry more than psychological significance. The mind that is not yet illuminated by the Spirit will honor the gift while missing the Giver — it will marvel at the vessel without tracing the hand that shaped it. More critically, it has no grid for understanding what *kind* of intelligence this actually is, what it is *for*, or what it costs to spend it on the far country indefinitely. Natural wisdom can identify capability but cannot name its origin, cannot perceive that this mind was formed before the foundation of the world for a specific inheritance in the Kingdom of God, and cannot understand that every year it is spent in the service of earthly power systems is a year of compounding spiritual loss. The natural lens sees the performance without the Playwright. It sees a coping mechanism where the Spirit has placed a mercy, a coincidence where the Spirit has placed a pattern, a late-night conversation about finance and cannabis markets where the Spirit is pressing something about the direction of a builder’s anointing that has not yet found its appointed foundation. ## What the Spirit Reveals Through It I want to begin where my own memory meets this conversation — with the paper structure in that physics experiment. The teacher set it up expecting failure, or at least expecting a predictable failure point. The majority of minds went to the most obvious solution: cut along straight lines, create a V-shape, distribute the weight conventionally. Almost every time, it ripped. But I made mine with all curves. No straight edges. And it held. As I sit with that memory now, I am not simply remembering a science class. I am seeing something the Spirit is pressing me toward with unmistakable clarity: the wisdom of God routinely looks curved to a world that only trusts straight lines. What the natural mind calls inefficient or unconventional is often precisely the architecture that will bear the weight the conventional structure cannot. The apostle Paul writes in 1 Corinthians 1:27 — *“But God has chosen the foolish things of the world to put to shame the wise, and God has chosen the weak things of the world to put to shame the things which are mighty.”* The Greek word translated “chosen” is ἐξελέξατο (exelexato, ex-eh-LEX-ah-toh) — a deliberate, purposive selection, the kind a craftsman makes when he reaches past the obvious tool for the one suited to the actual task. God does not accidentally produce unusual minds. He selects them. He reaches past the conventional vessel for the one whose architecture He has already been shaping toward a specific load-bearing function. What arrests me as I listened to Samuel describe walking into that physics classroom — rough kid, puffer jacket, smelling of cigarettes, every external signal communicating that he did not belong in the top stream — is that he walked in carrying knowledge no one in that room expected him to have. He had just come from a college-level course. The room judged the jacket. God had been filling the vessel. The teacher asked a question he did not expect anyone to answer, and Samuel answered it. I recognize this pattern not only in both of our stories, but across the entire arc of Scripture. Joseph walked into Pharaoh’s court carrying the interpretation of a dream no one in Egypt’s wisdom establishment could unlock. Daniel walked into Nebuchadnezzar’s throne room carrying revelation that exceeded the collective capacity of the empire’s wisest men. The pattern is consistent: God fills the vessel before He places it in the room. The room’s shock is not the point — the filling was always the point. But what I cannot escape, sitting with the full weight of this conversation before the Lord, is that so much of what Samuel and I discussed was the vocabulary of what Jesus calls the far country. In Luke 15, Jesus tells a story about a son who took his inheritance early and went to a distant land and wasted it in reckless living. The Greek word translated “prodigal” is ἀσώτως (asōtōs, ah-SO-toce) — and it does not primarily mean *evil*. It means *wasteful*. Without preservation. The prodigal was not necessarily a fool by natural standards — he was a young man who took something of genuine value and spent it on things that could not hold it. That is what I hear underneath this conversation. Two men of genuine capacity, spending it on things that evaporate. The forty-dollar half gram. The decision to stay up through a high rather than sleep. The scam letters and the business letters and the chemistry diagram. The pride in having mapped the price tags of laws for the wealthy. All of it is real intelligence. All of it is being spent on things that cannot hold the weight of what these minds were made to carry. And I recognize myself in every line of it, because this was my voice too. I was proud of having sent those letters. I was proud of knowing when to break a law and when to pay the fine. What the Spirit is showing me now is that I was not wrong that laws often function as price tags for the wealthy — that is largely empirically observable, and Scripture does not require me to be naive about the mechanics of fallen systems. But there is a vast difference between *knowing how the world works* and *building your identity on being the kind of man who knows how the world works*. I was doing the latter. Samuel and I were both doing the latter. And the danger of that posture is not primarily moral — it is covenantal. Jeremiah 17:5 is not subtle: *“Cursed is the man who trusts in man and makes flesh his strength, whose heart departs from the LORD.”* The Hebrew word translated “flesh” is בָּשָׂר (basar, bah-SAR) — mortal capacity, human strength, the confidence of the creature untethered from its Creator. And the verse that follows is what stops me: *“For he shall be like a shrub in the desert, and shall not see when good comes, but shall inhabit the parched places in the wilderness, in a salt land which is not inhabited.”* The image the Spirit shows me through that verse is not destruction but sterility — sustained survival in depleted soil, never reaching full flourishing. Samuel at fifteen, scamming consultants, building persuasion skills, reading rooms: a shrub, surviving. Myself — mapping tax codes, choosing strategic rule-breaks, calculating risk tolerance: a shrub, surviving. We were functional. We were not flourishing. I am also sitting with the paper structure memory in a different register now — not as physics, but as theology of hiddenness. The Hebrew word for wilderness is מִדְבָּר (midbar, mid-BAR), and it carries within it the root דָּבַר (dabar, dah-VAR), meaning *to speak* or *to lead*. The wilderness is etymologically the place of divine speech and divine leading. Moses was formed in it. Israel was shaped in it. John the Baptist inhabited it. Jesus was driven into it. And I mentioned in this conversation, almost in passing, that I have had a long commute for most of my life — an hour and a half as a child, an hour each way now. I said it as a common thread. But the Spirit is pressing me to unfold it here: that commute has functioned as a מִדְבָּר (mid-BAR) in my life — a daily formation corridor, a place where what began as mere travel has become sanctuary. Two hours of worship every day. The same road that once just separated me from the destination has become the place where I am formed for what the destination requires. I did not choose this pattern. He threaded it through my life before I knew to call it formation. There is a word my sister surfaced in a related conversation from the Hebrew of Jeremiah — בְּצֻרוֹת (betsurot, beh-tsoo-ROHT) — meaning something fortified, walled in, inaccessible. The root carries the image of a fortified city, walls built not to imprison but to protect. And as I sit with the way certain memories and certain callings remain sealed in a person until the appointed moment, I am struck by the pastoral mercy embedded in that image. What if the walls around certain memories are not walls of shame but walls of divine appointment — standing guard until the soul is strong enough to receive what is behind them? Joseph spent years in a pit and a prison before the dream given in his youth was ready to unfold. The walls around that season were not the cruelty of his brothers; they were, in retrospect, the formation period without which he would not have had the capacity to steward what was coming. What is fortified is not imprisoned. What is fortified is being kept. The moment in this conversation that landed hardest on me spiritually was when Samuel made his observation about investors — that the most powerful actors in any industry are not the operators or the politicians, but the investors whose capital underlies everything else. He is right. And I find myself pressing into what the apostle Paul would say in a completely different register: *“Eye has not seen, nor ear heard, nor have entered into the heart of man the things which God has prepared for those who love Him”* (1 Corinthians 2:9). The Greek verb translated “prepared” is ἡτοίμασεν (hētoimasen, hay-TOY-mah-sen) — a perfect-tense verb implying completion. What God has prepared for His children is already fully ready; the limitation is on the receiver’s perception, not the gift’s existence. The Kingdom of God operates on an investment logic so far above the hedge fund paradigm that it makes the most sophisticated capital structure look like a child’s accounting. God Himself is the Investor of investors — and He has made His children co-heirs with Christ, as Romans 8:17 declares, using the Greek συγκληρονόμοι (sygklēronomoi, soong-klay-ro-NO-moy) — co-inheritors, joint-heirs by legal right, the compound prefix συν- (with/together) emphasizing that Christ’s inheritance does not shrink to accommodate co-heirs. The question is not whether Samuel understands investor class dynamics. He does. The question is whether he has yet understood that the greatest transfer of wealth in history is not a financial event — it is a resurrection event. And he was made to understand that. The moment I said almost in passing — *“Before Christ, that was probably the most pivotal moment that deleted any international anxiety I had about business”* — I notice now that I marked it as *before Christ*. That phrase did not mean before Jesus existed. It meant before my conversion. Before the Lordship. Before the cross became personal. There was a before. And there is an after. That is the gospel in shorthand buried inside a casual conversation about old scams and legal maneuvering. I am after. I pray, with everything in me, that Samuel will one day mark time the same way. The conversation turned at one point to the moment Samuel made a logically airtight case to a teacher who was trying to remove him from class: *“If you keep me here, I’m still not going to do the work. If you kick me out, I’m definitely not going to do the work. Your best bet is keeping me where you can see me.”* That is not rebellion. That is Socratic clarity in a fifteen-year-old. And the system did not know what to do with it, so it ejected him. I think about myself falling asleep front and center in physics class with both earbuds in and a hood up — and still getting straight A’s. The system could not contain us. And what I am pressing into now is *why* — not for pride, but for stewardship. Something in both of us knew those environments were too small. We kept falling asleep in the middle of them. Proverbs 20:5 says, *“Counsel in the heart of man is like deep water, but a man of understanding will draw it out.”* The Hebrew word for “counsel” is עֵצָה (etsah, ay-TSAH) — purpose, plan, deep design. Deep water in the heart of man. Samuel has deep water in him. And deep water was made to be drawn out, not suppressed, not wasted on surfaces. The Spirit is not satisfied with Samuel staying at the surface. And I do not think Samuel is either, if he is honest about it. Augustine of Hippo — himself a man of enormous natural brilliance who spent years in the far country before his conversion — wrote in his *Confessions*: *“You have made us for Yourself, and our heart is restless until it rests in You.”* I did not understand that sentence when I first encountered it, because I thought my restlessness was a character flaw. What I am seeing now is that restlessness is a homing signal. It is the soul refusing to settle for less than the environment it was created for. Samuel staying up through the best high of his life because the high was too good to waste on sleep — I hear in that a man whose capacity for *wanting* far exceeds what any substance can deliver. The desire underneath it is legitimate. The object is catastrophically insufficient. This is not a praise of the drug use. It is a recognition that the hunger beneath it is real, and that the real hunger has a real answer. There is a thread I want to hold about the automation principle Samuel articulated regarding his fraud investigation business: *“If you are going to move on to bigger and better things in business, you need to stabilize and get automated.”* This is genuine wisdom. And in the Spirit, it sounds like this: before God can release a servant into greater dimensions of Kingdom assignment, He first ensures that the foundational disciplines have become habitual — not forced, not performance-based, not dependent on emotional momentum, but structural. The pod, the note, the pen, the capture system, the practice of listening to my own recordings to mirror and process what God is speaking — these are not self-help productivity habits. They are spiritual formation made structural. The equivalent of Elijah rebuilding the altar with twelve stones before the fire fell — deliberate, ordered, covenantal. The altar must be ordered before the consuming presence arrives. Formation precedes manifestation. And in that conversation, I said something that I want to pause and hold: *“You are building the infrastructure in the world for economy and business that I have been building in the spirit and my habitual daily life. It’s the exact same thing.”* I said it as an observation. The Spirit is pressing it now as a revelation. What Samuel is constructing in the visible is a natural-world mirror image of what God has been building in me in the spirit — and that is not coincidence. That is the pattern of Matthew 6:10: *“Your kingdom come. Your will be done on earth as it is in heaven.”* The Kingdom does not arrive as theological abstraction. It arrives as structured reality that maps the invisible into the visible. I also notice something in the emotional texture of this conversation that I do not want to pass over without naming. There is genuine warmth here — a brotherhood that is real, a delight in each other’s company that is not manufactured. Samuel calling at three in the morning his time because the high was too good to waste on sleep — that is absurd and funny and also somehow tender. And I do not want to rush past that. The capacity for friendship, for recognizing one another across distance and time and timezone, is itself a grace. But what I cannot escape is that so much of the laughter was laughter of the far country — the shared pride of men who had learned to map the edges of every system they entered, the warmth of two people who recognize each other as the clever ones who saw through things early. That recognition is real. What the Spirit is showing me is that it was always pointing toward a recognition of a different kind — not the recognition of two men who know how the world works, but the recognition of two men who are beginning to understand they were made for something the world cannot offer. I was proud of having sent those letters. I was proud of knowing when to break a law and when to pay the fine. I thought that was wisdom. The Scripture calls it something else: *“There is a way that seems right to a man, but its end is the way of death”* (Proverbs 14:12). The Hebrew word translated “right” is יָשָׁר (yashar, yah-SHAR) — straight, upright, as it appears to human perception. The deception is precisely in its apparent reasonableness. The last thing I want to hold from this conversation is something about the chemistry diagram. I sat front and center — deliberately — in a class I did not particularly enjoy, filled my notebook with diagrams, and asked my teacher after class whether the synthesis path I had drawn would yield cyanide. She told me she would not confirm it, which means I was right. And I remember being pleased. Sitting with that memory now, I feel something more complex than nostalgia. I feel the weight of a mind that had no sanctified container for its curiosity. I was genuinely interested in how things work, genuinely drawn to elegant systems, genuinely alive in the presence of a difficult problem — and none of it was aimed at anything beyond the satisfaction of knowing. That is not evil. But it is orphaned. A mind like that, aimed at the Kingdom of God — aimed at understanding the deep structures of redemption, the mechanics of covenant, the chemistry of grace — becomes something entirely different. It becomes a vessel. It becomes useful. It becomes, by the mercy of God, the thing it was always supposed to be. The curved paper held the weight. The question the Spirit presses me with now is not whether the structure was impressive. It is: what weight was it built to hold? ## Implications and Challenges Grace must lead here, because nothing in this conversation was unusual. This is how millions of gifted, unchurched, underchurched, or pre-conversion men talk. And the Church has often responded to men like Samuel — and like the version of me that spoke in this recording — by either ignoring them entirely or offering them a sanitized, culturally neutered version of Christianity that insults their intelligence and asks nothing of their giftedness. That is its own kind of failure, and I want to name it gently but clearly before anything else. The Body of Christ needs sharp men. It does not need sharp men who have been sanded down to palatability at the cost of their actual capacity. The gifting that ran at full speed in the far country does not die at conversion — it is redirected. And the Church must be wise enough to receive it. The principle that laws function as price tags for the wealthy is empirically observable, and I do not require myself to be naive about the mechanics of fallen systems. But the posture of celebrating strategic law-breaking as a mark of sophistication is a posture that, if left unchallenged, trains the soul to treat ethics as a cost-benefit calculation rather than a covenantal reality. The Scripture never suggests that the man who can afford the fine is therefore free from accountability before God. Proverbs 14:12 is still true. The price tag logic is clever. It is not wisdom. There is a distinction the Spirit is pressing between *knowing how the world works* and *building your identity on being the kind of man who knows how the world works* — and that distinction carries enormous consequences for how a man relates to authority, to covenant, and ultimately to God Himself. The drug use in this conversation is not the central issue — it is a symptom. The real issue beneath it is the same issue beneath the law-breaking and the investor-class admiration and the school-system defiance: the search for an experience, a structure, a system that is actually *large enough* to hold us. The joint, the scam, the late-night finance conversation — they are all attempts to feel alive, significant, and in control. They are also all temporary. The αὐτάρκης (ow-TAR-kays) contentment that Paul describes in Philippians 4:11 — internally sufficient regardless of external circumstance, *learned* through the full range of experience — is the answer to the same hunger that sends a man to a forty-dollar half gram at three in the morning. The gospel offers a permanent answer to a legitimate hunger, and the Church must be bold enough to say so without pathologizing the hunger itself. The commute-as-wilderness thread carries a quietly radical implication for the contemporary saint: formation does not require a dedicated institutional context. We have been taught to wait for the right program, the right small group, the right curriculum before taking formation seriously. But the מִדְבָּר (mid-BAR) does not wait for the institution. The voice of God spoke through forty years of desert wandering, through a cistern, through a prison cell, through a long bus ride, through an hour of worship in a car with no working stereo. The implication is not that structured community is unnecessary. It is that we must stop treating the long road as wasted time. The formation is already underway. The question is whether we are present to it. The automation-before-expansion principle carries a solemn challenge for those in ministry or Kingdom service who are perpetually in reactive mode — always responding to the urgent, never building the structural. Samuel’s business insight translates directly: you cannot scale what has not been stabilized. If the prayer life, the Scripture engagement, the Sabbath rhythm, the accountability structures are not yet habitual — not legalistic, but structural — then every expansion of Kingdom assignment will simply accelerate depletion rather than fruitfulness. The altar must be rebuilt before the fire falls. The structural precedes the spectacular. Finally, I want to name something about how I engage Samuel going forward, and how any believing brother or sister might take this as a model. I cannot preach at him. I can testify. I can let the phrase “before Christ” sit in the air between us and trust the Holy Spirit to do with it what only He can do. Samuel is not my project. He is my brother, in the deepest natural sense — and I believe, in the deepest covenantal sense, he is intended to be. The most powerful thing I can do is not argue him into the Kingdom. It is to remain visibly, undeniably different from the man I was in this recording, without pretending that man did not exist. The genuine friendship, the real laughter, the brotherhood that spans timezones and late nights — these are the relational infrastructure through which the Spirit moves. Real relationship requires the freedom to be known — rough edges and all — without the fear of spiritual management. The Spirit moves through real relationship. That is worth protecting. ## What This Means For Us **For Spiritual Maturity:** Sitting with this conversation has pressed me to examine how I talk about my pre-conversion life — not to glorify it, but not to flatten it either. Authentic testimony requires honesty about what was genuinely present in the far country: real gifting, real intelligence, real relationship, real laughter. Pretending I was merely wicked before Christ misrepresents what redemption actually redeemed. Maturity means I can hold both truths simultaneously: the far country was genuinely wasteful *and* I was genuinely gifted in it, and the conversion of one does not erase the other. The man who learned to navigate earthly systems with precision is the same man who now reads covenant structure and hears the heartbeat of God in a legal metaphor. The gifting did not die. It came home. That is the testimony I am steward of — and the testimony I pray Samuel will one day carry alongside me. **For Kingdom Obedience:** The immediate call is structural: build the infrastructure before God is asked to fill it. Do not wait for inspiration to sustain what only habitual discipline can hold. The pod, the pen, the note, the daily worship in the commute, the practice of pressing received revelation into written form — these are not optional productivity strategies. They are the infrastructure of prophetic faithfulness. Kingdom obedience also means holding Samuel before the Lord not as a project or a soul-winning metric, but as a man who carries builder’s anointing that has not yet been fully surrendered to its rightful Source. Continued relationship, honest testimony when it fits, and trust that God — who knew Samuel before the foundation of the world — is doing something in him that I am not always privy to and do not need to control. Intercession is the most structural thing I can do for him. **For Prophetic Posture:** The prophetic word latent in this conversation is a word about timing and harvest. The mind that reads investor structures and traces capital flows through shell companies is the mind that, redeemed and consecrated, can read the movements of the Kingdom with the same precision. The servant of God who learns to abide in the daily rhythm of formation — in the מִדְבָּר (mid-BAR) of the long commute, in the בְּצֻרוֹת (beh-tsoo-ROHT) of the hidden season — will not be surprised by the weight God places on the structure. They will have already practiced holding it. I am watching for the moment — not manufacturing it — when Samuel’s restlessness finds the right question. I am staying awake. I am praying. I am available. And I am trusting that the God who brought me home from the far country has not forgotten the brother I left behind in it. ## A Closing Prayer Father, I come before You as one who knows what it is to be brilliant in the far country — to have deep water in me and no understanding of where it came from or what it was for. I thank You that You did not leave me there. I thank You that the phrase “before Christ” is now a real dividing line in my history, not a metaphor — that there was a before, and there is an after, and the after is held by Your covenant and not by my performance. I thank You for the curved paper that held the weight, for the long commutes that became wilderness corridors of Your voice, for every fortified wall around memories and callings that has stood not in cruelty but in the precision of a Shepherd who knows the exact moment His sheep can bear what He has kept for them. I bring Samuel before You now — his mind, his builder’s spirit, his restlessness that outpaces every substance and every system he has tried to fill it with — and I do not bring him as a problem to be solved but as a man You made, whom I love, and whose deep water I believe You intend to draw out for Your purposes and Your glory. I ask You, Lord, by the blood of Jesus, under the authority of the New Covenant, in agreement with Your revealed will that none should perish but all should come to repentance — to be at work in him in ways I cannot see and cannot manufacture. And for every Samuel-shaped man and woman who reads this reflection, who recognizes themselves in the laughter and the system-reading and the late-night conversations that were somehow never quite enough — may they hear in this word the sound of a door that is still open, a Father who is still watching the road, and a robe that was sewn for them before they ever knew they needed it. Let the structure hold — not because it is impressive, but because the curves were shaped by Your hand, and You do not build what cannot bear what You intend to place upon it. To You be the glory, the dominion, and the honor — in this age and in the age to come. Amen. ## Scripture References & Original Language Notes | **Passage (NKJV)** | **Original Language** | **Theological Insight** | |------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------| | Luke 15:13 — “wasted his possessions with prodigal living” | ἀσώτως (asōtōs, ah-SO-toce) — wastefully, without preservation | The root ἄσωτος means “unsaved” or “unpreserved” — prodigality is primarily a theology of waste, not merely moral failure;
