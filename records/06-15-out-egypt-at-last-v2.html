# 06-15 Call Reflection: Faith, Providence, and the Inner Judas — Spiritual & Business Breakthrough **Title:** 06-15 at 18:18 — Out of Egypt at Last: Providence, the Wilderness, and the Inner Judas Who Won’t Let Go **Recorded:** 2026-06-15 18:18 (estimated from internal conversation context) **Source Discernment:** C — Recorded Conversation or Event (Live Capture) **Classification:** Call Reflection ## Opening Observation Listening back to this conversation with my brother Samuel, I am struck by how much kingdom revelation surfaced in the middle of what began as a casual late-night health check and a business update. What started with questions about kidney pain and indigestion became, almost without either of us fully steering it there, a raw theological confession about trust — about the interior architecture of belief, about where a man actually goes when his body threatens him, when money is slow, when systems fail. The Spirit has a way of doing that: He will use the most ordinary conversation to press on the exact places where faith has not yet fully displaced fear. This is what I am sitting with now. **Why This Matters:** The question of where we actually place our trust is not a peripheral discipleship issue — it is the central one. It is possible to confess with the mouth that Jesus is Lord and still, in practice, construct an entire life built on institutional scaffolding: insurance plans, hospital systems, financial portfolios, corporate structures, government protections. Not one of those things is sinful in itself. But when a man cannot imagine life without them — when the thought of stepping outside them produces more anxiety than the thought of stepping outside of prayer — something has been revealed about the true location of his faith. For those of us who feel called to an exodus, whether interior or exterior, the question is always the same: has the Egypt left us, even if we have left Egypt? This conversation pressed on that question in me with surprising force. ## The Worldly Mind’s Interpretation A reasonable, well-meaning person listening to this conversation would likely hear two young men — one anxious about his health, one stretched financially — talking themselves into a kind of idealistic anti-institutionalism that is charming but ultimately naive. The natural mind would say: hospitals exist because bodies fail in ways prayer cannot always reverse; insurance exists because catastrophe is statistically inevitable; and the enthusiasm about a business windfall, while encouraging, is the kind of excitement that needs to be tempered by realistic projections and fiscal discipline. The natural mind would commend the checkup, recommend the insurance, and gently caution against reading divine providence into a single viral post that generated one large sale. It would call this wisdom. It would call the alternative recklessness dressed in religious language. **The Limitation:** But the natural mind operates exclusively within the closed system of what is already visible and already quantifiable, and it has no categories for what God does when He moves. It cannot weigh the testimony of a man who survived ten hours of renal failure and was told by physicians he should be dead. It cannot account for the sudden momentum of a business that began — as my brother said plainly — only when he simply started posting because he sensed the Lord was telling him to go. The reasoning mind is a good servant but a poor sovereign when it comes to things of the Spirit, because it requires its evidence to arrive in measurable, repeatable forms. But the God of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob has always been in the habit of doing precisely the thing the reasonable observer said was statistically, medically, or financially impossible. To reduce the testimonies in this conversation to optimism bias or survivorship anecdote is to miss entirely what the Spirit is trying to say through them. ## What the Spirit Reveals Through It As I listen to the arc of this conversation, I am first arrested by something simple: the conversation began with the body, and it ended with the Kingdom. Brother Samuel opened with concern about physical symptoms, and I found myself — almost instinctively — redirecting not toward the medical system as the final court of appeal, but toward the living God who holds both the body and the outcome. I did not intend it as a theological statement in the moment. But sitting with it now, I recognize that the instinct itself was the fruit of something the Spirit has been building in me: a reordering of trust that is still incomplete, still contested, still being worked out with fear and trembling, but undeniably directional. The passage that rises immediately in me is from Matthew 6, where the Lord says not to be anxious about what you shall eat, or drink, or wear — and then, crucially, He grounds that command not in positive thinking or self-sufficiency, but in the Father’s knowledge: *“For your heavenly Father knows that you need all these things”* (Matthew 6:32, NKJV). The Greek word translated “knows” here is οἶδεν (oiden, OY-den) — a perfect tense verb carrying the sense of complete, settled knowing. Not that He is learning about your needs. Not that He is monitoring them and will eventually respond. He already, completely, thoroughly knows. This is not a promise that the Father will make life comfortable. It is a declaration that your needs are never outside His awareness, and therefore anxiety — not the symptom, but the posture of soul that says *I must secure this myself* — is always a theological statement. It is the heart saying: *He may not know in time, or He may not act, so I must cover myself.* This is where I began to feel the weight of what I had said aloud to my brother about the Inner Judas — a phrase that surfaced again in this conversation and that has been with me since a meditation back in February. The Inner Judas is not merely unbelief in the abstract. It is the specific, personalized, internal traitor that takes the form of the reasonable hedge. It sounds like wisdom. It presents itself as stewardship. But underneath it is a fundamental distrust of the sufficiency of God that has been so thoroughly normalized by the culture we were born into that we barely recognize it as distrust at all. Insurance is not evil. But the inner state that says *I cannot be at peace without insurance* is a form of spiritual bondage that needs to be named as such — and gently, carefully renounced. I think about the wilderness generation. They had seen the Red Sea part. They had eaten manna that fell from heaven. They had drunk water that came from a struck rock in a desert. And yet every new threat — hunger, thirst, enemy armies, delayed leadership — produced immediate panic and a nostalgic longing for Egypt. Not because Egypt was good, but because Egypt was familiar. The systems of Egypt were known quantities. They had learned to survive within them. The wilderness demanded something Egypt never did: daily, moment-by-moment dependence on an invisible God who refused to give them more than one day’s supply at a time. The manna could not be hoarded. Exodus 16:20 records that when the people tried to store extra, it bred worms and stank. God was not being cruel. He was dismantling the Egyptian architecture of self-sufficiency from the inside out — and that dismantling is painful, and it looks, from the outside, like recklessness. I said something in this conversation that surprised even me as it came out: *“My Lord told me — the interior and exterior exodus.”* I believe that. I believe it more every day. The exterior exodus is the one that gets sung about and theorized — leaving the broken systems, the compromised institutions, the structures of Babylon that have metastasized into every corner of Western life. But the interior exodus is the one that actually determines whether the exterior one produces freedom or just relocation. You can leave the hospital system and still be in bondage to fear of your body. You can leave a corporate job and still be enslaved to the metrics of financial security. The exterior move means nothing if the interior Egypt is still governing. The Israelites left Egypt, but Egypt had not left them — and that generation died in the wilderness with their longing still pointed backward, toward Pharaoh’s table. The business conversation with my brother carried this same undercurrent, though on the surface it sounded like entrepreneurial planning. What struck me most was the moment he said: *“All I had to do, bro, was book a meeting. That was it.”* And I remembered — I genuinely remembered — the phone call where he told me he kept feeling the Lord saying *go, get started, post.* And I had sat and prayed and interceded for this. And then one video. Four hundred and eighty-two views. A sale worth over five thousand dollars. Enough to buy the car I have been without. In a single phone call. From a video that by any conventional marketing metric should not have performed at all. The Spirit is doing something here that I do not want to domesticate by calling it strategy. It is provision. It is the Lord saying *I know what you need, and I know when you need it, and I will move the exact moment I move and not a moment before.* Paul writes in Philippians 4:19 that *“my God shall supply all your need according to His riches in glory by Christ Jesus”* (NKJV). The Greek behind “shall supply” is πληρώσει (plērōsei, play-ROH-say) — from πληρόω, meaning to fill completely, to make full, to bring to completion. This is not a promissory note for abundance in the American prosperity-gospel sense. It is a declaration rooted in the riches of the risen Christ Himself — not the riches of market conditions or business acumen or the right tax structure, though He may use all of those things. The supply flows from who He is. And the vessel that receives that supply must be positioned in dependence, not self-sufficiency, because a vessel that has already filled itself with contingency plans has no room left for manna. The tax and business structures my brother and I discussed — the Dubai registration, the employee benefits, the UK company — I do not view those as unspiritual. Systems, when they are clean, can be tools. But I noticed something in my own spirit as we spoke: the excitement was real, but I wanted to be careful not to let the cleverness of the structure become the source of my confidence. The goal I named — thirty thousand dollars in gross revenue before I leave my job — was not arbitrary. It was a threshold of proof. *If God has done this, He will do this much.* That is a reasonable thing to say. But I also want to hold it with open hands. Because the Lord has never been bound by my thresholds. He may move faster. He may ask me to step out before the number is reached. And if I have hardened the threshold into a requirement, I have, functionally, made it a new form of the same institutional insurance I was critiquing. I come back to the moment I told my brother: *“Is anything too much for the Lord?”* That is the word given to Abraham in Genesis 18:14 — הֲיִפָּלֵא מֵיְהוָה דָּבָר (hayippaleh meYHWH davar, hah-yee-pah-LEH may-Yah-WEH dah-VAR) — *“Is anything too wonderful, too extraordinary, too hidden, for the LORD?”* The Hebrew root פָּלָא (pala, pah-LAH) carries the sense of something that surpasses what human capacity can accomplish or explain. God says this immediately before announcing that Sarah — ninety years old, long past the biological possibility of childbearing — will have a son. He is not asking for feedback. He is making a declaration that His ability to act is not subject to the same constraints that govern human possibility. When I said it to my brother about the thirty thousand dollars, I meant it. But I also felt the Spirit pressing me: *Do you mean it about the hospital? Do you mean it about the car? Do you mean it about the baby?* My wife is pregnant, and I said in the conversation that if it were my choice, I would do things differently than the hospital system prescribes. I stand by the instinct, even if the specifics need wisdom and prayer and mutual discernment. What I know is this: the first cry of every newborn enters the same world that the Spirit of God hovered over in Genesis 1:2 — and that רוּחַ (ruach, ROO-akh), that breath, that Spirit, that wind, is not confined to the delivery rooms that meet hospital accreditation standards. The Lord who knit my child together in the womb (Psalm 139:13) is not wringing His hands over hospital policy. He is sovereign over every threshold of entry into this world. That does not mean we are careless. It means we do not confuse protocol with protection. Protocol is man’s attempt to approximate what God guarantees to those who trust Him. And this brings me to the final thread the Spirit has been pulling through all of it: the confession at the end of this conversation, almost spoken as a throwaway line — *“No, that’s God, bro.”* My brother had just described the mechanics: four hundred and eighty-two views, one phone call, five thousand two hundred dollars. And I said: *That is God.* Not the algorithm. Not the offer structure. Not the ad spend. God. He takes, as Paul writes in 1 Corinthians 1:28, *“the things which are not, to bring to nothing the things that are”* (NKJV). The Greek there is τὰ μὴ ὄντα (ta mē onta, tah may ON-tah) — the things that do not exist, the things that have no standing, the things the world has not assigned value or power to. God specifically chooses those things. Not despite their weakness, but through it — so that no flesh should glory in His presence. This business was not built on a ten thousand dollar ad spend and a polished funnel. It was built on obedience to a prompting. *Start posting.* That is the theology of this moment. And I want to remember it when the system gets complicated and the numbers get large and the temptation arises to believe that our cleverness is what is making it work. ## Implications and Challenges There is a real danger, when God begins to move financially, of spiritualizing the momentum in a way that slowly displaces prayer as the actual engine. The early days of a venture, when you have nothing but obedience and a prompting, tend to be the most Spirit-saturated — because they have to be. When resources accumulate, it is easy, gradually and almost imperceptibly, to begin managing the work rather than yielding it. The challenge for my brother and me as this grows is to ensure that the prayer and intercession that preceded the first sale remains as central and as serious as it was when we had nothing. The manna must still be gathered fresh each morning. The structure cannot become the substitute for the Spirit. The conversation surfaced something I have said before but need to keep saying: Western Christianity has largely made peace with institutional dependency in a way that has severely atrophied the faith-muscle required for actual miracle. When the first recourse for every physical symptom is the insurance portal, and the first recourse for every financial threat is the credit line, and the first recourse for every question of governance is the policy manual — prayer becomes the fourth or fifth option rather than the first. It is not that those resources are always wrong to use. It is that the order reveals the actual hierarchy of trust. Where do I go first? That answer tells me who my functional lord is, whatever my confessional lord may be. The Inner Judas language deserves more than a passing reference. The traitor within is not always dramatic. He rarely presents as outright unbelief. More often he presents as caution, as maturity, as the voice that says: *Yes, but you also have to be realistic.* And realistic, in that framing, always means: constrained by what human systems can accomplish or insure against. The antidote is not recklessness. It is childlike faith — which Jesus did not commend as a nice sentiment but as a literal prerequisite for entering the Kingdom (Matthew 18:3). Childlike faith is not the absence of discernment. It is the presence of a trust that has not yet learned to be embarrassed by dependence. The modern Western hospital system is not the enemy, but it is also not the savior. My ten hours of renal failure that the doctors said should have killed me is a testimony, not an argument against medicine. It is a witness that the God who made the kidney is not surprised when it fails, and is not limited by what the textbooks say should happen next. The problem is not going to a doctor. The problem is constructing a worldview in which God is the backup plan and medicine is the primary one. The order is everything. Finally, I want to name something about the business vision with clarity and sobriety: God-blessed prosperity is real, and it is biblical — but it arrives with a stewardship that the prosperity-gospel movement has almost entirely failed to preach. The Abrahamic blessing was never primarily about Abraham’s comfort. It was about making him a vehicle of blessing to all the families of the earth (Genesis 12:3). Thirty thousand dollars, or three hundred thousand, only matters Kingdom-wise if it is flowing through hands that remain open. The structures — Dubai, UK, employee benefits — are fine tools. But the spirit behind them must be generosity and mission, not clever accumulation for its own sake. If the money God is releasing is going to mean something, it has to be stewarded toward the purposes He had in mind when He released it. ## What This Means For Us **For Spiritual Maturity:** Spiritual maturity is not the absence of hardship or the arrival of comfort. It is the deep, tested reordering of trust so that God is not merely confessed as sovereign but actually, practically treated as sovereign — in the doctor’s office, in the empty bank account, in the delivery room, and in the business meeting. The conversations I am having with my brother are training me in this. Every moment where I said *that’s God* instead of *that’s the algorithm* was a small act of confession that shapes the interior man. Maturity is made of those small confessions, accumulated over years, until the whole architecture of the soul has been rebuilt around a different center. **For Kingdom Obedience:** The call right now is to continue in the prayer and intercession that opened this door, to give my brother his two weeks as I promised my manager because integrity in small things is the prerequisite for authority in large ones, and to hold the thirty-thousand-dollar threshold with open hands — willing to step out sooner if the Spirit says go, and willing to wait longer if He says remain. Obedience is specific and immediate, not general and eventual. **For Prophetic Posture:** The prophetic posture required in this season is the posture of the man standing at the Jordan: feet in the water before the river parts (Joshua 3:13). The priests did not wait for proof before stepping in. The parting came as a consequence of the step, not as a prerequisite for it. To remain in a watching and waiting posture that refuses to move until all conditions are guaranteed is not wisdom — it is the old Egypt speaking again. The prophetic person keeps interceding, keeps posting, keeps honoring commitments, keeps praying for the sick brother, keeps trusting the Lord with the pregnant wife — and watches the waters recede. ## A Closing Prayer Father, I come before You as one of Your servants — a co-heir with Christ, under the New Covenant sealed in His blood — and I bring this conversation, this evening, this season, into agreement with Your will and Your word. You are the God for whom nothing is too wonderful, too extraordinary, too far beyond the boundary of what You can accomplish. You survived my ten hours of renal failure with me. You moved my brother to post when it made no earthly sense. You are moving in the finances of this household and in the households of those who are trusting You in this season. I thank You for that. I ask You now to keep dismantling the Inner Judas — every careful, reasonable, respectable compartment of unbelief that I have constructed and called wisdom. Teach me the difference between wise stewardship and faithless hedging. Cover my brother Samuel with Your hand tonight, whatever that pressing pain is, and let him wake in the morning knowing that his healer is not the doctor’s office but the same Jesus who made mud from the ground and pressed it into blind eyes. Cover my wife and our coming child with the same ruach that hovered over the deep before anything existed, and let Your sovereign care be more real to us than any protocol or policy. We are in the last stretch of this wilderness, Lord. The Jordan is close. Lead us through. May everything that comes next — the money, the companies, the platform, the influence — flow through us as a blessing to every family it touches, to the glory of Your name alone. Amen. ## Scripture References & Original Language Notes | **Passage (NKJV)** | **Original Language** | **Theological Insight** | |---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------| | Matthew 6:32 — “your heavenly Father knows that you need all these things” | οἶδεν (oiden, OY-den) — perfect tense, settled and complete knowing | The Father’s knowledge of our needs is not reactive or developing;
