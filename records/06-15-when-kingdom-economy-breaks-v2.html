# 06-15 Call Reflection: Kingdom Economy, Brotherhood & Spiritual Breakthrough **Title:** 06-15 at 15:00 — When the Kingdom Economy Breaks Through **Recorded:** 2026-06-15 15:00:09 **Source Discernment:** C — Recorded Conversation or Event (Live Capture / Phone Call) **Classification:** Call Reflection ## Opening Observation I was on the phone with my brother Samuel when something shifted in the conversation — what began as excited news about a business closing became something I can only describe as a Kingdom moment breaking through ordinary life. There was a \$5,000 consulting deal, a baby on the way, a car almost out of gas, and somewhere underneath all of it, the unmistakable fingerprints of God moving in the margins of two men who are still being made. What arrested me most was not the money — it was the moment my brother said, *“This is the only time I am doing it with God now. I’ve never done it with Jesus. Now I’m doing it with Jesus and it’s working.”* That sentence has stayed with me like a burning coal. **Why This Matters:** The Kingdom of God does not arrive in the places we expect it to arrive. It does not wait until a man is fully polished, fully repentant, or fully sanctified before it begins to move through him. What I witnessed in this conversation was the raw and living intersection of covenant faithfulness and human weakness — and that intersection is precisely where the Gospel has always done its most honest work. For those of us walking in the early days of genuine surrender, it matters enormously to see that God does not despise the beginning of transformation. He enters it. He works within it. He multiplies through imperfect vessels who have simply turned their face toward Him and begun — truly begun — to build with Him rather than beside Him or in spite of Him. This call was not just two entrepreneurs celebrating a win. It was a testimony of what happens when prayer and intercession are actually allowed to bear fruit in the material world, and when two brothers are honest enough with each other to name both the grace and the mess simultaneously. ## The Worldly Mind’s Interpretation From a purely natural vantage point, what happened here is straightforward: a talented salesman with strong communication instincts posted a video on a new TikTok account, converted a low-view post into a \$5,000 consulting engagement through sheer persuasive ability, and is now planning to scale that result through paid advertising and a team of closers. The natural mind applauds hustle, systems, and the kind of entrepreneurial grit that turns 482 views into five figures. It sees the financial strategy — the crypto liquidity conversation, the ICP targeting, the installment structure — as the real substance of the call. It would credit my brother’s charisma and our shared strategic framework as the cause of the breakthrough, and it would be satisfied with that explanation. The natural mind would file this under *“young men building something together,”* note the excitement, and move on. **The Limitation:** But that reading leaves the most important moment completely unaddressed. It cannot account for the prayer closet. It has no category for the *secret place* my brother mentioned — the return again and again to intercession, to seeking the face of God over the business, over the people it would touch. The natural mind does not know what to do with a man saying, *“I’ve been doing it wrong my whole life. Now I’m doing it with Jesus and it’s working.”* It cannot hear the weight of repentance in that sentence, or the theological enormity of what it means for a man who came from criminal living to now be standing in a place of covenant alignment — not perfectly, but genuinely. What the flesh cannot perceive is that this conversation was not primarily about sales conversion rates. It was about the conversion of a man, still in progress, still rough at the edges, already bearing fruit in the Kingdom. ## What the Spirit Reveals Through It As I sit with this conversation and let the Spirit press through it, the first thing I notice is the image of the secret place. I mentioned it directly — returning to that place of intercession three times, and then a fourth time when I was not even asking, and something was confirmed to me without my solicitation. This language is not accidental. It echoes Psalm 91:1, the one who *abides in the secret place of the Most High* and *dwells under the shadow of the Almighty.* The Hebrew word there for secret place is סֵתֶר (seter, SEH-ter) — a concealed, sheltered space of divine covering. It is not merely a metaphor for private prayer. It is a covenantal positioning — the place where God speaks what He will not say in the open, because the open is not where the listening posture has been cultivated. What I described on that call — going back again and again, receiving a little more of the Kingdom economy each time, and then receiving an unsolicited confirmation — that is the fruit of סֵתֶר (SEH-ter). That is what happens when a man stops demanding that God speak on his timetable and simply returns, repeatedly, with an open hand. The Kingdom economy language is something the Spirit has been pressing into both of us for some time, and I heard it surface organically in this call. My brother said it plainly: *“Kingdom economies are coming clutch.”* That phrase carries more theological weight than it may appear to at first. Throughout the Old Testament, God’s economic provision was always tethered to covenant faithfulness — the land produced when Israel walked with Yahweh, and withheld when she turned away. In the New Covenant, this principle does not dissolve; it deepens. The Apostle Paul wrote to the Philippians that God would supply all their need *“according to His riches in glory by Christ Jesus”* (Philippians 4:19, NKJV) — but the context of that promise was a community that had partnered with Paul in the *work of the Gospel*, not merely a congregation that hoped to prosper. Kingdom economy is not name-it-claim-it theology. It is the principle that when a man or a company of men genuinely aligns their labor with the purposes of God, the provision that flows is not coincidental. It is covenantal. I am seeing something important about the nature of the breakthrough my brother described. He closed a \$5,000 deal from 482 TikTok views on a brand new, untrusted account. From every natural metric, that should not have worked. The ratio is absurd. And yet I find myself thinking about Gideon’s three hundred, about Jonathan and his armor-bearer taking a garrison alone, about the five loaves and two fish that somehow fed five thousand. Scripture has a pattern: God consistently engineers results that the natural mind cannot claim credit for, specifically so that the testimony that emerges cannot be attributed to human cleverness alone. I do not think my brother’s gift for communication is irrelevant — God made him with that tongue, and he is right that it will be used. But the *timing*, the *ratio*, the *confirmation* — those carry the signature of a God who is working to make certain that when this testimony is told, it is told with the word *“Jesus”* in it. And it was. My brother put it there himself, unbidden. The moment in this conversation that I keep returning to is the repentance thread. My brother said something I want to sit with carefully: *“I’ve not been playing it wrong, but I’ve not been playing it clean.”* And then: *“I fucked up so much and still fucking up, but I’m correcting myself now and I’m stable now. I’m safe now.”* I did not dismiss any of that. I said back to him: *“The fact that you can admit that you were wrong is the beginning of repentance.”* The Greek word for repentance is μετάνοια (metanoia, meh-TAH-noy-ah) — literally a change of mind, a turning of the νοῦς (nous, NOOS), the inner faculty of perception and moral reasoning. Repentance is not first the cessation of behavior. It is the reorientation of perception. And what I heard in my brother’s words was exactly that — a man whose perception has shifted, who can now see the old patterns from the outside, who is watching himself and naming what he sees. That is μετάνοια (meh-TAH-noy-ah) in its earliest, most honest form. It is not the finished product. It is the beginning. And the beginning is sacred. There is a pastoral weight in what I said next that I want to unpack more fully: *“It’s not your job to change. You leave it up to Jesus, you leave it up to the Father. The Father changes the heart from within.”* This is not passivity theology. I was not telling my brother to do nothing. I was pointing him toward the correct locus of transformation. The prophet Ezekiel carried this word from God centuries before the Cross: *“I will give you a new heart and put a new spirit within you; I will take the heart of stone out of your flesh and give you a heart of flesh. I will put My Spirit within you and cause you to walk in My statutes”* (Ezekiel 36:26-27, NKJV). Notice the grammar: God gives, God puts, God takes, God causes. The walking is ours. But the internal renovation is His. What my brother is experiencing — the growing capacity to feel conviction, to see his old patterns, to name what needs to change — that is the Spirit of God already fulfilling this Ezekiel promise in him. He did not manufacture that sensitivity. It was given. I also notice the image of the mirror that came up briefly in this call. My brother mentioned having a mirror, and in that same moment I described listening back to my own recorded meditations in my own AI-cloned voice and catching the pride, catching the ego. There is something Scripturally resonant in both of these images. James wrote that a man who hears the Word and does not do it is *“like a man observing his natural face in a mirror”* — he sees, and immediately forgets (James 1:23-24, NKJV). But the man who looks *“into the perfect law of liberty and continues in it”* is the one who is *“blessed in what he does”* (James 1:25, NKJV). The Spirit is using both of us — in different ways — to look at ourselves through the lens of something more honest than our own self-perception. For me it is the recorded word played back to my own ear. For my brother it is the growing capacity to see old relational patterns as they happen. Both are forms of the Spirit holding up the mirror of the Word and refusing to let us look away. The conversation about crypto and financial infrastructure is worth a brief word, not because it is the spiritual center of the call, but because it surfaces something real: my brother and I are genuinely trying to build something that operates across national borders in the service of Kingdom purpose. The practical wisdom of using stablecoins like USDT or USDC for international liquidity — pegged to the dollar, near-instant on certain blockchain networks — is sound. But what I hear underneath that conversation is something deeper: two men trying to build financial infrastructure that is free from the slowness and friction of systems that were not designed with them in mind. There is a long biblical precedent for God’s people needing to navigate economic systems creatively and faithfully — from Joseph in Egypt, to Daniel in Babylon, to the early church’s radical redistribution described in Acts 2 and 4. The goal is always the same: keep the mission moving, keep the brethren cared for, keep the hands free to do the work. That is the spirit in which these conversations need to happen. What moved me most about this call — and what I want to be honest about as I reflect on it — is that my brother said one of the most important things either of us has said in a long time, and he said it casually, in the middle of a business call, while I was almost out of gas and he was closing deals in the UK: *“I think so. And you know what the best thing is — I’m gonna put it all back into ads.”* He had just closed \$5,000. And his first instinct was to reinvest. Not to hoard. Not to disappear. To multiply. The parable of the talents lives in that instinct (Matthew 25:14-30, NKJV). The servants who were faithful were not the ones who protected what they had. They were the ones who pressed it back into motion. My brother is, perhaps without fully knowing it, operating by a Kingdom principle: you do not sit on the first fruit of breakthrough; you plant it. I also heard something in this call about the nature of God’s timing relative to human desperation. While my brother was sharing \$5,000 worth of good news, I was on speakerphone because I had forgotten my headphones, almost out of gas, waiting on a Stripe payment that still had not arrived, wondering if I could make it home. The contrast was not lost on me. And yet — and this is something I have seen before — God does not deliver all things at once, all at once, to every vessel equally. He sequences His provision in ways that keep us dependent, that keep the conversation going, that keep us reaching toward one another and toward Him. The Stripe delay, the gas tank approaching empty, the wife I needed to call for twenty dollars — these are not signs of abandonment. They are the texture of the life of faith, the very terrain through which the God of Elijah proves Himself again and again as the God who provides *in* the need, not always *before* it. Finally, I want to sit with what was said near the very end of the call. My brother said, *“I’m safe now.”* Two words. And I did not press him on them in the moment, but they have stayed with me. Safe. From what? From the version of himself that was operating outside of covenant. From the consequences of a life lived without divine alignment. From the accumulated wreckage of hustle without holiness. When a man who came from where my brother came from says *“I’m safe now”* — and means it not financially, not relationally, but spiritually — that is the language of a man who has found the shadow of the Almighty. That is סֵתֶר (SEH-ter) not just as a prayer practice, but as a posture of life. He is beginning to live under the covering. And the fruit on the vine — 482 views, \$5,000, the hunger for righteousness, the capacity to name his own sin — that fruit does not lie. A tree is known by what it produces (Matthew 7:20, NKJV). ## Implications and Challenges The first challenge this call presents to us — and I include myself — is the tendency to spiritualize our successes and naturalize our struggles. When the deal closed, my brother and I both immediately reached for Kingdom language. Good. But when the gas tank was empty and the Stripe payment was delayed, my first instinct was frustration, not intercession. The Spirit is pressing me to apply the same theological framework to both the abundance and the lack. Paul said he had *“learned, in whatever state I am, to be content”* (Philippians 4:11, NKJV) — and the verb *learned* is key. Contentment in scarcity is not natural. It is a discipline acquired through repeated encounter with the faithfulness of God in the tight places. I am still learning this. The second challenge is the temptation to let financial breakthrough become the primary metric of spiritual alignment. My brother said something true: *“I’ve never done it with Jesus. Now I’m doing it with Jesus and it’s working.”* And it is working. But if the framework in our minds becomes *“Jesus = business success,”* we have drifted toward a prosperity gospel that Scripture does not support. Jesus also led His disciples through seasons of apparent failure, public rejection, and material poverty. The evidence of covenant alignment is not always abundance. Sometimes it is the peace that surpasses understanding in the middle of an empty gas tank. We need both testimonies to be honest. The third implication is corporate and apostolic. My brother already has a team of closers. He is already thinking about how to scale, how to multiply, how to place others into roles of Kingdom usefulness. This is not just entrepreneurship. This is a form of discipleship — raising up men and women who are hungry, giving them a framework, a mission, and a place to work. The early church did not separate spiritual community from economic community. They ate together, worked together, prayed together, and shared together. What my brother is building — imperfectly, in progress — carries the seed of something that could be genuinely apostolic in its economic and communal shape. That is worth stewarding carefully. Fourth, the repentance conversation we had near the end of the call is a model I want to hold up as a challenge to how we often do Christian community. We talked openly about failures — not in a confessional performance, but in honest, mid-conversation acknowledgment. My brother named specific patterns. I named mine. Neither of us tried to fix each other in that moment. We pointed each other toward the Father as the One who changes the heart. That kind of conversation — raw, mutual, Christ-centered rather than behavior-management-centered — is exactly what the Body of Christ desperately needs more of and has largely lost in institutional settings where image management has replaced genuine accountability. Finally, I want to name a caution about the crypto discussion specifically. The tools are legitimate. The strategy is sound. But when my brother said the word *“untraceable,”* I want to note — as I did, however briefly — that all our transactions must remain clean. The Kingdom cannot be built on the infrastructure of concealment. Stablecoins, crypto wallets, and international liquidity tools are not inherently wrong. But the posture toward them must remain one of stewardship and transparency, not evasion. We are building something for God. What is built for God must be able to withstand the light. ## What This Means For Us **For Spiritual Maturity:** This call is a reminder that God does not wait for the finished version of a man before He begins to move through him. What it calls us to is the honest practice of returning — to the secret place, to prayer, to intercession, to the Word — repeatedly, even when we come back imperfect and still carrying the old edges. Maturity is not the absence of the old self. It is the growing capacity to see the old self clearly and to yield the renovation process to the Father rather than trying to manage it ourselves. **For Kingdom Obedience:** The obedience this calls forth is practical and spiritual simultaneously: build the thing, pray over the thing, reinvest the first fruit, cover your brother, and refuse to let financial pressure become a theological crisis. Keep interceding for the business, for the people it touches, for the brethren who are hungry and building alongside you. Do not wait until everything is stable to begin walking in generosity. Begin now, with what is in your hand. **For Prophetic Posture:** The prophetic posture this call demands is one of watching for the Kingdom fingerprints on natural events. 482 views should not have produced a \$5,000 client. A man who came from criminal living should not be sitting in a posture of repentance and covenant alignment this quickly. These things are signs. The saints who watch will see them. The saints who are too busy being impressed by the numbers will miss what the numbers point to: a God who still moves on behalf of men who turn their face toward Him. ## A Closing Prayer Father, I come before You with gratitude that is almost too large for words — gratitude that You do not wait for perfect men before You begin to build Your Kingdom through imperfect ones. Thank You for my brother Samuel. Thank You for the 482 views that became a testimony. Thank You for the empty gas tank that kept me humble on the same day the breakthrough came. You are the God of both the abundance and the lack, and I am learning — slowly, honestly — to receive both as gifts from Your hand. I stand before You as a co-heir of the Kingdom, as one who has been sent to build and to pray and to intercede, and I make agreement with what You have been speaking over this business, over this brotherhood, over the families that are growing and the people that will be touched. Let the revenue engine become a testimony engine. Let every dollar that flows through our hands be traceable back to Your faithfulness. Let the secret place remain the foundation — the altar beneath the enterprise, the prayer beneath the pitch, the surrender beneath the sale. Transform us from within, Father. Change what we cannot change in ourselves. And let the fruit we are beginning to see be only the beginning of what You have planned. To You be the glory, in Jesus’ name.
