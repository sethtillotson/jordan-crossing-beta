# 06-22 Call Reflection: Commission, Providence, and Kingdom Partnership **Title:** 06-22 at 12:00 — Commissioned in Obscurity: Providence, Partnership, and the Pressing of the Spirit **Recorded:** 2026-06-22 12:00:55 **Source Discernment:** C — Recorded Conversation or Event (Live Capture) **Classification:** Call Reflection ## Opening Observation As I listened to this conversation with my brother Samuel unfold in real time — business terms, financial logistics, flickers of light, and a stranger on the street mentioning church — I found myself struck not by the transactional surface of what was being discussed, but by the undercurrent running beneath all of it. Something was being commissioned here that neither of us could fully articulate in the moment. The mundane and the sacred were refusing to stay separated, and the Spirit kept pressing that through the cracks of ordinary conversation. **Why This Matters:** The Kingdom of God does not confine itself to prayer meetings and Scripture studies. It presses into business negotiations, financial decisions, moments of obscurity, and half-connected phone calls with signal dropping in and out. If we are to walk as sons and daughters who have genuinely been sent — commissioned, not merely motivated — then we must learn to discern the hand of God moving through the fabric of ordinary life. The inability to see that hand is not a small thing; it is the difference between striving in the flesh and laboring with God. This conversation is a window into what it looks like when two brothers are beginning to understand that their work together is not merely entrepreneurial — it is ordained. ## The Worldly Mind’s Interpretation From the perspective of the natural mind, this is simply two business partners catching up on a call: a new client has been signed, equity has been locked in, payment infrastructure needs to be organized, and both men are navigating real financial pressure in the short term while positioning for long-term gain. The conversation about AI tools reads as ambition and innovation. The mention of staying put while others moved reads as a biographical detail. The repeated references to God and provision read as cultural religiosity — the kind of spiritual language that entrepreneurs sometimes layer over business decisions to make themselves feel better about risk. Even the flickering lighter and the comment about “moving into the light” would be dismissed as coincidence, metaphor, or confirmation bias by someone reasoning from flesh alone. **The Limitation:** The natural mind is not equipped to perceive the thread of divine intentionality woven through what appears to be logistical conversation. It can track cause and effect — effort produces result, planning reduces risk, timing determines outcome — but it cannot perceive commission. It cannot hear the difference between ambition and calling, between entrepreneurial momentum and the pressing of the Holy Spirit. The mind of the flesh evaluates what is visible: contracts signed, percentages owned, revenue milestones. But the mind of the Spirit perceives what is happening in the unseen realm: a season turning, a man emerging from obscurity, a partnership being consecrated for purposes larger than either partner yet fully understands. To stay only at the surface of this conversation is to miss everything actually happening in it. ## What the Spirit Reveals Through It As I sit with this conversation and let the Spirit press through it, the first thing that arrests me is my own statement near the middle of the call: *“I went on a whole spiritual journey following Jesus. And now we’re here back to this. But it’s commissioned this time.”* I said that almost in passing, almost as punctuation. But it is the theological center of everything else being discussed. There is a categorical difference between doing something because you want to and doing something because you have been sent. The Greek word ἀποστέλλω (apostellō, ah-pos-TEL-oh) — to send forth with authority — is the root of apostle, and while I am not claiming apostolic office over a business venture, I am claiming something Scripture consistently affirms: that the Lord commissions labor, that He directs the steps of a man, and that the return from obscurity is not regression but deployment. I think of Joseph. The pit, the prison, the silence — none of it was accident, and none of it was waste. When Joseph finally stood before Pharaoh, he was not trying to escape obscurity. He had been formed in it. Proverbs 16:9 says, *“A man’s heart plans his way, but the Lord directs his steps.”* My brother Samuel moved countries twice during the same season I stayed put. He was being repositioned. I was being hidden. Both are valid movements of divine orchestration. The mistake would have been to interpret hiddenness as abandonment or to interpret movement as superiority. Neither of us was ahead of the other — we were being shaped on different anvils for the same commission. What strikes me further is the recurring image of light in this conversation. My brother’s lighter broke. He mentioned moving into the light. Earlier in our conversations he had spoken of nanometers of wavelengths, and the alley light had been flickering. I want to be careful here — I will not manufacture prophetic symbolism where only coincidence may exist. But I also will not dismiss what the Spirit may be using as a recurring sign. Scripture consistently presents light as the domain of revelation, righteousness, and the unveiled presence of God. יְהוָה (YHWH) is called a lamp to the feet in Psalm 119:105. Paul writes in 2 Corinthians 4:6 that God who commanded light to shine out of darkness has shone in our hearts to give the light of the knowledge of the glory of God in the face of Jesus Christ. If my brother is sensing that he is moving into a season of light — of exposure, of clarity, of emerging — then the broken lighters may simply be an earthly echo of a heavenly transition: the old fire-making apparatus is insufficient for where we are going. The business itself carries spiritual weight I do not want to overlook. Twenty percent equity in JD Digital Whales, locked in by covenant agreement, with a fee structure that grows as the company grows — this is a picture of what it means to be joined to something and to share in its increase. It reminds me of the principle embedded in the Parable of the Talents in Matthew 25. The servant who was given something was expected to multiply it, and the multiplication was both for the master’s glory and the servant’s inheritance. We are not merely service providers. We are co-laborers in something we did not originate, and our faithfulness to steward it well is the thing that unlocks further increase. I said on the call that the AI tools I have been building “just poured out of me” and that I did not know what they would be used for, only that it was “a gift from God.” I want to linger here because this posture matters enormously. Many men build things to serve their own vision. What I am describing is something different — building without full comprehension of the destination, only with the conviction that the pressing is real. This is the posture of the craftsmen Bezalel and Oholiab in Exodus 31, whom God filled with the רוּחַ (ruach, ROO-akh) — Spirit, breath, wind — of God in wisdom, understanding, and knowledge in all manner of workmanship to build the Tabernacle. They did not invent the blueprint. They were filled and then they built what they were given to build. The Spirit-breathed craftsman does not always understand the full purpose of what he is constructing; he simply remains faithful to the design he has been given. The financial conversation near the middle of the call — the discussion about overdrafts, liabilities, first fruits, and reinvestment — is not merely practical wisdom. It is a spiritual diagnostic. I said, and I meant it from personal experience: *“The devil plays a game where he makes it not that important until it becomes an issue.”* This is a kingdom principle rooted in what Jesus said in Luke 16:10 — *“He who is faithful in what is least is also faithful in much.”* The enemy’s strategy is not always frontal assault. Often it is the patient erosion of small disciplines: the overlooked liability, the untracked subscription, the penny charge that reactivates and collapses the whole account. Proverbs 24:33–34 describes the field of the sluggard overgrown with thorns — not from one dramatic decision, but from a little sleep, a little slumber, a little folding of the hands. The faithful steward does not wait for large stakes before practicing faithfulness. I also want to sit with what happened when the stranger passed by on the street — the man my brother introduced as someone with mild disability, heading to the café, exchanging a few words, being told about church on Sunday. That moment, tucked into a business call, is a quiet image of something I believe deeply: the Kingdom does not compartmentalize. My brother did not pause the conversation and say *“let me switch to ministry mode now.”* He was already in it. He saw a man, he named him to me, he told him I was a fellow Christian, and he reminded him of Sunday. That is the lifestyle of the son of God in the earth — not performing evangelism as a separate department of life, but carrying the Kingdom into every moment, every street corner, every smoke break. The final exchange about gas and provision is, to me, the most quietly powerful moment in the conversation. I told my brother I was out of gas and asked if he could send something. His reply was that he was waiting on payment. And then I said: *“The provision will arrive just in time. As it always does.”* This is not optimism. This is not positive confession as a psychological technique. This is testimony — the accumulated witness of a man who has walked through lean seasons and found God faithful at the margins. Philippians 4:19 says, *“And my God shall supply all your need according to His riches in glory by Christ Jesus.”* The operative word in that verse is not “supply” — it is “according to.” Not according to my urgency. Not according to my timeline. According to His riches. The supply is always proportionate to the glory of the One supplying it, and the timing is always calibrated to produce the maximum faith rather than the maximum comfort. The thunder and lightning at the end of the call — my brother suddenly remarking on a storm breaking out in the heat — brings the whole conversation to an almost liturgical close. In Scripture, thunder and lightning consistently accompany the presence and the pronouncements of God. At Sinai in Exodus 19, thunder and lightning and thick cloud preceded the giving of the law. In Revelation 4:5, thunderings and lightnings proceed from the throne. There is something about the atmosphere my brother described — hot, then suddenly broken by storm — that mirrors the very arc of this season: a long, pressurized waiting, and then the sudden breaking of something new. I do not prophesy from this. I simply observe it and give thanks. What I am left with, as I sit with this whole conversation, is a conviction that what Samuel and I are building is being watched over. Not because we are remarkable men, but because we have submitted the work to the One who is remarkable. The commission is real. The obscurity was formative, not punitive. The tools being built are gifts, not achievements. The provision is coming just in time — not a minute before it is needed, and not one penny short of what is required. That is the signature of a God who is intimately acquainted with the details of the lives of His servants. ## Implications and Challenges The first challenge this meditation presses is the tendency to separate spiritual life from vocational life as though they occupy different realms. Many Western Christians have been shaped — consciously or not — by a sacred-secular divide that reserves God-language for church contexts and defaults to purely human strategy in the marketplace. But the Scriptures know no such divide. The hand of God in Joseph’s career, in Nehemiah’s reconstruction project, in Paul’s tentmaking — these are not incidental. God governs the marketplace as fully as He governs the sanctuary, and disciples who have not learned this will consistently underinterpret what He is doing in their working hours. Second, this meditation challenges the prosperity-gospel distortion that would take my statement about timely provision and weaponize it into a formula for guaranteed financial comfort. That is not what I am saying. I am saying that faithfulness in the lean season — tracking liabilities, staying disciplined in the small things, not despising the day of small beginnings — positions the servant to receive the provision when it arrives. Zechariah 4:10 asks who has despised the day of small things. The answer in context is no one with spiritual discernment, because small faithfulness is the seed of large inheritance. Third, and perhaps most pressing for those in entrepreneurial or ministry-adjacent work: the gift that “pours out” must be stewarded with both humility and seriousness. I said I did not know what the AI tool would be used for. That kind of holy uncertainty is actually the correct posture — it prevents the craftsman from hijacking the tool for his own purposes before God has revealed its full design. But that same posture must be matched with diligence, record-keeping, transparency between partners, and the kind of accountability that prevents gifts from being squandered through carelessness. Grace does not eliminate discipline; it undergirds it. Fourth, this conversation carries a subtle but important word about how we handle one another’s financial vulnerabilities within Kingdom partnership. I did not shame my brother for his overdraft situation. I named it, I connected it to a spiritual principle, I shared from my own hard-learned experience, and I moved forward. That is the model Paul describes in Galatians 6:1 — restore such a one in a spirit of gentleness. Financial accountability within Kingdom-oriented partnerships must be held with both clarity and grace, because money is one of the primary arenas in which the enemy establishes accusation, shame, and division among brothers. Fifth, the brief encounter on the street with the man heading to the café deserves serious reflection for all of us who compartmentalize “ministry” into scheduled events. The Kingdom is not a department. It is a nature. When my brother named that man to me, introduced me as a fellow Christian, and reminded him of Sunday, he was not doing a task — he was being a son of God on a street in the middle of a business call. That is the kind of integrated Kingdom life that Jesus modeled in every Gospel narrative, and it is what the Spirit is pressing us all toward. Finally, the season of obscurity — staying put while everyone pushed me to move — deserves to be named as a gift rather than a grievance. God has a long history of hiding His servants before He deploys them. Moses in Midian. David in the cave of Adullam. Paul in Arabia. The hiddenness is not punishment; it is preparation. And the commissioned emergence from that season is not triumphalism — it is simply the faithful servant stepping into the next thing the Master has prepared. ## What This Means For Us **For Spiritual Maturity:** We are being called to develop eyes that see God’s hand in the vocational and financial fabric of daily life — not to spiritualize everything artificially, but to remain genuinely awake to the fact that He governs all of it. Maturity here means refusing the sacred-secular split and learning to steward both gifts and liabilities with equal faithfulness before the Lord. **For Kingdom Obedience:** Obedience in this season looks like doing what I described: tracking the small things, building what pours out of me without hijacking it for my own vision, maintaining transparency and accountability with covenant partners, and trusting that the provision is calibrated to the glory of the One providing it — not to my comfort level. **For Prophetic Posture:** The prophetic posture this meditation calls for is one of watching without manufacturing — noticing the recurring images, the atmospheric shifts, the confirming signs that God places along the path, without inflating them into false predictions or collapsing them into mere coincidence. Remain awake. Remain humble. Let the thread emerge from the Lord’s hand, not from our own interpretive eagerness. ## A Closing Prayer Father, I come before You as one who has been pressed back into the field after a season hidden in Your hand — and I come with gratitude, not reluctance. You commissioned this work before I understood it. You hid the tools inside me before I knew what they were for. You kept me in obscurity when everyone around me wanted movement, and in that staying, You were forming something that striving never could have produced. I ask now that You guard the partnership You have established, that You keep our accounts transparent before one another and before You, that You anoint the tools we are building with purpose beyond our comprehension, and that You bring the provision in Your time — just in time, as You always have. Let this work be Yours: the equity, the contracts, the advisory agreements, the AI gifts, the long-term plays and the short-term needs. Let nothing we build serve our own name above Your glory. And let us never forget the man on the street corner, the brother with the flickering lighter, the small moment of naming one another as fellow Christians in the middle of a business call — because that is the Kingdom too, and You love it no less than the great commissions. Thunder and lightning, or hot stillness, or signal dropping in and out — let us hear You through it all. We are Your servants, and we are listening. In the name of Jesus Christ, who is the same yesterday, today, and forever — Amen. ## Scripture References & Original Language Notes | **Passage (NKJV)** | **Original Language** | **Theological Insight** | |-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------| | Proverbs 16:9 — “A man’s heart plans his way, but the Lord directs his steps.” | דֶּרֶךְ (derek, DEH-rech) — way / path / journey | The Lord’s direction does not override human planning; it governs it.
