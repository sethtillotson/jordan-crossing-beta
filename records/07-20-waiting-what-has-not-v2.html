# 07-20 Personal Meditation: Holy Waiting, Gender Reveal, and Divine Appointed Times **Title:** 07-20 at 12:16 — Waiting on What Has Not Yet Been Revealed **Recorded:** 2026-07-20 12:16:48 **Source Discernment:** C — Recorded Conversation or Event (Live Capture) **Classification:** Personal Meditation ## Opening Observation As I listened to this brief exchange with my wife, something quiet and sacred pressed itself into the ordinary texture of the moment — hunger, rest, waiting, and the deliberate choice to hold a secret a little longer. There is a child forming in the hidden place, and we have chosen not to rush the knowing. I find myself arrested by that choice, and by what the Spirit is saying through it. **Why This Matters:** The posture of chosen waiting — of saying *not yet* to a revelation that is already available — is not merely a cultural preference or a celebratory custom. It is a parable of something deeper. In the Kingdom of God, there are truths that are already established in the heavens, already sealed and certain, yet withheld from full disclosure until the appointed time. The saints are perpetually being trained to trust what is already *known by God* without requiring that it be *immediately known by us*. How we handle the small waitings of daily life reveals whether our faith is genuinely rooted in the faithfulness of God, or whether it is merely a calm we maintain only when all information is already in hand. ## The Worldly Mind’s Interpretation The natural mind, hearing this exchange, would process it cleanly and practically: a routine prenatal checkup, a two-month timeline for a follow-up ultrasound, a couple choosing a gender reveal event as a social milestone. The world would celebrate this as an organizational decision — thoughtful, festive, perhaps emotionally intelligent in its desire to share the moment communally rather than receive it privately. Nothing more would be seen here than the ordinary choreography of expectant parenthood, the quiet logistics of new life entering an already busy household. The mind of the flesh would be satisfied with that interpretation and move on, seeing nothing beyond the surface of a tired wife, a hungry stomach, and a doctor’s appointment. **The Limitation:** But there is a dimension to this moment that the natural eye simply cannot perceive without the Spirit’s illumination. The flesh cannot see that *waiting on the unrevealed* is itself a spiritual discipline — that the choice to delay knowing, even when knowing is within reach, is a form of consecration. The carnal mind has no framework for understanding why delay could be gift, or why mystery could be mercy. It has been trained by an age of instant access and total disclosure, and so it instinctively reaches for answers, diagnoses, and certainties the moment they become available. The deeper wisdom — that some things are meant to be received in season, in community, with wonder intact — requires a posture that the unrenewed mind finds genuinely foreign. ## What the Spirit Reveals Through It I am sitting with the image of my wife resting and hungry, and of a child whose identity is already fully known in heaven but not yet disclosed to us on earth. There is something in that layering that feels deeply canonical. The Psalmist writes, *“For You formed my inward parts; You covered me in my mother’s womb”* (Psalm 139:13). The Hebrew word translated *“formed”* is קָנָה (qanah, kaw-NAW) — to possess, to create, to acquire with intention. This child is not an accident of biology. This child is a *possession of God*, crafted with deliberate artistry in the hidden place, covered and known long before any ultrasound could trace a silhouette. What strikes me in this is that God already knows. He has always known. The gender, the name, the calling, the destiny — none of it is uncertain to Him. And yet He has also woven into this moment our choosing to wait, to withhold even from ourselves what is already knowable, so that the revealing might come with fullness. I find in this a shadow of the way the Kingdom operates. The Apostle Paul wrote that God has *“chosen the foolish things of the world to put to shame the wise”* (1 Corinthians 1:27), and I wonder if one of those foolish things is the willingness to live peacefully in the not-yet-knowing, trusting that the One who knows all things will reveal in His time. There is a word in the Greek New Testament that I feel pressing here: ἀποκάλυψις (apokalypsis, ah-poh-KAH-loop-sis) — an unveiling, an uncovering, a disclosure of what was previously hidden. Every instance of this word in Scripture implies something that was already real, already complete, already true — and is now being brought forward into visibility. The gender reveal we are planning is, in miniature, a picture of this. The child’s identity is *already established*. The reveal does not create it; it simply uncovers what has always been. And that is exactly what God does with prophetic disclosure, with the revealing of the sons of God, with the unfolding of mystery in its appointed season. Romans 8:19 says that *“the earnest expectation of the creation eagerly waits for the revealing of the sons of God.”* The same root — ἀποκάλυψις (ah-poh-KAH-loop-sis) — sits beneath that word *revealing*. All of creation is in a posture not unlike where my wife and I are right now: holding its breath, waiting on what is already certain in the heavens to be made manifest on the earth. The child is coming. The sons of God are coming into their full revelation. The mystery is not whether it will happen, but *when* and *how* the unveiling will take place. I am also moved by the brevity of this exchange and by what was nestled inside it. She said she was hungry. She said she needed to sleep. These are the embodied realities of a woman carrying new life — her body making extraordinary demands in the most ordinary language. And I think of Mary, who *“kept all these things and pondered them in her heart”* (Luke 2:19), who carried the Son of God in a body that also grew tired, also grew hungry, also moved through the unremarkable dailiness of human flesh even while bearing the most remarkable thing heaven had ever entrusted to earth. The sacred is so rarely announced with fanfare in its early stages. It comes quietly, incrementally, hidden in the womb of waiting. The two-month timeline the doctor gave us is significant to me spiritually as well. There is a rhythm to gestation — biological, yes, but also prophetic. Things that God is growing in us do not arrive on demand. The writer of Ecclesiastes declares that *“to everything there is a season, a time for every purpose under heaven”* (Ecclesiastes 3:1). The Hebrew word עֵת (et, AYT) — appointed time — appears repeatedly in that passage, and it carries the weight of divine scheduling. God does not rush His work. He does not abbreviate the gestation of what He is forming. He lets it develop fully, in the dark and the hidden, until the time appointed for its emergence. This chosen restraint — our decision to hold off on knowing the gender until we can share it with those we love — is itself a small act of covenantal community. We are refusing to receive privately what we believe should be received together. There is something profoundly New Covenant about that instinct. The early church did not hoard revelation; they broke bread together, they shared what they had received, they made space for the whole body to encounter the goodness of God in community. *“And they continued steadfastly in the apostles’ doctrine and fellowship, in the breaking of bread, and in prayers”* (Acts 2:42). The gender reveal, held rightly, is not mere sentimentality — it is a declaration that this life belongs not only to us but to the community into which this child will be born. I am also thinking about the phrase: *“we don’t want to know yet.”* What a holy thing it is to voluntarily limit oneself when limitation is not required. This is not ignorance — it is chosen mystery. And God Himself operates in this mode. He has not told us the day or the hour. He has not disclosed every detail of what is coming. He has given us enough — *“the secret things belong to the LORD our God, but those things which are revealed belong to us and to our children forever”* (Deuteronomy 29:29) — but He has also withheld what would overwhelm or what must await its proper season. To walk willingly in that space, trusting the Revealer rather than demanding the revelation, is one of the deepest forms of faith. There is a Christological thread here that I cannot leave unexamined. Jesus Himself said of His return that *“of that day and hour no one knows, not even the angels of heaven, but My Father only”* (Matthew 24:36). The Son, in His kenotic humanity — κένωσις (kenōsis, keh-NO-sis), the self-emptying described in Philippians 2:7 — lived in the posture of yielded not-knowing, trusting the Father’s timing for all things. If the Lord of all creation could inhabit the discipline of withheld information with perfect peace, then surely this is a posture worth learning. Not as passivity. Not as avoidance. But as active, trusting, covenantal surrender to the One who holds every timeline. My wife is resting. She is hungry. She is carrying life that has not yet been fully named to us. And in all of this — in the waiting, in the tiredness, in the ordinary medical appointments and the two-month countdowns — God is at work. The Spirit is present in the hidden place, superintending what no eye has yet seen and what no announcement has yet made public. I am learning, in this small and sacred domestic moment, what it means to be a people who trust the Revealer more than they grasp for the revelation. ## Implications and Challenges The modern Western church has largely lost the capacity for holy waiting. We have built entire spiritual cultures around the demand for immediate clarity — immediate healing, immediate direction, immediate confirmation. When God does not disclose on our timeline, we interpret His silence as absence rather than as the patient gestation of something too good to be rushed. This moment gently rebukes that posture and invites us back into the ancient rhythm of trustful expectation. There is also a challenge here to the way we treat communal revelation. In an age of instant social media announcement, the impulse is to publish the moment it is known, to share individually and widely before the community has even gathered. My wife and I are choosing instead to wait until we can be present together with those we love. That is countercultural. It is worth asking: in what other areas are we receiving privately what was meant to be shared communally, or rushing into disclosure what should be held until the fullness of time? The idea that *mystery can be mercy* is foreign to a generation that treats uncertainty as a problem to be solved rather than a gift to be received. But Scripture is full of seasons of hiddenness — Joseph in the pit, David in the cave, Jesus in the tomb — where the most critical work of God was happening in the place of least visibility. To be comfortable in the not-yet is not spiritual weakness; it is a cultivated trust that the God who hides is the same God who reveals, and that His timing is never arbitrary. There is a tenderness in this exchange that I want to name carefully: the exhaustion and hunger of a woman carrying life are not inconveniences to be spiritualized away. They are the real and costly embodiment of new creation work. The church sometimes spiritualizes suffering and sacrifice to the point of dishonoring the physical reality of those who bear them. Life coming into the world costs something. It costs the body. It costs sleep and appetite and comfort. That cost is holy, and it deserves to be honored plainly, not only metaphorically. Finally, there is a prophetic implication here for how we hold the promises of God that are already established in heaven but not yet manifest on earth. We know in whom we have believed. We know the child is coming. The gender reveal does not make the child real — the child is already real. In the same way, the promises of God are not made real by our confession of them; they are already real, already secured in Christ, already written in eternity. Our role is not to create the reality through striving but to receive what is already certain, in the appointed time, with the community of faith gathered around us to witness the unveiling. ## What This Means For Us **For Spiritual Maturity:** This moment trains us to distinguish between the *certainty of God’s promises* and our *present possession of their fullness*. Growing in maturity means learning to rest in the gap between what is already true in heaven and what has not yet fully appeared on earth — not with resignation, but with the quiet confidence of those who know the Revealer. **For Kingdom Obedience:** We are called to hold revelation communally, to resist the impulse to receive privately what God intended to be shared in the context of covenant relationship. Kingdom obedience here looks like patience, like shared anticipation, like building community around the things God is bringing forth rather than retreating into private spiritual experience. **For Prophetic Posture:** The prophetic saints are not those who demand full disclosure before they will trust or move. They are those who have learned to inhabit the appointed time — the עֵת (AYT) — with open hands, watching for the ἀποκάλυψις (ah-poh-KAH-loop-sis) when it comes, and trusting the God who covers and forms and reveals in His perfect season. ## A Closing Prayer Father, I come before You as one who stands between heaven and earth, in agreement with Your will and Your ways — as a servant of the Most High, a co-heir with Christ, and one who is learning to trust what You have already established before I could fully see it. Thank You for the life You are forming in secret. Thank You for a wife who carries what You have entrusted to us with faithfulness even through hunger and weariness. Thank You that You are the God who covers in the womb, who schedules the revealing, who makes the appointed time the most beautiful time. Lord, I ask that You sanctify our waiting — make it holy, make it trustful, make it a testimony to those around us that Your people are a people who do not grasp for what has not yet been disclosed but who receive with open hands what You bring forth in fullness of time. Let this child come into a family, a community, and a Kingdom that has learned to wait well. And let every hiddenness in our lives — every promise not yet manifest, every calling still in gestation — be held in Your faithful hands, knowing that what You have begun, You are faithful to complete. To You be all glory in the church and in Christ Jesus, to all generations, forever and ever.
