# 07-14 Call Reflection: Holy Spirit Discernment, Occult Entanglement, and the Command to Flee **Title:** 07-14 at 10:18 — When the Spirit Speaks Before the Sentence Ends **Recorded:** 2026-07-14 10:18:08 **Source Discernment:** C — Recorded Conversation or Event (Live Capture) **Classification:** Call Reflection ## Opening Observation There are moments when the Holy Spirit speaks so swiftly, so cleanly, that the warning arrives before the words do — before the full picture is painted, before the story is complete, before the natural mind has assembled enough information to form a conclusion. I am sitting with one of those moments now. A brief exchange between brothers, a name, a city, and then the word came up through my spirit like a reflex: *flee*. What arrests me here is not the drama of the situation — it is the precision of the Spirit’s movement in a moment most would dismiss as ordinary conversation. **Why This Matters:** The capacity to discern before comprehending is not a psychological gift or a cultivated intuition — it is the fruit of abiding. The saints of God must learn to recognize this faculty, because the enemy does not always announce himself with horns and fire. Sometimes he arrives wrapped in a relationship, in romantic connection, in occult practice dressed as personal spirituality. When the Body of Christ loses its sensitivity to these early warnings — when discernment is replaced by sentiment, by social grace, by the fear of seeming judgmental — brothers and sisters walk into defilement that takes years to unwind. What happened in this brief call between two men of God is worth examining carefully: prayer preceded exposure, and the Spirit confirmed what prayer had already prepared. ## The Worldly Mind’s Interpretation From a purely natural vantage point, this exchange looks like two friends catching up — brief, casual, unremarkable. One man mentions a woman he knows, mentions that something unsettling came to light involving tarot cards, and the other man relates it to his own past relational experience. The natural mind might frame this as pattern recognition: *we have both been burned before, so we have learned to be cautious around certain warning signs.* Psychology would call it schema development — learned associations formed through relational pain. The broader culture might even soften it further, suggesting that labeling something like tarot as dangerous is superstitious, judgmental, or rooted in religious fear rather than genuine discernment. The natural mind is generous toward complexity and suspicious of anything that sounds like a firm boundary or a swift verdict. **The Limitation:** But the natural mind, however compassionate its framing, cannot account for what actually happened in that moment. It has no category for a man hearing half a sentence and receiving a complete spiritual verdict before the other man finished speaking. Natural reasoning builds conclusions from accumulated data — it needs the full story, the evidence, the context. The Spirit does not operate on that timeline. He is not gathering evidence; He already knows the tree by its fruit, the root by what it produces, and the spirit behind the practice by the nature of what that practice serves. What the natural mind calls intuition or experience, the Spirit has already named as discernment — a gift given to those who have trained their senses through the Word and through sustained communion with the living God. ## What the Spirit Reveals Through It I am struck first by the sequence my brother described: he prayed, and then the thing revealed itself. This is not coincidence. This is the posture of a watchman who has positioned himself before the Lord and asked for clarity, and the Lord — who is faithful — answered by allowing what was hidden to surface. Throughout Scripture, this is a recurring pattern: the one who watches and prays is granted sight that the passive observer is denied. In the book of Daniel, it is the man given to fasting and intercession who receives understanding of visions that leave the learned men of Babylon utterly silent. Prayer is not merely petition — it is a posture that opens the eyes of the inner man to see what is already present but not yet visible. The detail about tarot cards is not incidental, and I do not want to pass over it lightly. The Word of God is unambiguous about the practice of divination. In Deuteronomy 18, the Lord speaks to Israel entering the land and gives an explicit list of abominable practices that characterized the nations being displaced — including one who practices divination, one who interprets omens, or a soothsayer. These were not condemned arbitrarily. They represent a fundamental repositioning of the soul’s orientation — away from the God who speaks, and toward spirits that counterfeit His voice. Tarot is not a neutral hobby. It is a spiritual inlet, an open door to a realm that the Lord did not authorize and does not inhabit. When my brother shared this detail, the Spirit in me recognized the nature of the thing immediately — not from cultural programming, but from the witness of Scripture written on the inner man. And this is where the word *flee* becomes important. I did not reach for that word consciously. It rose. And what I find remarkable in sitting with this now is that *flee* is precisely the apostolic language for this category of sin. Paul writes to the church at Corinth — a congregation swimming in a city saturated with sexual immorality and pagan spiritual practice — and he uses this exact command. In 1 Corinthians 6:18, he writes, “Flee sexual immorality.” And in 1 Corinthians 10:14, just chapters later, he writes, “Flee from idolatry.” The Greek word in both places is φεύγετε (pheúgete, FYOO-ge-teh) — a present imperative plural, meaning: *keep running, do not stop, make it your ongoing posture to be in motion away from this thing.* It is not a suggestion. It is not an invitation to evaluate. It is a command that presumes danger serious enough to require immediate physical and spiritual departure. What strikes me about the pairing of these two commands in the same epistle is that Paul did not treat sexual immorality and idolatry as separate categories requiring different pastoral advice. He recognized their kinship. Occult practice and sexual immorality are frequently entangled — both involve a surrender of the self to something other than the Lord, both require a kind of spiritual intimacy, and both are capable of forming soul-ties that are remarkably difficult to sever without deliberate, covenantal repentance. This is not speculation; it is traceable across the canon. In Numbers 25, Israel’s idolatry at Baal-Peor was inseparable from their sexual immorality with the daughters of Moab. The two sins arrived together, and the judgment for both was swift and severe. The enemy understands the linkage even when the church has grown comfortable enough to treat them as unrelated lifestyle choices. I also want to sit with the fact that this discernment came through relationship — through the Body of Christ in its most elemental form: two brothers on a brief phone call, checking in with one another, sharing what the Spirit has been doing. There was no formal setting, no pulpit, no structured discipleship meeting. Just two men who know the Lord, walking in enough transparency to share what is happening and to receive what the Spirit speaks through it. This is the New Covenant community in its most organic expression, and it should not be underestimated. The writer of Hebrews exhorts the assembly to “exhort one another daily, while it is called ‘Today,’ lest any of you be hardened through the deceitfulness of sin” (Hebrews 3:13). The daily quality of that exhortation is significant. The hardening that sin produces is gradual, incremental, almost imperceptible from the inside — it requires the outside voice of a brother or sister who can see what we have grown too comfortable to notice. What my brother did in praying on the situation before drawing a conclusion is worth naming as a model of spiritual maturity. There is a version of discernment that is merely reactive — that operates from past wounds and personal suspicion rather than from prayer and the witness of the Spirit. The enemy can corrupt discernment by rooting it in trauma rather than in the Lord. But what my brother described is different: he brought the question to God, he waited, and then God allowed the answer to surface in the natural. This is what James means when he writes, “If any of you lacks wisdom, let him ask of God, who gives to all liberally and without reproach, and it will be given to him” (James 1:5). Wisdom sought in prayer becomes wisdom received in time — and what is received in time can be trusted in a way that mere instinct cannot. There is something I cannot pass over about the geographic and cultural detail my brother mentioned — a girl from Liverpool. It does not matter specifically where she is from, but it does matter that this is not a sequestered, monastically protected environment. These are men living in the ordinary world, encountering ordinary relationships, and navigating the spiritual terrain of those ordinary encounters with the equipment of the Kingdom. The gospel was never designed for the spiritually insulated. Paul wrote to Corinth, to Ephesus, to Rome — cities that would make most modern believers deeply uncomfortable. The call to discernment and holiness is not a call to retreat from the world but to walk through it with eyes that see what the world cannot see about itself. The relational note — that I had my own experience with an ex-girlfriend that paralleled this — carries weight for the corporate dimension of this meditation. There is a solidarity in fallen patterns. The enemy does not invent new strategies for every person he approaches; he reuses what works. Occult spiritual practice as an entry point into relational defilement is not a new tactic. What is new, in each generation, is the willingness of sons and daughters of God to name it clearly and refuse it with the authority that belongs to those who have been purchased at a price. Paul writes in 1 Corinthians 6:20, following his command to flee sexual immorality: “For you were bought at a price; therefore glorify God in your body and in your spirit, which are God’s.” The price paid at Calvary is the theological ground of every command to flee. We do not run merely because the thing is dangerous. We run because we belong to Another, and that belonging is the source of both our protection and our dignity. There is also a quieter word in this exchange that I do not want to lose in the larger theological unfolding. At the end of the call, my brother said simply: *God bless, brother.* And the call ended. Brief. Unsentimental. But entirely sufficient. I am aware that the Kingdom often moves through what the world considers too small to notice — a quick call before work, a word that rises before the sentence is finished, a simple blessing spoken at departure. The disciples did not carry elaborate systems for every encounter. They carried the Spirit, and the Spirit proved sufficient for every moment. I am reminded of the way Jesus sends out the seventy in Luke 10 — not with institutional infrastructure, but with authority, simplicity, and the peace that settles on every house that is worthy of it. The same Spirit who moved in the upper room is the Spirit who moves in a brief phone call between brothers who are truly walking with God. Finally, I want to address what makes this moment reproducible for any son or daughter of God who is paying attention. The conditions for this kind of real-time discernment are not mysterious or reserved for a spiritual elite. They are: sustained prayer, a willingness to bring uncertainty to the Lord rather than resolving it independently, a community of transparency where spiritual experience is shared without shame, and a familiarity with the Word deep enough that the Spirit can draw on its vocabulary in real-time. These are not extraordinary conditions. They are the ordinary conditions of a life genuinely submitted to the Kingdom. What my brother and I experienced in that brief exchange is available to every believer who chooses to cultivate these conditions rather than waiting for them to appear spontaneously. ## Implications and Challenges There is a persistent temptation in contemporary Christian culture to treat the category of the occult as either dramatically dangerous — the stuff of horror films and exorcism narratives — or as entirely harmless cultural expression. Both extremes are theologically incoherent. Tarot, astrology, divination in any form, and the spiritual atmospheres they generate are real in their effect, not because they carry independent power, but because they represent a real opening of the soul to spiritual influence that is not sourced in the living God. The church must recover a sober, scripturally grounded conversation about these things — not driven by fear, but by love for the souls being harmed and the authority of the Word that names them clearly. The instinct to wait before acting — to bring something to prayer before drawing a conclusion — is deeply countercultural in an environment that privileges speed of decision, instant emotional response, and the constant accessibility of outside opinion. Social media has made the instant verdict feel virtuous. But the sons and daughters of God are called to a different pace: the pace of the Lord, who is neither hurried nor delayed, and whose timing always arrives as both gift and confirmation. The discipline of praying before concluding is not spiritual passivity — it is active positioning before the One who sees the end from the beginning. The relational transparency that made this exchange possible — two brothers freely sharing spiritual experiences, including personal failures with past relationships — is not universal in the church. Many congregations have cultivated a culture of polished testimony, in which only victories are shared publicly and the ongoing struggles of the interior life remain hidden. This kind of culture does not produce the “exhort one another daily” quality of community that Hebrews envisions. It produces isolation dressed in the clothing of corporate worship. The saints must be willing to be known — not for the sake of vulnerability as a value, but for the sake of the mutual accountability and intercession that the Body of God requires to remain healthy. The speed and certainty of Spirit-led discernment can also be misused, and this must be named honestly. Not every swift inner reaction is the Holy Spirit. The flesh is capable of producing conviction that feels spiritual but is rooted in personal offense, past woundedness, or cultural bias. The test is not the speed of the impression but its alignment with Scripture, its consistency with the character of Christ, and its capacity to bear the fruit of love, sobriety, and Kingdom order. Discernment that leads to flight from sin is one thing. Discernment that leads to judgment of persons is another matter entirely, and must be handled with far greater caution and pastoral wisdom. For those who have been in relationships marked by occult entanglement — whether a past partner, a family member, or a close friendship — the call to flee is not merely geographical or relational. It extends to the interior life: to the agreements made in moments of spiritual intimacy, to the soul-ties formed through shared spiritual experience, and to the residual influence that can linger in thought patterns, emotional reflexes, and spiritual appetite. The grace of God is fully sufficient for the undoing of every tie, every agreement, and every open door — but it requires a deliberate, honest, covenantal repentance that names what happened and brings it specifically under the lordship of Jesus Christ. ## What This Means For Us **For Spiritual Maturity:** Maturity in the Spirit is measured in part by the distance between a stimulus and our response — not in the sense of emotional suppression, but in the sense of how naturally and immediately we refer to the Lord before concluding, reacting, or engaging. The goal is not to be slow but to be rooted — so that when the Spirit speaks swiftly, as He did in this moment, we recognize His voice because we have been in sustained conversation with Him long enough to know it. **For Kingdom Obedience:** The practical obedience this reflection calls for is the willingness to actually flee — not to linger at the border of what the Lord has named as dangerous, exploring whether the danger is really as serious as Scripture suggests, but to move with the same clarity and speed the Spirit offers. Obedience in the area of spiritual and sexual purity is rarely comfortable. It often costs a relationship, a social connection, or a version of belonging the flesh was hoping to preserve. The price is real. The peace on the other side is more real still. **For Prophetic Posture:** The prophetic community is formed through exactly these kinds of ordinary, prayerful, transparent exchanges between brothers and sisters who are genuinely attentive to the Spirit’s movement in their daily lives. We are not waiting for a platform to be prophetic. The prophetic posture is the posture of those who walk with God through the ordinary and find that the ordinary is perpetually charged with His presence, His instruction, and His warning. Stay close to the brothers and sisters who pray before they conclude. Stay close to those who name what they see with clarity and then say simply, *God bless, brother* — and mean every word of it. ## A Closing Prayer Father, I come before You as Your son, a co-heir with Christ, standing in the authority of the New Covenant sealed by the blood of Jesus — and I bring before You this moment, this exchange, this word that rose through my spirit before it was fully formed in my mind. I thank You that You are a God who speaks — who does not wait for us to assemble all the evidence before You offer discernment to those who walk with You in prayer and transparency. I thank You for the brother You placed in my life, for the faithfulness of prayer that preceded exposure, and for the word *flee* that belongs to a generation that must know how to run in a world that calls every boundary a failure of love. Lord, would You seal every open door in every life reading these words — every agreement made in ignorance, every soul-tie formed in relational entanglement with what does not serve Your Kingdom, every interior space that was surrendered to what You did not authorize. Let the blood of Christ speak over those places. Let the Spirit reclaim what was given away. And let the Body of Christ grow in the kind of sober, loving, scripturally grounded discernment that allows brothers to guard one another in a single phone call between work shifts, because Your Kingdom is not confined to sanctuaries — it moves through every voice that is willing to carry it. To You be all glory, all honor, and all worship, now and forevermore.
