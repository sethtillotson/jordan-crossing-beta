# 06-18 Call Reflection: Faith, Ego Death, and Kingdom-Oriented Business **Title:** 06-18 at 12:23 — When God Killed the Ego and Business Came Alive **Recorded:** 2026-06-18 12:23:14 **Source Discernment:** C — Recorded Conversation or Event (Live Capture) **Classification:** Call Reflection ## Opening Observation As I listened to this conversation unfold between my brother Samuel and me, something beneath the surface of the business talk began to press on me with weight I could not ignore. What started as a lunch-break catch-up about clients, ad spend, and chatbot configurations carried within it a current of testimony that the natural ear might easily miss. There is a moment in the exchange — almost casual, almost offhand — where everything true about the last two years gets named: *“Only when we introduced God did business start working.”* That line is not a footnote. That line is the whole sermon. **Why This Matters:** The Body of Christ in this generation is desperately in need of living testimonies that God’s lordship is not a liability in the marketplace but the very source of its fruitfulness. Many of the brethren have been taught — subtly, systemic-ally, and sometimes explicitly — to keep their faith private and their business secular, as though the Kingdom of God has no jurisdiction over commerce, strategy, creativity, or stewardship. What I heard in this conversation is a refutation of that lie told through two men who tried it the other way first, paid the price for it, and are now walking in something different. This matters not because we are extraordinary, but because the pattern is scriptural, the testimony is real, and beloved brethren who are still grinding in their own strength need to hear that there is another way. ## The Worldly Mind’s Interpretation The natural mind listening to this call would hear a straightforward story of entrepreneurial momentum: two young men who have developed transferable skills, found a motivated client, deployed smart digital marketing strategy, achieved measurable ROI, and are now scaling intelligently by positioning themselves as consultants rather than laborers. The world would rightly identify the savvy in separating advisory from execution, the wisdom in building passive income through percentage-based deals, the efficiency of leveraging UGC and paid social media. It would nod approvingly at the idea of reinvesting half the profit into more ad spend and call this good business instinct. The world would even find the mention of God palatable as a kind of motivational footnote — the way athletes thank God in post-game interviews — culturally acceptable, emotionally warm, and largely unthreatening to the real engine, which it assumes is human cleverness and hustle. **The Limitation:** But that reading misses the most consequential part of what was said. The world has no category for what my brother and I were actually confessing to each other: that there was a version of us before this — ambitious, talented, striving — and that version did not work, not because of missing skills or wrong timing, but because it was filled with something that had to die. Ego. Pride. A self-oriented orientation toward the world and its rewards. The natural mind cannot perceive that the fruitfulness now present is not the result of learning better tactics, but of surviving a divine surgery. The soul that has not been broken open by God genuinely cannot distinguish between a season of favor earned and a season of favor given, because it has never experienced the terrifying mercy of having something stripped away by a Father who loved it too much to let it succeed in the wrong thing. ## What the Spirit Reveals Through It I am sitting with the weight of what my brother said almost in passing — *“We introduced God and business started working”* — and I cannot move past it, because the Spirit is pressing something into it that is far older than our story. This is the pattern of the Kingdom. It does not begin with breakthrough. It begins with burial. And what we were describing, without using the theological vocabulary, is exactly what the Lord Jesus meant when He said in John 12:24, *“Most assuredly, I say to you, unless a grain of wheat falls into the ground and dies, it remains alone; but if it dies, it produces much grain.”* The grain that remains alone — that is the version of us that was talented, driven, and completely self-referential. It could not produce much because it would not die. And God, in His mercy, did not let us succeed in that condition. What arrests me here is the specificity of what had to die. I named it plainly in the call: ego and pride. And I want to sit with those words because they are not merely personality flaws to be managed through better self-awareness. In the language of Scripture, what we are describing is what Paul calls in Galatians 5:20 the works of the flesh — not just sexual immorality and idolatry, but specifically *“selfish ambitions”* and *“contentions.”* The Greek word behind “selfish ambition” is ἐριθεία (eritheia, eh-ree-THAY-ah), and it carries with it the ancient picture of a day laborer who works not for the love of the work or the good of the household, but purely for personal gain and competitive advantage. That was the spirit animating the earlier version of the business — not demonic in the dramatic sense, but shaped by ἐριθεία (eh-ree-THAY-ah) at its root. And that spirit, no matter how talented the person it inhabits, will always produce strife, burnout, and ultimately emptiness. The Lord did not patch that. He killed it. And I find it remarkable that both my brother and I use the language of death and resurrection naturally, without forcing it, because that is genuinely what it felt like. There were seasons — and Samuel and I both know them — where what we were building collapsed. Where deals fell through, where partnerships broke, where money was lost and confidence was shaken. The natural mind calls those failures. The scriptural mind calls them the grain going into the ground. You cannot arrive at *“now it’s just making money”* without passing through the grave. The sequence is not optional. What I am also seeing in this conversation is a beautiful illustration of what stewardship looks like after the ego has been surrendered. My brother describes his posture with his young client with real precision: *“I’m not doing it. I’m telling him what to do.”* On the surface, that sounds like the practical wisdom of a consultant protecting his time. But there is something deeper operating here. He looked at this young man and said, *“I feel like I am looking at my younger self.”* That moment of recognition — of seeing your own past in someone else’s present — is one of the most profound gifts the Lord gives to those He has walked through the fire. It is the gift of compassionate authority. Not authority that dominates, but authority that has been tested and now serves. This is the elder who has earned the right to speak not because of a title but because of a scar. This resonates deeply with what Paul writes in 2 Corinthians 1:4 — that God *“comforts us in all our tribulation, that we may be able to comfort those who are in any trouble, with the comfort with which we ourselves are comforted by God.”* The Hebrew concept underlying this kind of comfort is not mere emotional soothing; it is the idea of coming alongside with strength. The word in the Greek here is παράκλησις (paraklēsis, pah-RAH-clay-sis), which shares its root with the title of the Holy Spirit Himself — the Paraclete, the One called alongside. When my brother positions himself alongside this young entrepreneur and sets up his infrastructure, secures his payments, builds his visibility, and refuses to simply run his business for him — he is, whether he knows it consciously or not, operating in a paracletic posture. He is not consuming the younger man’s potential; he is cultivating it. And the fruit of that is reflected in the numbers, but the numbers are not the point. The point is what the numbers represent: order where there was disorder, credibility where there was instability, growth where there was stagnation. This is the pattern of Proverbs 11:14 — *“Where there is no counsel, the people fall; but in the multitude of counselors there is safety.”* God has positioned my brother as a counselor to this young man not because Samuel pursued that title, but because he was faithful in the furnace and came out knowing things that can only be learned there. I am also arrested by the vision that began to emerge toward the end of the call — the idea of gathering a small group of Christian investors to fund something Kingdom-facing: an event, a camp, a gathering where prophetic voices are released and the resources flow not inward but outward. What strikes me about this vision is not its scale but its orientation. The first instinct after the breakthrough is not *“how do we scale the business?”* but *“how do we steward this for the Kingdom?”* That instinct is not natural. That instinct is the fruit of a heart that has been genuinely reoriented by the Lord. Ecclesiastes 5:10 says, *“He who loves silver will not be satisfied with silver; nor he who loves abundance, with increase.”* The heart shaped by ἐριθεία (eh-ree-THAY-ah) will never arrive at *“let’s give it back.”* Only a heart that has been broken of the love of increase and re-filled with love for God and His people will, in a moment of financial momentum, instinctively begin dreaming about prophets and gatherings and Kingdom investments. The detail my brother offered — that both the investors and the prophets would be present, and that every cut taken would be paid back in Kingdom investment — carries with it an echo of the Levitical principle. In Numbers 18, the Levites received the tithes of Israel as their inheritance, but they were then commanded to present a tithe of the tithe to the Lord. There was no exemption from the principle of return even for those who served at the altar. What Samuel was describing — taking a percentage of business revenue and dedicating it toward prophetic gathering and Kingdom events — is a New Covenant expression of that same ancient logic: those who receive must also release. I am also struck by something simpler but deeply important: the fact that we spoke of sending a monthly Bible study newsletter to every person who has ever entered the pipeline — every contact, every lead, every past client. This detail could easily be lost in the business specifics around it, but it is not incidental. It is the confession of a vision that sees the marketplace not as the destination but as the mission field. Every person who came seeking a website or a marketing solution is also a person for whom Christ died. The business becomes the net, and the net is not for us to keep but to cast. This is consistent with what Jesus said in Matthew 4:19 — *“Follow Me, and I will make you fishers of men.”* The skill set does not change; the purpose of it does. And then there is the chatbot. The second half of the call shifts into practical technical discussion — WhatsApp AI, inbound qualification, Google Meet booking, calendar integration. The natural mind might see this as a gear change from the spiritual to the secular. But I am not sure the Lord sees it that way. Excellence in craft, precision in service, building systems that honor the time and dignity of others — these are not separate from holiness. Colossians 3:23 does not carve out an exception for technical work: *“And whatever you do, do it heartily, as to the Lord and not to men.”* The chatbot built with integrity, deployed in service of genuine qualification and honest communication, is as much an act of worship as the newsletter. The Kingdom does not abandon the practical. It sanctifies it. Finally, I am sitting with the weight of the closing words of the call — almost throwaway, almost routine: *“All right, I’ll catch you then. God bless you. Yeah, bless you.”* Two men, in the middle of a working day, closing a business call with blessing. Not performance. Not religious habit. Just the natural overflow of two hearts that have been changed enough that blessing is the language that comes out when the call ends. That is not nothing. That is the fruit of a long, painful, mercy-filled transformation. That is grain that went into the ground and came up as something the world did not plant and cannot explain. ## Implications and Challenges There is a pervasive theology in much of the Western church that treats business, entrepreneurship, and marketplace success as spiritually neutral at best and spiritually dangerous at worst. This call challenges that framing directly. Commerce conducted under the Lordship of Christ, shaped by genuine death-to-self, and oriented toward Kingdom stewardship is not a compromise of the sacred — it is an extension of it. The implication is that we need a more robust theology of work, not a more careful quarantine of it. The conversation also challenges the idea that spiritual maturity produces passivity. My brother is deeply engaged, strategically sharp, and willing to work hard. What changed is not the energy but the source and the aim of the energy. Maturity does not produce withdrawal from the world; it produces engagement with the world from a different center. Beloved brethren who are waiting to “feel spiritual enough” before re-engaging their vocations should hear this: the Lord wants you in the field, not merely in the prayer closet, though you must pass through the prayer closet to be trusted in the field. There is also a challenge here to those who mix faith and commerce but never genuinely surrender the engine. Samuel and I were not transformed by adding Christian language to an unchanged ambition. The transformation came through death — through seasons of loss that God permitted and even orchestrated. Those who want the fruit without the furnace will find themselves producing the same hollow results they always have, with a Scripture verse attached. The grain that will not die remains alone. That is not a threat — it is a law. The vision of gathering Christian investors and releasing resources toward prophetic gathering confronts a common poverty mindset in the church that treats financial increase as inherently suspect. The issue is never whether the money is present; the issue is always whether the money is master or servant. My brother’s instinct to immediately route margin back toward Kingdom purposes is the evidence that the money is servant. The church needs more of this — not prosperity gospel sentimentality, but genuine covenant stewardship that sees increase as ammunition for the mission, not reward for the righteous. Finally, the detail of the newsletter — the quiet intention to reach every contact with the Word — carries an implicit rebuke to the idea that evangelism and marketplace activity must be kept formally separate. The pipeline becomes pastoral ground. Every person who submitted a form or took a meeting is also a potential recipient of the living Word. This is not manipulation or bait-and-switch; it is the natural overflow of a life that cannot contain what God has put in it. May the Lord multiply that instinct in us and in every believer who has been given both a skill set and a testimony. ## What This Means For Us **For Spiritual Maturity:** This conversation calls me — and those who will read this — to stop treating the death seasons as interruptions and start receiving them as the essential preparation for fruitfulness. Maturity is not the accumulation of spiritual experiences; it is the willingness to let God kill what needs to die, however long and however painful the process. What came out of the furnace in this testimony is not merely better business strategy — it is a different quality of person, and that quality only forms in the fire. **For Kingdom Obedience:** The obedience this meditation calls forth is specific: bring every domain of life — every skill, every contact, every margin, every platform — under the active Lordship of Christ. Not as a spiritual exercise performed alongside the “real” work, but as the governing reality that shapes what the real work is for. This means asking, in every deal and every conversation, not only *“Is this profitable?”* but *“Is this what the Lord would have me build, and for whom?”* **For Prophetic Posture:** The prophetic posture this conversation models is one of patient, strategic, Spirit-led stewardship. The vision of a Christian investor gathering, a newsletter reaching the pipeline, a consultant who speaks the path without walking it for others — all of these are postures of one who sees further than the immediate transaction and is willing to build slowly toward something that outlasts the deal. This is the posture of the faithful steward in Matthew 25 — not the one who buried the talent in religious caution, and not the one who spent it on himself, but the one who multiplied it for the Master’s return. ## A Closing Prayer Father, I come before You with gratitude that reaches deeper than words for what You have done in my brother and in me over these two years. You did not let us succeed in the wrong version of ourselves, and that mercy — though it was painful in the seasons of its application — is now something I would not trade for anything. You killed the ego. You buried the pride. You let the grain go into the ground. And now You are bringing up fruit that we did not plant and could not produce on our own, and we know it, and we say so plainly. Lord, I ask now that You guard this fruitfulness — that You keep the spirit of ἐριθεία (eh-ree-THAY-ah) far from us as the numbers increase, that You preserve in us the instinct to give back, to bless, to build for Your Kingdom rather than our own names. Let the newsletter reach hearts that are hungry. Let the vision of a Christian gathering come to pass in Your timing and by Your provision. Let every client, every contact, and every conversation be held in the light of eternity. And for every believer who reads this and recognizes the furnace they are currently in — Lord, speak to them now. Tell them the grain is not wasted in the ground. Tell them the death they are experiencing is not the end of the story. You are faithful. You finish what You start. And the grain that falls into the ground and dies does not remain alone — it produces much fruit, for Your glory and for the blessing of Your people.
