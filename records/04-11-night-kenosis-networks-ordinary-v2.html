This is not a novel idea in biblical theology; it traces back through the Reformation doctrine of imputed righteousness, through patristic discussions of the old Adam and new creation, and into the Hebrew courtroom imagery of Job and Zechariah. What makes it worth noting here is that it is being articulated with care, in the middle of an ordinary evening, as something genuinely believed and not merely quoted.
