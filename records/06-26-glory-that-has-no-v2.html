# 06-26 Call Reflection: Kingdom Entrepreneurship, Divine Authority, and the Glory That Belongs to God **Title:** 06-26 at 15:03 — The Glory That Has No Room for Your Strategy **Recorded:** 2026-06-26 15:03:41 **Source Discernment:** C — Recorded Conversation or Event (Live Capture) **Classification:** Call Reflection ## Opening Observation As I listened to my brother work through the sting of a failed ad campaign, something far weightier than marketing strategy began to surface in our exchange. The real tension pressing through this conversation was not about click-through rates or cost-per-acquisition — it was about the ancient and unresolved question of whose authority governs the building. I kept finding myself pressed by the Spirit to say it plainly: God will not lend His glory to a structure built without Him at the foundation, and He will often let our best strategies collapse precisely so that the glory, when it comes, belongs to no one but Him. **Why This Matters:** This matters because the Body of Christ is filled with sincere, Spirit-touched people who are simultaneously operating from two incompatible authorities — they have received a genuine word from God about what He intends to do, and yet they are executing that word through the wisdom of the age rather than through the obedience of faith. The gap between those two postures is not a gap of effort or intelligence; it is a gap of lordship. When we confuse sincere desire to build for God with actual submission to how God said He would build, we do not merely waste resources — we misrepresent the nature of the Kingdom and rob the watching world of the testimony that would have pointed them unmistakably to heaven. The collapse of a strategy is not always judgment; sometimes it is the most merciful thing the Lord can do before the thing grows too large to easily surrender. ## The Worldly Mind’s Interpretation From the vantage point of natural human reasoning, what my brother was experiencing in this call is simply a painful but instructive business lesson: ads require targeting expertise, proper audience segmentation, and sufficient budget to generate meaningful data. The mind of the flesh would conclude that the problem was execution — wrong platform settings, insufficient spend, poorly constructed creative — and the obvious correction would be to optimize the next campaign rather than abandon the channel entirely. This is not an evil framework; it is competent, practical, and even wise by the standards of the marketplace. A secular coach or a business mentor would walk my brother through a post-mortem analysis, adjust the variables, and encourage him to try again with more refined targeting. The emotional weight he was carrying — the discouragement, the sense of having wasted what little he had — would be reframed as tuition paid at the school of entrepreneurship, a recoverable setback on the road to eventual market fit. **The Limitation:** The problem with that framework is not that it lacks intelligence; it is that it lacks the capacity to perceive what kind of story is actually being written. Natural reasoning reads the circumstances but cannot read the Author. It can analyze why an ad campaign failed by the metrics of the industry, but it has no instrument to measure whether God withheld conversion precisely because the method bypassed His instruction. The natural mind sees a free video that generated three sales from fewer than five hundred views and files it under “anomaly” or “lucky break” — something to be scaled through investment and infrastructure. But the Spirit reads that same event as a signature, a thumbprint left in the data by the hand of One who was announcing: *this is how I intend to move*. The veil between those two readings is not lifted by more analysis; it is lifted by the fear of the Lord and the memory of what He actually said in the secret place. ## What the Spirit Reveals Through It As I sit with what moved through that conversation, I am struck by how the entire exchange orbited around a single, deceptively simple principle: God’s methods are chosen precisely because they cannot be mistaken for human competence. The apostle Paul pressed this very point into the Corinthian community when he wrote that God chose the foolish things of the world to put to shame the wise, the weak things to put to shame the mighty, and the base and despised things — things which are not — to bring to nothing the things that are, *that no flesh should glory in His presence* (1 Corinthians 1:27–29, NKJV). I kept returning to that phrase as my brother described his ad spend: *that no flesh should glory*. The Lord is not merely indifferent to our strategies when He withholds blessing from them. He is actively guarding the glory that must attach to the testimony when it finally arrives. There is a word in the Greek New Testament that captures the texture of what the Lord was doing in that collapse — παιδεία (paideia, pie-DAY-ah) — fatherly training, discipline administered by a father who loves the child too much to let them build a monument to their own wisdom. I actually used the word aloud near the close of the call, almost instinctively, because something in the Spirit had already named what was happening before my mind could. παιδεία (pie-DAY-ah) is not punishment in the penal sense; it is the precise, measured intervention of a Father who sees the whole arc of what He is forming and refuses to allow a shortcut that would produce a smaller, lesser version of the son He intends. The writer of Hebrews tells us that this training *yields the peaceable fruit of righteousness to those who have been trained by it* (Hebrews 12:11, NKJV) — but only to those who remain in it rather than fighting their way out of it with a new strategy. What arrests me here is the contrast I kept drawing in that conversation between two kinds of knowledge: the knowledge that comes from projected KPIs, LTV calculations, and cost-per-click benchmarks, and the knowledge that comes from one instruction received in private prayer, in the secret place, and obeyed immediately. I am not dismissing the former as irrelevant — once God is building, the measuring instruments serve Him. But the instruments cannot be the source. In Scripture, every great builder was first a listener. Noah did not design the ark from structural engineering principles and then ask God to bless the blueprint. He received exact specifications — dimensions, materials, the number of decks — and built to the word precisely as it was given, down to the cubit (Genesis 6:14–16, NKJV). The specificity of the divine instruction was itself the testimony. When the flood came, nobody could look at Noah’s survival and say, *he was a brilliant naval architect.* I am seeing this same pattern echoed in Gideon’s army. The Lord did not merely ask Gideon to trust Him in the face of overwhelming odds; He systematically removed every possible source of human credit from the equation. He reduced thirty-two thousand men to three hundred (Judges 7:2–7, NKJV), and He did so explicitly on the grounds that *Israel might claim glory for itself against Me, saying, “My own hand has saved me.”* The language there is remarkable — God names the spiritual consequence of allowing human sufficiency to remain in the equation. It does not merely produce pride; it produces a false narrative about who is the actual victor. And a false narrative about who won the battle is ultimately a false narrative about who God is. The method carries the message. My brother’s free video that generated sales from fewer than five hundred views was a Gideon moment. It was the Lord reducing the army, whispering: *watch what I do with almost nothing, so that when I multiply it, everyone who sees it will know it was Me.* The tragedy of the ad spend was not financial — though the financial sting was real. The deeper grief is that the impulse to spend was an impulse to add soldiers back to the army, to make the outcome more explainable, more scalable, more attributable to competent execution. And the Lord, in His mercy, simply let the spend produce nothing — not to embarrass my brother, but to protect the testimony that is still being formed. I found myself telling him what I have had to learn through my own decade of building, winning, and losing nearly all of it almost instantly: the intercessory, hidden, prophetic path is not the path I would have chosen either. I wanted to be the builder. I wanted to be the entrepreneur. I pursued that with everything I had for years, and I made real progress — and then watched it collapse because the foundation, if I am honest, was my own ambition sanctified with prayer rather than His instruction executed in faith. There is a difference between *building for God* and *building with God as the source and architect.* The first can produce very impressive structures that will not stand. The second often looks embarrassingly small for a very long time before it becomes something that cannot be explained away. The conversation then moved into a different but connected territory — my brother’s decision to remain unseen when family members arrived who carry a disruptive spiritual atmosphere. He had simply stayed away, not out of bitterness or conflict-avoidance, but out of a deliberate choice to protect what is being formed in him. I recognized that instinct immediately as something Scripture honors far more than modern Christian culture typically allows. Jesus Himself withdrew — often. He withdrew from the crowds after feeding the five thousand (John 6:15, NKJV). He withdrew to the wilderness before the launch of His ministry (Luke 4:1–2, NKJV). He withdrew to a solitary place before major decisions (Luke 6:12, NKJV). Withdrawal is not weakness. In the Kingdom, strategic hiddenness is a form of warfare — it protects the thing being formed in the secret place from being diluted, argued into ambiguity, or pulled sideways by the demands of relationships that are not yet aligned with the assignment. What I said to him in the call was this: there are seasons where we need to be hidden so that what is being formed in us can eventually be sent back into those darkened territories with real authority. That is not avoidance; that is gestation. The wilderness does not reject the prophet — it finishes him. John the Baptist was in the wilderness until the day of his public appearance to Israel (Luke 1:80, NKJV), and no one was permitted to rush that formation on his behalf. The isolation that feels like loss is often the very container in which the Lord is doing His most irreversible work. I also want to sit with something I said near the middle of the call that I believe carries real weight, even in the midst of laughter: *God told you what way He’s going to do it, and you are not listening.* I said it plainly, and I said it with love, but I want to unfold it here because the implications are serious. When God gives a specific word about the method — not just the destination, not just the promise, but the *how* — and the recipient overrides that word with a more sensible-seeming strategy, something very particular happens. The strategy does not merely fail to perform well; it begins to compete with the word. Every investment in the alternative strategy is, spiritually speaking, a vote of no-confidence cast against the Lord’s instruction. This is what happened at Meribah — the word said *speak to the rock* (Numbers 20:8, NKJV), Moses *struck* it, and water came out anyway, but the disobedience in the method cost him the land. The miracle did not validate the deviation from the instruction. This brings me to the deepest layer I want to name: there is a kind of faith that produces results through human ingenuity and then credits God for the open doors — and there is a kind of faith that produces results through radical obedience to a specific divine instruction, and God receives the full credit because no other explanation is available. The first kind of faith is common. The second kind of faith is rare, and it is costly, and it is the only kind that produces testimonies powerful enough to shift atmospheres. My brother is being invited into the second kind. The failure of the ads is not the end of his story; it is the first clear chapter of a testimony that will one day make it unmistakably evident that what he built, God built — and nobody can take that away from him. I am also sitting with the brief but weighty moment when he said *I’m not gonna give up.* Something about that landed with me in the Spirit, because it revealed that his resolve is real — he is not fragile, he is not finished, and the discouragement, while genuine, has not broken the covenant thread running through his life and his calling. That is παιδεία (pie-DAY-ah) doing its work. A man who cannot be broken by a failed strategy but can be re-directed by the Lord’s correction is exactly the kind of man through whom the Kingdom advances without apology. Finally, there is something I want to honor in the way this conversation held together — the laughter, the ribbing, the unfiltered brotherhood running through the whole exchange. Covenant relationship is not sterile. It is not always solemn. The Lord builds His people through authentic connection, through the kind of honesty that can say *I told you so* without destroying the relationship, and through the kind of loyalty that says *I’ll pray for you* as a closing word and means every syllable of it. What I saw in my brother through this call was a man being shaped, in real time, into someone who will one day know with unshakeable certainty that *this was not my idea and I could not have built it* — and that knowledge will be worth every dollar spent on ads that went nowhere. ## Implications and Challenges The instinct to scale what God is doing through conventional growth mechanisms — advertising, funnels, audience targeting — is so normalized in contemporary Christian entrepreneurship that it rarely gets examined spiritually. We have absorbed the assumption that good strategy, sanctified by prayer before we launch it, is essentially the same thing as Spirit-led obedience. This conversation challenges that assumption at the root. There is a categorical difference between asking God to bless a plan and receiving a plan from God. The former keeps us in the driver’s seat with a co-pilot who is expected to navigate toward our preferred destination. The latter requires genuine surrender of the steering wheel, which most of us are willing to offer in worship but resist in practice. There is also a quiet idolatry that lives inside metrics-driven thinking, and it is easy to miss because the metrics themselves are morally neutral. KPIs, LTV, cost-per-click — these are tools, and tools have their proper season. But when the metrics become the primary language through which we interpret God’s movement and approval, we have created a measurement system that the Holy Spirit did not ordain and cannot be reduced to. The result is that we read divine favor through human performance indicators and interpret a failing campaign as a failing call — when sometimes a failing campaign is simply God refusing to fund a method that would steal His glory from the testimony He is preparing. The willingness to remain hidden — to stay away from environments that would drain or dilute what is being formed — is almost entirely unsupported by the contemporary Christian cultural expectation of constant visibility, constant connection, and constant output. My brother’s instinct to avoid his cousins during that family visit was spiritually sound, but it would have looked to the outside observer like antisocial behavior or even spiritual pride. The Church needs to recover a theology of hiddenness, of gestation, of the wilderness season that is not a failure to launch but a necessary formation before the launch can mean anything. We must also grapple with how rarely we actually obey specific instructions from the Lord without immediately negotiating with them. The transcript surfaced this pattern clearly: a word had been given, a method had been indicated, and my brother’s impulse was to supplement that method with something more statistically promising. This is not unusual — it is the default posture of sincere believers who are not yet fully persuaded that God’s way will actually work. The remedy is not condemnation; it is memory. We must cultivate the practice of remembering what God actually said, writing it down, returning to it, and measuring our activity against it rather than against industry benchmarks. Finally, the pattern of prophetic friendship modeled in this conversation deserves to be named and honored. I did not simply agree with my brother to protect the relationship. I pressed him repeatedly — with love, with laughter, with shared personal failure — toward the actual word of the Lord. That kind of covenant accountability is increasingly rare in the Body of Christ, where affirmation is often mistaken for encouragement and correction is feared as condemnation. True covenant friendship will risk the discomfort of speaking plainly because it values the brother’s destiny more than the brother’s momentary comfort. ## What This Means For Us **For Spiritual Maturity:** Growing into genuine spiritual maturity in the area of Kingdom entrepreneurship and calling means developing the capacity to distinguish between the voice that says *scale it* and the voice that says *obey the specific instruction I gave you.* It means learning to read a failed strategy not primarily as a business lesson but as a theological event — a moment in which the Lord is clarifying whose authority governs the building. It also means cultivating the discipline of remembering and returning to the specific word received in the secret place, especially when circumstances are applying pressure to abandon it for something more sensible. **For Kingdom Obedience:** The call to Kingdom obedience that emerges here is specific and demanding: do what the Lord said, the way He said it, immediately after He said it, and resist the impulse to supplement that word with strategies drawn from the wisdom of the age. This does not mean disengaging from the practical realities of building a business or a ministry. It means ensuring that the *source* of the method is divine instruction rather than projected outcomes. It also means honoring the seasons of hiddenness as valid and necessary rather than treating them as embarrassing delays to be overcome through more aggressive effort. **For Prophetic Posture:** The prophetic posture being shaped through an exchange like this one is a posture of radical patience — the willingness to let God build at His pace, through His method, toward His testimony, without forcing the timeline through human initiative. It is also the posture of covenant faithfulness to brothers and sisters who are in the middle of their own formation, speaking plainly when plain speech is needed and trusting the relationship to hold the weight of honest correction. The prophet who only speaks what his brother wants to hear is not a prophet; he is a flatterer with spiritual vocabulary. Covenant truth-telling, spoken in love and backed by shared life, is one of the most irreplaceable gifts the Body of Christ can offer one another. ## A Closing Prayer Father, I come before You with gratitude for the covenant You have woven between brothers who are being formed by the same hand and sent toward the same Kingdom. I thank You that You are faithful enough to let strategies fail when those strategies would have stolen the glory that belongs to You alone. I ask that You continue to press into my brother everything that the παιδεία (pie-DAY-ah) of this season is designed to produce — not discouragement, but recalibration; not defeat, but reorientation toward the specific instruction he received from You in the secret place. Lord, make him unmistakably a man through whom You build in ways that leave no room for any explanation but Your own sovereign grace and faithful provision. Guard what is being formed in the hidden season. Protect the peace that belongs to those who remain in covenant with You and with each other. And when the testimony finally stands — when the thing that could only have been You is undeniably visible — let every witness know that You did it, that You chose the foolish things, the weak things, the despised things, so that no flesh would glory in Your presence. To You alone belong the glory, the honor, and the dominion, now and in every age to come.
