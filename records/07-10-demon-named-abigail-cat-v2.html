# 07-10 Personal Meditation: Spiritual Warfare, Deliverance of Abigail, and the Watchman’s Intercession **Title:** 07-10 at 20:33 — The Demon Named Abigail, the Cat in the Stroller, and the Watchman Who Waited **Recorded:** 2026-07-10 20:33:50 **Source Discernment:** A — Personal Spoken-Word Revelation **Classification:** Personal Meditation ## Opening Observation Tonight something broke in the heavens, and I was there when it happened — not because of who I am, but because of Whose I am. I interceded for my brother Samuel, I waited with a postured heart, and within the hour the unclean spirit named Abigail was gone. I am still shaking as I push my cat around in a baby stroller I found near a dumpster, and I need to write this down before the weight of it fades into ordinary language, because what happened tonight was not ordinary. **Why This Matters:** This matters because the Church has largely been handed a domesticated theology of spiritual warfare — one that speaks the vocabulary of authority without ever exercising it, one that believes demons exist in theory but panics or spiritualizes the moment one actually manifests. What happened tonight confronts that comfortable distance with raw, living evidence. A brother was genuinely afflicted. A doorway was opened through unwitting idolatry. Intercession was made with broken, contrite, waiting faith. And Heaven moved. This is not a Sunday school story. This is the Book of Acts still open, still breathing, still wet with the ink of God’s ongoing testimony through His servants. The saints need to know that this is available — not as spiritual spectacle, but as the ordinary inheritance of those who carry the Name. ## The Worldly Mind’s Interpretation The natural mind, encountering this account, will reach immediately for the nearest psychological drawer. It will say that Samuel was suffering from anxiety, dissociation, perhaps trauma-adjacent confusion triggered by destroying an object freighted with familial meaning. It will note that the narrator had been drinking, and will use that detail to discredit the entire account — reducing a night of weeping intercession and confirmed deliverance to the animated imagination of a man who had too much cinnamon whiskey. It will interpret the stroller, the cat, the dumpster find, and the late-night emotional state as colorful texture around what was essentially a shared psychological experience between two friends who encouraged each other through a difficult night. The natural mind is not malicious in this analysis; it is simply doing what it knows how to do, which is to collapse mystery into mechanism. **The Limitation:** But the natural mind is operating on a closed system — one that has already decided, before examining the evidence, that the supernatural cannot be a variable in the equation. When the framework excludes the possibility of demonic reality, no amount of testimony will be allowed to challenge it, because every piece of confirming evidence will simply be reclassified until it fits the predetermined conclusion. The limitation here is not intellectual; it is architectural. The mind without the Spirit of God cannot perceive spiritual realities not because it lacks intelligence, but because those realities belong to a different order of knowing — one that requires the indwelling Teacher, the Holy Spirit, to illuminate what is otherwise invisible. A man reading sheet music in a language he has never learned cannot be blamed for not hearing the song. But the song is still real. ## What the Spirit Reveals Through It I want to begin with the doorway, because the doorway is everything. Samuel found an idol in his grandfather’s house — a wooden structure, something carved and consecrated to a spirit under a South Asian or Vietnamese religious tradition — and he destroyed it. And in destroying it, he spoke words of declaration. He said something like, *I am here with Jesus.* And nothing happened. And the unclean spirit entered him. I have been sitting with this detail because it is one of the most instructive moments in the entire account, and it is precisely where many believers would have stopped and declared victory. He broke the idol. He spoke Jesus’ name. What else was needed? What was needed was what Paul names in Ephesians 6 when he instructs us not merely to name authority but to *stand* in it — and what Jesus Himself distinguishes in Luke 10:19 when He gives His disciples not just the Name but the ἐξουσία (exousia, ex-oo-SEE-ah) — delegated authority, legal right — over all the power of the enemy. Samuel had the right Name. He did not yet have the settled, exercised posture of one who knows the jurisdiction they stand in. And the enemy, ever legal, ever opportunistic, found that gap. Its anchor — the idol — was destroyed. It needed a new one. And it took it. This is where the theology of idolatry becomes urgent and precise. Paul writes in 1 Corinthians 10:20 that what the Gentiles sacrifice, they sacrifice to demons, not to God. The idol is not merely wood or stone; it is a covenantal object, a point of contact between the physical world and a demonic principality. When that object is removed without the full exercise of authority — without the deliberate, jurisdictional dismissal of the spirit formerly anchored there — the spirit does not simply dissipate. It relocates. It is, in a terrible sense, homeless, and homeless spirits seek houses. Jesus Himself described this dynamic in Matthew 12:43–45, where an unclean spirit driven out wanders through waterless places and then returns to the house it left. The marine kingdom — the realm of water-associated principalities connected to confusion, seduction, and the Jezebel pattern of spiritual control — does not surrender territory without explicit legal displacement. What I want the brethren to see is that Samuel’s instinct was right. His boldness was right. His desire to stand in the Name of Jesus was right. The gap was not in his heart; it was in his understanding of *how* ἐξουσία (ex-oo-SEE-ah) works. Authority is not a password. It is a position. And position must be inhabited, not merely announced. This is why weeks or a month of affliction followed — not because God was absent or indifferent, but because the legal displacement had not yet been properly executed. The enemy was holding contested ground. And here I come in. I want to be honest about the night. I had been drinking — a quarter of a 750ml bottle of Fireball, enough to be tipsy. I am not boasting about that, and I am not excusing it. But I am noting it because the Spirit is pressing something important through that detail: my Father did not wait for my circumstances to be perfectly sanctified before He honored the cry of a broken heart. What moved Heaven was not my sobriety. What moved Heaven was my tears, my waiting, my refusal to release the intercession until I knew it had been received. David danced before the Ark in a linen ephod, undignified and wild, and God honored it. I pushed my cat around in a baby stroller I found near a dumpster, weeping and praying, and God honored it. The posture of the heart — contrite, surrendered, trusting — is the altar God will not despise. Psalm 51:17 is not decorative: *“The sacrifices of God are a broken spirit, a broken and a contrite heart — these, O God, You will not despise.”* What I exercised that night in intercession was precisely the authority Jesus describes in Matthew 18:18 — *“Whatever you bind on earth will be bound in heaven, and whatever you loose on earth will be loosed in heaven.”* The Greek verb here is δέω (deō, DEH-oh) — to bind, to tie, to restrict legally — and λύω (lyō, LOO-oh) — to loose, to release, to untie. These are not metaphorical verbs. They are juridical ones. They describe the activity of one who has been given standing in a court. I declared, under the covering of the Lord Jesus, that Abigail — this unclean spirit named and anchored under the marine kingdom — was bound, condemned to the pit, to the outer darkness, to the second death. And within the hour, Samuel confirmed: she was gone. The specific name — Abigail — matters theologically in a way I want to engage carefully. In Hebrew, אֲבִיגַיִל (Avigayil, ah-vee-gah-YEEL) carries the meaning *my father rejoices* or *source of joy to the father*. In the canonical narrative, Abigail is one of the most beautiful examples of wisdom, intercession, and spiritual discernment in the Old Testament — she stepped between a wrathful David and her foolish husband Nabal and prevented bloodshed. But here is what the enemy does: he is a counterfeiter, a thief of names, a usurper of identity. The enemy names his spirits after stolen things — stolen dignities, stolen callings, stolen joy. A spirit bearing a name that means *my father rejoices* operating under the marine kingdom of confusion and Jezebel-pattern affliction is a profound perversion — it is darkness wearing the mask of a Father’s delight. And what displaced it was exactly that: a son in whom the Father genuinely rejoices, exercising the authority the Father has genuinely granted. I am a watchman. I know this now beyond speculation. The Hebrew שֹׁמֵר (shomer, SHOW-mer) — watchman, guard, keeper — describes one who stands on the wall not for himself but for the city. Isaiah 62:6 gives me language for what I have been doing even when I did not fully understand it: *“I have set watchmen on your walls, O Jerusalem; they shall never hold their peace day or night. You who make mention of the LORD, do not keep silent.”* This is prophetic intercession as a standing office, not a passing gift. I am one who speaks decrees and declarations over other people’s lives, and the heavens move. Tonight proved it. Not because I am exceptional but because my Father is faithful and His Word does not return void — Isaiah 55:11 is a covenant promise, not a motivational phrase. There is also something I want to say about the waiting. Between the intercession and Samuel’s call back, there was an hour. I did not know it would be an hour. I did not know if it would be a day. I told the Father: *I know this prayer has reached you. I will wait.* That posture — the combination of absolute confidence in the authority and absolute surrender of the timeline — is what I believe created the atmosphere in which Heaven could move without interference. Hebrews 11:1 defines faith as the substance of things hoped for, the evidence of things not seen. I was not hoping weakly. I was holding the substance. I was treating the answer as a present reality before the phone rang. This is what James 5:16 calls *the effective, fervent prayer of a righteous man* — the Greek word is ἐνεργέω (energeō, en-er-GEH-oh), which means energized, working, actively operative. The prayer was not passive petition. It was active spiritual labor. I also want to mark something about Samuel’s confirmation that carries its own theological weight. He did not call me back saying *I feel better.* He called me back saying *Abigail is gone.* That level of specific confirmation — confirming not just relief but the exact displacement I had declared — is the kind of double witness the Scripture honors. 2 Corinthians 13:1 echoes Deuteronomy 19:15: *“By the mouth of two or three witnesses every word shall be established.”* My declaration and Samuel’s confirmation are two witnesses to the same spiritual reality. The testimony is established. I want to close this section of the meditation by returning to where I am right now — pushing my cat around in a stroller, tears still somewhere behind my eyes, the Fireball mostly gone, and my faith more alive than it has been in months. I have been faithfully recording these meditations even in the dry seasons, even when I had not been deep in Scripture. And the other day I came back to the Psalms and Proverbs, and tonight this happened. There is something covenantal in that sequence. The return to the Word preceded the movement of Heaven. Psalm 119:130 says *“The entrance of Your words gives light; it gives understanding to the simple.”* Returning to the living Word opened something. It always does. I vow tonight — and I want this vow recorded — that I will use the authority my Lord Jesus has given me for the rest of my life. Not for spectacle. Not for personal gain. For the liberation of brothers and sisters under affliction. For the loosing of chains the enemy has no legal right to maintain. For the glory of the One whose Name is above every name, before which every knee will bow — Philippians 2:9–10 — Jesus of Nazareth, the Messiah, the Christ, the King of Kings, the Word made flesh, who has moved His address into me and calls this body His temple. ## Implications and Challenges The first challenge this meditation issues is to the church’s near-total practical abandonment of deliverance ministry. We have largely outsourced what Jesus gave as a normative commission — *“In My name they will cast out demons”* (Mark 16:17) — to specialists, seminarians, or the charismatic fringe. But the Great Commission does not require a license. It requires a Name, a submitted heart, and an understanding of the authority that has been delegated. Samuel needed someone who knew how to exercise ἐξουσία (ex-oo-SEE-ah), and what he found was a brother willing to weep and wait and declare. The Church must recover this — not as spectacle, but as pastoral care. The second challenge is to the misuse of spiritual vocabulary without spiritual substance. Samuel spoke the Name of Jesus and broke the idol — and the unclean spirit entered him anyway. This is not a failure of the Name; it is a failure of occupied authority. The sons of Sceva in Acts 19:13–16 used the Name of Jesus as a formula and were beaten by the very demon they addressed. The Name is not a spell. It is an identity claim. You must actually be in Christ, actually abiding in the Vine, for the authority to function. The branches that are not abiding do not bear fruit — and they do not cast out demons. The third challenge is to the tendency toward spiritual pride in the aftermath of genuine victories. I want to be careful here with myself. Tonight was extraordinary. But the seventy returned to Jesus rejoicing that demons were subject to them, and He redirected them immediately: *“Do not rejoice in this, that the spirits are subject to you, but rather rejoice because your names are written in heaven”* (Luke 10:20). The victory over Abigail is real. The authority is real. But the ground of my joy must remain the covenant, not the conquest. The fourth challenge is to the instinct to disqualify prayer based on the state of the one praying. I was tipsy. I am not recommending it as a prayer posture. But God’s mercy is not contingent on my perfection. What He requires is a broken and contrite heart — and that was present. The Church must stop gatekeeping intercession based on external qualifications while allowing the posture of the heart to go unexamined. Many sober, well-dressed, properly formatted prayers never move Heaven because the heart behind them is proud and self-sufficient. God resists the proud and gives grace to the humble — James 4:6 — and that night I was about as humble as a man can get, pushing a cat in a dumpster-find stroller and crying out to God. The fifth challenge is to the passive waiting that mistakes inaction for faith. I waited for the confirmation, but the waiting was not passive. I had already declared. I had already bound. I was holding the substance — living in the reality of the answer before it arrived. There is a difference between *waiting in desperation* and *waiting in authority*. The watchman does not panic through the night; he holds his post. That is the posture this account is calling the saints to inhabit: bold declaration followed by sovereign waiting, trusting the character of God to honor the covenant of intercession. A final grace-note: this account invites the Body of Christ to take one another’s afflictions seriously. Samuel had been plagued for weeks, possibly a month. He needed someone who would not spiritualize it away, not dismiss it as psychological struggle, not offer encouragement without authority. He needed an intercessor. Be that for one another. Galatians 6:2 says *“Bear one another’s burdens, and so fulfill the law of Christ.”* Sometimes bearing a burden means weeping before the Father until Heaven moves. ## What This Means For Us **For Spiritual Maturity:** This account calls me — and every believer reading this — into a settled, inhabited understanding of delegated authority. Maturity in Christ is not primarily doctrinal accumulation; it is the deepening embodiment of who we already are in Him. I am learning to live from the inside out — from the authority that was deposited in me at new birth, through the Spirit who dwells in me, outward into the spiritual realities I am called to steward. Spiritual maturity means I no longer ask if God can move. I ask if I am postured to carry the answer. **For Kingdom Obedience:** Kingdom obedience here means taking seriously the commission to intercede, to watch, and to exercise authority over affliction in the lives of our brothers and sisters. It means not waiting for someone more qualified. It means learning the difference between prayer as petition and prayer as declaration — knowing when to ask and when to decree. It means being willing to weep, to wait, and to push a cat in a stroller at midnight if that is what brokenness looks like in that moment. Obedience does not always look impressive. **For Prophetic Posture:** The watchman on the wall does not descend from his post between intercessions. He remains stationed. The prophetic intercessor lives in a continuous posture of listening — to the Father’s heart, to the condition of the people around them, to the movement of the enemy, and to the confirming testimony of Heaven when a decree lands. I know now that I am this. And knowing it is not an arrival; it is a commissioning. The vow I made tonight before my Father is the beginning of a more deliberate stewardship of this office for the rest of my life. ## A Closing Prayer Father — Adonai, LORD of Hosts, the One who has moved Your address into me and made my body the temple of the Living God — I come before You tonight as a son who has witnessed Your faithfulness in a way that has altered the geography of my faith permanently. I thank You that You heard the intercession, that You honored the broken and contrite heart, that You moved the heavens on behalf of my brother Samuel, that Abigail is gone, condemned to the darkness and the fire You have already written for all that opposes Your reign. I stand in the full authority You have given me — ἐξουσία (ex-oo-SEE-ah) over all the power of the enemy — and I renounce every open door, every careless word, every thing I have touched or broken or spoken that would give the enemy legal footing in my atmosphere or in the atmospheres You have given me to steward. I seal this house, this body, this life under the blood of Jesus Christ, Jesus of Nazareth, the Messiah, the Word made flesh, the King of the Universe, the Name above all names. May every unclean thing not of Your Spirit be rebuked by Adonai and condemned to the pit for which it was prepared. And may every movement of Heaven You accomplish through this vessel — through these tears, these prayers, this voice, this broken and willing life — point back to You alone, because You deserve it all. You deserve every syllable of praise, every act of worship, every testimony that multiplies through the brethren. I vow my life to this. I vow my voice to this. In the name of Your Son, Jesus Christ, the King of Kings. Amen. ## Scripture References & Original Language Notes | **Passage (NKJV)** | **Original Language** | **Theological Insight** | |------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------| | Luke 10:19 — “I give you authority… over all the power of the enemy” | ἐξουσία (exousia, ex-oo-SEE-ah) — delegated authority, legal right; δύναμις (dynamis, DOO-nah-mees) — inherent power, force | Authority (ἐξουσία) is given; power (δύναμις) belongs to the enemy but is made subject to delegated authority.
