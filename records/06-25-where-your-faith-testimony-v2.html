# 06-23 — Where Is Your Faith? The Testimony of Commissioned Works **Title:** 06-23 — Where Is Your Faith? The Testimony of Commissioned Works — Brother Samuel’s Testimony **Recorded:** 2026-06-23 (date inferred from “yesterday” relative to content creation date 2026-06-24 10:20:19; exact time not provided) **Source Discernment:** C — Recorded Conversation or Event (Live Capture) **Classification:** Conversation Reflection ## Opening Observation I got off the phone with Brother Samuel and I had something to report — not a complaint, not a question, but a testimony. What I witnessed yesterday was not luck, not hustle, not market timing. It was the faithfulness of God completing a work He already assigned. And underneath the testimony, a question was pressing on me before I even had time to voice it fully: *Where is your faith?* **Why This Matters:** The Body of Christ is drowning in a sea of marketplace strategy, platform dependency, and monetized methodology — and far too many believers in business, ministry, and calling are measuring the possibility of their obedience by what the economy says is required. When a brother is commissioned by God, confirmed in the Body, and given a specific directive from the Lord, and he still says *I didn’t think it would be this easy* — something in the formation of faith is still being completed. That gap between the word received and the expectation held is precisely where the Spirit does His deepest shaping work. This matters because discipleship is not theoretical. It shows up in whether we actually believe what God said when the results arrive. ## The Worldly Mind’s Interpretation The natural mind, hearing this story, would reach immediately for an explanation that flatters human ingenuity or validates conventional wisdom after the fact. It would say: organic content outperforms paid ads when authenticity is present, and the algorithm rewards consistency — Brother Samuel simply stumbled into a growth-hacking principle that marketers have been teaching for years. The natural mind might even reframe the rejected TikTok ads as a fortunate failure that forced a better strategy. It would credit the results to relational sales instinct, to the particular psychology of short-form video in a local market, or to beginner’s luck on a fresh account. And it would gently but firmly suggest that the next step is to systematize what worked, hire someone to scale it, and invest in the infrastructure that sustainable business requires. **The Limitation:** What the natural mind cannot perceive in this moment is that the sequence itself was the word. Four rejections were not a detour — they were the removal of a distraction. The ordinary videos, the 194 views, the two appointments, the two sales, the eight hundred dollars — these were not the output of a strategy. They were the visible confirmation of an invisible commission. The flesh reads results and constructs a method. The Spirit reads the same results and says: *this is what obedience looks like when it trusts the One who sent it.* The mind of the flesh is constitutionally unable to honor the difference between a man who succeeded because he followed God and a man who succeeded because he followed a framework — because to the flesh, the outcome is the same. But to the Spirit, the posture is everything. ## What the Spirit Reveals Through It As I sat with what I heard from Brother Samuel — and then with my own brief, unfinished reflection afterward — the word that kept surfacing was the word the Lord gave through Paul in Philippians 1:6: *“being confident of this very thing, that He who has begun a good work in you will complete it until the day of Jesus Christ.”* This is not a motivational promise. It is a theological declaration rooted in the character of God as the One who *finishes* what He starts. The Greek word translated “complete” or “perform” there is ἐπιτελέω (epiteleō, ep-ee-tel-EH-oh) — to bring fully to its appointed end. God does not initiate and then abandon. He does not commission and then leave the commissioned to figure out the mechanics alone. What I witnessed in Brother Samuel’s testimony was ἐπιτελέω (ep-ee-tel-EH-oh) made visible in real time, twenty-four hours, four rejections, two appointments, two sales. Brother Samuel said he didn’t think it would be this easy. I heard that and something rose up in me — not frustration with him, but a holy recognition of where the Spirit is still forming him. There is a gap between *receiving a word from the Lord* and *expecting the word to perform what it said.* That gap is not unbelief exactly — it is immature faith. It is faith that has not yet been stretched by the discipline of watching God be faithful across enough cycles that expectation catches up to revelation. The writer of Hebrews defines faith in Hebrews 11:1 as “the substance of things hoped for, the evidence of things not seen.” Substance. ὑπόστασις (hypostasis, hoo-POS-tah-sis) — a standing under, a foundation, a title deed. Faith is not emotional optimism. It is the legal and spiritual reality of a promise already secured in the heavenly places, even before it manifests in the earthly timeline. What I had said to Brother Samuel previously — *you don’t need UGC creators, you don’t need ad spend, you don’t need to do what the marketplace economy tells you to do* — was not entrepreneurial wisdom. It was a prophetic reading of what the Lord had already said to him specifically. He had been told to work the business without distraction. He had been told to record videos, be real, be authentic, book appointments, show up, close the sale. That word was not a suggestion. It was a commission. And a commission carries with it the authority and the provision of the One who issues it. Jesus said in Luke 10:19, *“Behold, I give you the authority to trample on serpents and scorpions, and over all the power of the enemy, and nothing shall by any means hurt you.”* He did not say *try this and see if it works.* He said *I give you.* The sending contains the supply. This is precisely the pattern we see in the sending of the twelve in Matthew 10, and again in the seventy in Luke 10. Jesus did not send them with a marketing strategy or a platform presence. He sent them with a word, an authority, and a posture of dependence. He told them not to take extra provisions because the sending itself was the provision. When they returned in Luke 10:17 with joy, saying *“Lord, even the demons are subject to us in Your name,”* Jesus responded with a cosmic declaration about Satan falling like lightning — because their obedience to the commission was not just personal success, it was the movement of the Kingdom. Brother Samuel’s two sales are not a small thing. They are a sign. They are a stone of remembrance. I think about Samuel in the Old Testament — not Brother Samuel, but the prophet — and how the word of the Lord came to him as a child while he was still being formed under Eli. The Lord had to call him three times before Samuel understood who was speaking and how to respond. It was Eli, the aging priest with failing eyes, who finally gave him the instruction: *“Go, lie down; and it shall be, if He calls you, that you must say, ‘Speak, LORD, for Your servant hears’”* (1 Samuel 3:9). There is something in that story that resonates with where Brother Samuel is right now. The word has come. The calling is confirmed. The formation is still happening. Spiritual formation is ongoing — I said that before I ended the call, and I meant it as something worth preserving. You don’t graduate from formation. You graduate *through* it into increasing faithfulness, increasing expectation, increasing correspondence between what God says and what you actually believe will happen. The four rejected TikTok ads are worth pausing on. In the economy of the Spirit, the closed doors were not failures — they were redirections. The word for rejection here, in practical terms, is the same grace we see operating when Paul and his companions were *“forbidden by the Holy Spirit to preach the word in Asia”* in Acts 16:6, and again when *“the Spirit did not permit them”* to go into Bithynia. The repeated closure was not God abandoning the mission. It was God *protecting the mission* by refusing to let the servants exhaust themselves on the wrong pathway. Four rejections on paid ads were four redirections toward the organic, the authentic, the unsponsored — which, as it turned out, is exactly what God had already prescribed. Obedience to the simpler instruction bore immediate fruit. One hundred and ninety-four views. I want to sit with that number for a moment because the natural mind would say that’s a small number. By the standards of a TikTok growth strategy, 194 views on a new account is nothing. You wouldn’t even begin to celebrate until you were in the tens of thousands. But two of those 194 became appointments. Two of those appointments became sales. Eight hundred dollars into the company. That is a conversion rate that no ad spend can buy. This is the arithmetic of the Kingdom — the same arithmetic that feeds five thousand with five loaves and two fish (John 6:9–13), that fills six waterpots to the brim at a wedding in Cana (John 2:6–10), that sends a coin out of a fish’s mouth to pay the temple tax (Matthew 17:27). The Kingdom does not multiply through abundance of resources. It multiplies through abundance of *obedience*. The loaves were small. The obedience to present them was not. I also want to name what Brother Samuel apparently carries as a natural gift — the ability to close, to agree, to bring a conversation to a signed commitment. He has apparently never lost a sale, or at least never failed to reach a mutual agreement. This is not coincidental. Gifts are given, not earned (Romans 11:29 — *“the gifts and the calling of God are irrevocable”*). The Greek word here is ἀμεταμέλητος (ametamelētos, ah-meh-tah-MEL-ay-tos) — without change of mind, without recall. What God gives, He does not take back. The gifting for relational closing, for covenant-level agreement, is not a sales personality trait. It is a Kingdom equipment — an enabling grace suited precisely to the assignment. This is why the question *where is your faith?* is not a rebuke. It is an invitation. It is the same question Jesus asked His disciples repeatedly — after the stilling of the storm (Matthew 8:26), after Peter’s sinking (Matthew 14:31), across the wilderness moments when what had worked before seemed suddenly inaccessible. *Where is your faith?* is not *why don’t you believe?* It is *you have already been given everything you need — have you turned to look at it?* The faithfulness of God is not a concept to be agreed with. It is a reality to be *inhabited*. Brother Samuel is being invited, through this testimony, to close the gap between the word he received and the expectation he carries going forward. And then there is the other thread — the one I barely had time to name before ending the call. I was reflecting on the tool I built, on the frustration of watching something work and then return to unreliability. I said something I want to sit with: *the more I use it, the more other people use it, the closer it’ll be to what the Spirit has led me to build.* And then: *I’ll leave it in His hands.* There is a theology in that final line that is not resignation. It is stewardship. I am called to build. I am not called to perfect. Perfection is the work of the One who ἐπιτελέω (ep-ee-tel-EH-oh) — who brings to completion. The builder’s role is faithfulness and iteration. The Spirit’s role is the finishing. Every tool I construct for Kingdom use is an act of co-laboring with God, and co-laboring means I bring what I have, I offer what I can make, and I trust the One who superintends the process to bring the work to its appointed end. This is not passivity. This is the mature posture of a servant who knows where his authority ends and where God’s faithfulness begins. Spiritual formation is still going on in Samuel. That sentence — my last note before I closed the call — is the frame for everything. Formation is the long work. The testimony of one day’s faithfulness is not the end of the story. It is a stone of remembrance placed in the riverbed so that the next generation — and Brother Samuel himself, the next time doubt whispers — can point to it and say: *the Lord did this. On this day. Through obedience to a word He had already spoken.* Let it be written. Let it be witnessed. Let it be carried. ## Implications and Challenges The modern Western church has largely inherited a theology of provision that is indistinguishable from the theology of the marketplace. Success is expected to require infrastructure, capital, platform, and proven methodology. When someone succeeds without those things, we call it anomalous or attribute it to a fortunate season rather than recognizing it as the fruit of obedience to a commission. Brother Samuel’s story is a gentle but direct challenge to that assumption. The question is not *what did the market reward?* The question is *what did God say, and was it obeyed?* There is also a challenge here to the tendency to spiritualize the word and then naturalize the implementation. Many believers will receive a prophetic word or a sense of calling, sincerely believe it, and then immediately outsource the *how* to conventional wisdom. They believe the destination is from God but assume the path must follow the world’s road. Brother Samuel was told specifically: record videos, be real, book the appointments, show up, close the sale. The simplicity of that instruction is itself a gift. Complexity is often a form of unbelief dressed in diligence. The frustration I named about the tool I built — watching something work, trusting it, and then experiencing its failure in a later moment — is something many in the Body carry in their callings. We were faithful. It worked. We trusted. And then it didn’t hold the way we expected. The temptation is to over-correct, to over-engineer, to try to build something that never misfires. But the only thing that never misfires is the Lord Himself. Every instrument we build, every system we create, every structure we raise in His name will require ongoing iteration, ongoing surrender, and ongoing trust. The goal is not a perfect tool. The goal is a faithful servant who keeps building and keeps releasing it to the One who completes. The phrase *where is your faith?* also confronts the emotional rhythm many believers mistake for spiritual health. When things go well, they feel faith. When things don’t go as expected, they lose it. But biblical faith is not a feeling attached to outcomes. It is a substance — a ὑπόστασις (hoo-POS-tah-sis) — a settled foundation that does not shift with the weather of circumstance. The formation Brother Samuel is undergoing is the formation from *outcome-dependent confidence* to *commission-grounded certainty.* That is not a small formation. It is the work of years, and it is ongoing. Finally, there is a grace in naming that the testimony is not yet complete. Spiritual formation is still going on. This is a word for the whole Body. We are not waiting for some of our brothers and sisters to finish being formed before we celebrate what God is doing in them. We celebrate the work *in process* because the One who began it is faithful to complete it. The stone of remembrance is placed *now* — not when the story is over, but when the Lord moves — so that the ongoing formation has a foundation of memorial to stand on. ## What This Means For Us **For Spiritual Maturity:** Closing the gap between the word received and the expectation held is the work of maturing faith. Every stone of remembrance laid — every testimony witnessed, recorded, and carried — builds the foundation from which the next level of trust is possible. We grow in faith not by working harder to believe but by rehearsing faithfully what God has already done and letting the memory of His acts become the soil of our expectation. **For Kingdom Obedience:** Obedience to a simple, specific word from the Lord is more powerful than any strategy, any platform, any ad spend. The commission carries the provision. When God assigns a work, He also equips for it, opens the pathway for it, and closes the distractions that would divert from it. Our part is to hear, obey, and show up — and to resist the temptation to improve on the instructions. **For Prophetic Posture:** We are called to be witnesses. To watch what God is doing in one another. To speak what we see. To record what He accomplishes. And to hold steady when the tool we’ve built, the ministry we’ve stewarded, the brother we’ve walked with shows signs of incompleteness — knowing that incompleteness is not failure. It is formation still in motion. We release it into His hands and we keep building, keep watching, keep reporting. ## A Closing Prayer Father, we come before You as witnesses — those who have watched You move in the lives of the ones You have commissioned and sent. We thank You for Brother Samuel, for the four closed doors that were four mercies, for the 194 views that became two appointments that became two sales and eight hundred dollars of confirmation that You complete what You begin. We thank You that formation is not finished, that You are not rushing it, and that every stone of remembrance we place today becomes the bedrock of the faith our brothers and sisters will stand on tomorrow. We ask that where faith has not yet caught up to revelation, Your Spirit would close that gap — not with pressure but with encounter, not with rebuke but with remembrance. We ask the same for every tool built in Your name, every system offered for Your use — that what we release into Your hands, You would carry to its appointed completion, that the misfires would not discourage but instruct, and that the iteration would be fellowship with You rather than frustration with ourselves. You are the One who began every good work. You are the One who ἐπιτελέω — who brings it fully to its end. We trust that. We rest in that. We leave it in Your hands and we give You all the glory.
