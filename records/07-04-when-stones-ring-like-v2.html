# 07-04 Teaching Reflection: Ancient Energy, Hidden History, and Biblical Discernment **Title:** 07-04 at 09:59 — When the Stones Ring Like Bells: Ancient Energy, Hidden History, and the Question of Who We Truly Are **Recorded:** 2026-07-04 09:59:58 **Source Discernment:** E — Recorded Sermon, Video, or Online Reel + Reflection **Classification:** Teaching Reflection ## Opening Observation I have been sitting with a teaching that moves fast and wide — from the stone circles of southern Africa to the towers of Bologna, from cymatics to cathedrals, from The Matrix to the Sumerian tablets — and something in me wants to press pause, not to dismiss it, but to ask the harder question underneath all of it. The speaker is circling something real: that human beings have been profoundly deceived about who they are, where they come from, and what the world around them actually is. That question — *who are we, really, and what have we been told about ourselves that is simply not true?* — is not a fringe question. It is, at its root, a deeply biblical one, and I find myself unwilling to let it pass without bringing it before the Word. **Why This Matters:** The stakes of this conversation are not academic. If the history we have inherited is distorted — if institutions have systematically suppressed the depth of human antiquity, the sophistication of ancient civilizations, and the nature of the world’s most mysterious structures — then what we believe about ourselves has been shaped by a narrative we never chose and never questioned. That has profound consequences for how we understand human dignity, human origin, and human purpose. But here is where I must slow down and be careful, because the answer to a corrupted narrative is not simply a *different* narrative — it is *truth*. And truth, the Scripture insists, is not merely a better information system. Truth is a Person. Jesus said, *“I am the way, the truth, and the life”* (John 14:6, NKJV). So while I find the questions this teaching raises genuinely compelling, I want to bring those questions into the light of the One who does not merely explain reality but *is* the ground of it. ## The Worldly Mind’s Interpretation The natural mind receives this teaching with a mixture of fascination and vertigo. It feels like the floor dropping out from under a worldview — all the comfortable certainties of mainstream history, archaeology, and physics suddenly looking like a stage set with a hidden backstage. The psychological pull is real and understandable: the desire to *finally know* what has been hidden, to be among those who have woken up while others are still asleep, to locate the reason for the disorientation of modern life in a vast and coordinated suppression. The worldly mind, even when it is sincerely searching, reaches for a framework that makes sense of the chaos — and alternative history, ancient energy grids, and suppressed civilizations offer exactly that: a new unified theory of everything. The speaker is articulate, the imagery is striking, and the emotional register is one of revelation. For someone genuinely hungry for meaning, this can feel like the answer to a prayer. **The Limitation:** But there is a hunger beneath this hunger that the lecture itself cannot satisfy. To know that ancient stones ring like bells, that silica conducts sound and light, that stone circles may have been acoustic energy devices — even if all of that is precisely true — does not tell us *why* we are here, *who* made us, or *what* we owe to the One in whom all things, including every ancient stone and every lost civilization, were created. Paul writes that creation itself declares the invisible attributes of God — His eternal power and divine nature — and that humanity is therefore without excuse when it suppresses that testimony (Romans 1:20). The tragedy is not merely that history has been hidden; the tragedy is that even recovered history, without the fear of the Lord as its beginning, becomes another layer of the same blindness. The natural mind can catalogue the mystery without ever kneeling before the Maker of the mystery. What the Spirit reveals through this material is something more foundational than ancient energy technology — it is the testimony that God has written His fingerprints into the very fabric of the earth He formed, and that no suppression, ancient or modern, can ultimately erase them. ## What the Spirit Reveals Through It As I sit with this teaching, the first thing that arrests me is its opening and closing image: Morpheus holding up the battery, declaring, *“We are the source of energy.”* The speaker returns to this image at the end, and I notice that it lands not as a disturbing claim but as a liberating one — as though finally understanding that we are being harvested is somehow the beginning of freedom. But I want to press that image against the Scripture, because the Bible has something to say about humanity as a *source* that is far older and far deeper than any science fiction film. In Genesis 2:7, God breathes into the nostrils of the man He has formed from dust, and the man becomes נֶפֶשׁ חַיָּה (nephesh chayyah, NEH-fesh khah-YAH) — a living soul. We are not batteries. We are breath-bearers. The energy the speaker describes — the sound, the resonance, the frequency — finds its true origin not in a suppressed physics of silica magnetrons but in the *ruach* of the Almighty breathed into mortal clay. That is the deepest truth about human energy: it is borrowed breath, sustained by the One who gave it. I notice the speaker moves quickly through what he identifies as the central wound of our age: that institutions — educational, governmental, media — have consistently told us a story about ourselves designed to keep us small, confused, and dependent. And I cannot dismiss that critique. The Scripture does not dismiss it either. Paul describes the god of this age as one who blinds the minds of those who do not believe (2 Corinthians 4:4), and Revelation speaks plainly of nations that have been deceived (Revelation 18:23). Deception at a civilizational scale is not a conspiracy theory — it is eschatological reality. The question is not *whether* the world’s dominant narratives have been shaped by powers hostile to truth. The question is *which* truth do we run to when we discover the deception? The Tartarian Empire, the towers of Bologna, the stone circles of southern Africa — I find myself genuinely curious about these things, and I do not think curiosity is sin. Proverbs 25:2 says, *“It is the glory of God to conceal a matter, but the glory of kings is to search out a matter.”* We are a kingly people — a royal priesthood, Peter says (1 Peter 2:9) — and the searching out of what has been hidden is not foreign to the Spirit-led life. But I am also aware that the searching must be anchored. Without the anchor of the Word, recovered mysteries can become new idols. The Athenians in Acts 17 were among the most sophisticated searchers in the ancient world, spending their time in nothing else but telling or hearing some new thing — and Paul did not mock their curiosity. He redirected it. He pointed them to the God who made from one blood every nation of men, who determined their appointed times and the boundaries of their dwelling, so that they should seek the Lord (Acts 17:26–27). What strikes me about Adam’s Calendar, the heat signatures, the electromagnetic readings — the 375 gigahertz sound measurements that the speaker says have never been recorded anywhere else on earth — is that they point to something embedded in the ground itself, something that cannot be fully explained by current science, something that *exceeds* our frameworks. And the Spirit whispers to me: *of course*. The earth was spoken into existence by a God whose word carries frequency, whose breath is wind and fire (Psalm 104:4), whose voice shook Sinai (Hebrews 12:26) and will shake the heavens and the earth once more. If God *spoke* creation into being — and the Scripture declares He did — then the idea that creation carries resonance, frequency, sound, and memory is not pagan mysticism. It is the natural consequence of a universe created by the speaking Word. John 1:3 is not merely theological poetry: *“All things were made through Him, and without Him nothing was made that was made.”* If sound is foundational to the architecture of the ancient world, that is a testimony to the Word through whom all things were made, not merely to the wisdom of ancient engineers. I find myself returning to the speaker’s observation about cymatics — that each stone circle represents the cymatic pattern of a specific sound frequency at a specific location on earth. He describes them as “plug points” into the energy of the earth itself. And while I hold that specific claim loosely, the image it generates in me is genuinely biblical. The Scripture describes the earth as full of the glory of God (Isaiah 6:3), as declaring His handiwork night and day (Psalm 19:1–2), as crying out under oppression (Genesis 4:10) and longing for the revelation of the sons of God (Romans 8:19–22). Creation is not inert. Creation is *vocal*. The stones themselves, Jesus said, would cry out if His people fell silent (Luke 19:40). If ancient peoples understood that the earth resonates, that certain frequencies amplify at certain geographic nodes, that structures can be built to channel and focus those frequencies — perhaps they were not primitive at all. Perhaps they were listening more carefully to what the Maker had written into the ground than we have allowed ourselves to believe. The Sumerian text the speaker quotes — from the Schoyen Collection, not a Sitchin translation — is interesting to me for a different reason than the speaker finds it interesting. He reads it as evidence of an energetic grid mirrored from the sky. I read it and hear something else: the echo of a pre-Flood world that had access to knowledge now lost, that heard something about the ordering of heaven and earth and attempted to structure civilization around it — and that ultimately, in its pride, reached for what belonged only to God. Genesis 11 is the Scripture’s account of what organized human intelligence applied to the manipulation of heaven and earth looks like when it is untethered from the fear of the Lord. *“Come, let us build ourselves a city, and a tower whose top is in the heavens; let us make a name for ourselves”* (Genesis 11:4). The sophistication is real. The ambition is real. And God Himself said, *“Nothing that they propose to do will be withheld from them”* (Genesis 11:6). That is not admiration. That is a warning. The speaker’s observation about cathedrals — that they are acoustic chambers, that their windows are magnetron-shaped, that their spires and domes focus and transmit frequency — is one I want to hold carefully. There is something true in the observation that sacred architecture in every tradition has been built to do something to the human body in a space, to create an environment that alters the interior state of the person within it. And the Christian tradition has not been innocent of borrowing the architectural language of empire and power in ways that have served the institution more than the Lord. But I also notice that the speaker slides past something significant: the medieval cathedral builders were, by their own testimony, building for the glory of God. Were their structures also doing something with sound and light? Perhaps. But the question the Spirit presses me toward is not *what does the building do with frequency?* It is *who are the people in it worshiping, and is that worship in spirit and in truth?* (John 4:23–24). A building can be acoustically perfect and spiritually dead. There is a moment late in the teaching when the speaker says plainly: *“We are just like literally lambs to the slaughter. We’re just being lied to, abused, eaten, slaughtered, tortured.”* And something in me grieves — not because the statement is entirely without foundation, but because of the spirit of it. There is a profound difference between the revelation that we have been deceived and the revelation that we are victims. The Scripture speaks of a people who have been misled — but also of a Shepherd who lays down His life for those sheep (John 10:11), of a Redeemer who purchases back what the thief came to steal (John 10:10), of a King whose kingdom cannot be shaken even when every earthly kingdom is exposed and dismantled (Hebrews 12:28). The answer to a world that has lied to us about who we are is not a better conspiracy theory. It is the testimony of the One who said, *“You shall know the truth, and the truth shall make you free”* (John 8:32). What this teaching cannot give — and what I feel the Spirit pressing me to name clearly — is the *telos*, the purpose, the *why*. The speaker admits it openly more than once: *“For what, that remains the mystery.”* The energy grids, the stone circles, the ancient machines — even if every measurement and every claim were perfectly accurate — still leave us standing before an open question. Why were human beings made? What is the purpose of our sound, our presence, our breath? And the Scripture does not leave that question unanswered. We were made for fellowship with God (Genesis 1:26–28), for the priestly stewardship of His creation (Genesis 2:15), for the worship that aligns our frequency — if we may borrow that language — with His own glory. The great tragedy is not merely that ancient history was suppressed. It is that the knowledge of God Himself has been suppressed — and that suppression is older than any fallen empire. It began in a garden, with the oldest deception: *“You will be like God”* (Genesis 3:5). I am left, finally, with this: the questions this teaching raises deserve serious engagement, not dismissal. Ancient civilizations were far more sophisticated than the dominant academic consensus has been willing to admit. The earth does carry properties that current science does not fully understand. History has been shaped by powers with interests in keeping populations confused and dependent. The Spirit-led believer should be the sharpest discerner in the room — not credulous, not dismissive, but genuinely searching, genuinely anchored. And the anchor is not a better alternative history. The anchor is the Word made flesh, the One through whom all things were created, who holds all things together (Colossians 1:16–17), and who has promised that the hidden things of darkness will be brought to light (1 Corinthians 4:5). I do not need ancient energy grids to tell me the world is stranger and deeper than I was taught. I have the Book of Job, the Psalms of Ascent, the Revelation of John. I have a God whose ways are past finding out (Romans 11:33) — and who has invited me, nonetheless, to seek and to find. ## Implications and Challenges The first challenge this material places before us is the challenge of *credulity dressed as awakening*. There is a particular spiritual danger in the moment when someone believes they have finally seen through the deception — because in that moment, the hunger to believe the newly offered narrative can be every bit as uncritical as the hunger that accepted the old one. The Spirit calls us to test all things and hold fast what is good (1 Thessalonians 5:21). That testing applies not only to mainstream institutions but equally to alternative ones. The fact that an institution lied to you does not make its opponent automatically trustworthy. The second challenge is the absence of the Creator in a teaching that is ostensibly about creation. The speaker moves through megalithic structures, resonant stones, ancient energy grids, and Sumerian cosmology without once pausing to ask *who* designed the acoustic properties of silica, *who* ordered the energy nodal points of the earth, *who* wrote the fixed rules encoded in the sky. Creation is treated as a self-organizing system of energy rather than the intentional handiwork of a personal God. This is not a small omission. Romans 1 calls it suppression — and the result, Paul warns, is not enlightenment but a darkened heart (Romans 1:21). A third challenge is the reduction of human beings to energy sources. The speaker begins and ends with the Matrix battery image, and while he intends it as an exposure of exploitation, he never offers an alternative account of human worth and dignity. The Scripture does. Human beings are not batteries, not energy nodes, not sound-generating machines in a planetary grid. We are image-bearers of the living God (Genesis 1:26), purchased with blood (1 Corinthians 6:20), destined for resurrection and co-reign with Christ (Revelation 20:6). The difference between those two accounts of humanity is not incidental. It is the difference between a world from which you need to escape and a world for which you are called to intercede. A fourth challenge is the implicit hierarchy of knowledge this teaching creates. Those who understand the energy grids and the acoustic technology and the suppressed history are the awakened; everyone else is a lamb to the slaughter. But the Scripture does not offer hidden gnosis as the path to freedom. Jesus said, *“I am the door”* (John 10:9) — not the hidden code, not the recovered ancient technology, not the correct interpretation of cymatics. The truly awakened community is not the one with the most alternative information; it is the one that has been genuinely transformed by the Spirit of truth into the likeness of the Son. Finally, there is the challenge of what we *do* with recovered history and suppressed knowledge once we have it. The speaker never quite gets there. He exposes, he speculates, he marvels — and then the lecture ends without a call to action, without an ethic, without a community formed around the truth. The Kingdom of God does not work that way. The testimony of truth is not for private fascination; it is for communal transformation. *“You shall know the truth”* — that is personal — *“and the truth shall make you free”* (John 8:32) — that is communal, covenantal, and costly. ## What This Means For Us **For Spiritual Maturity:** Spiritual maturity does not mean refusing to engage difficult questions about history, archaeology, or the hidden structures of the world. It means bringing every question before the Lord with an open hand — willing to receive what is true, willing to release what is speculation, and willing to remain rooted in the Word even when the ground beneath familiar certainties begins to shift. The mature believer can sit with mystery without being destabilized by it, because their identity is not anchored in what they know but in Whom they know. **For Kingdom Obedience:** The Kingdom call is to be the sharpest, most discerning, most truth-loving people on earth — not credulous consumers of alternative narratives, but neither defenders of institutional deceptions. We are called to speak what is true in love (Ephesians 4:15), to expose what is hidden (Ephesians 5:11–13), and to do it with the sobriety and patience of those who know that the final exposure belongs to the returning King. Our obedience is not to the energy grid. It is to the God who made the earth that grid runs through. **For Prophetic Posture:** The prophetic community in this hour must be fluent in the questions the world is asking — about suppressed history, hidden powers, human dignity, and the meaning of existence — and must be prepared to speak the answer the world cannot find on its own. That answer is not an alternative documentary. It is the testimony of Jesus Christ, the Living Word, who is the same yesterday, today, and forever (Hebrews 13:8) — who was present at the founding of those ancient stone circles, who hears the frequencies the earth still emits, and who is returning to make all hidden things plain. ## A Closing Prayer Father, I come before You as one who stands between heaven and earth, as a servant of the Word who has heard Your prophets and who carries the testimony of Jesus in a generation that is waking — sometimes to truth, sometimes to a more sophisticated form of the same old darkness. I thank You that You are not disturbed by suppressed history, by hidden empires, or by the energy grids embedded in the ground You spoke into being. You made the stones, and You know what they carry. I ask You now to give Your people clarity: the discernment to engage hard questions without being swept into them, the courage to name You as the Creator when every conversation circles around creation without finding You, and the love to speak truth to those who are genuinely hungry but are being fed on information alone. Let the sound that rises from Your people be the sound of worship — the one frequency that aligns all of creation with its true purpose — and let that worship ascend not through magnetrons and spires but through hearts transformed by the Spirit and lives poured out for the glory of Your name. You are the Lord of the ancient stones and the modern cities and the generations yet to come. May Your Kingdom come, and may Your truth — which no institution can suppress and no empire can bury — be heard clearly in this hour. In the name of Jesus Christ, the Word through whom all things were made, Amen. ## Scripture References & Original Language Notes | **Passage (NKJV)** | **Original Language** | **Theological Insight** | |----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------| | Genesis 2:7 — “And the LORD God formed man of the dust of the ground, and breathed into his nostrils the breath of life; and man became a living being.” | נֶפֶשׁ חַיָּה (nephesh chayyah, NEH-fesh khah-YAH) — living soul / breathing creature | Humanity’s “energy” is divine breath, not merely biological or acoustic resonance.
