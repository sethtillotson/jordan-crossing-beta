# 07-10 Call Reflection: Intercession, Demonic Deliverance, and Kingdom Legal Authority **Title:** 07-10 at 21:30 — When the Heavens Moved: Intercession, Demonic Deliverance, and the Legality of the Kingdom **Recorded:** 2026-07-10 21:30:22 **Source Discernment:** C — Recorded Conversation or Event (Live Capture) **Classification:** Call Reflection ## Opening Observation Tonight I called my father because I could not hold it alone — something had broken open in the Spirit that was too large for silence. I had just witnessed, through intercession and specific covenantal prayer, the departure of an unclean spirit that had been afflicting my brother Samuel for weeks, and the confirmation of its departure came from Samuel’s own mouth the moment he rang me back. What undoes me most is not the dramatic nature of what occurred, but the *legality* of it — the way the Kingdom of God operates not through emotional intensity or ritual performance, but through the precise application of covenantal understanding. I am still trembling as I write this, and I want to be careful to say what is true and only what is true. **Why This Matters:** The Body of Christ in the Western world has largely inherited one of two dysfunctions regarding spiritual warfare: either a fearful denial that the demonic is operationally present in daily life, or an emotionally charged, theatrically performed “deliverance” culture that substitutes spiritual volume for spiritual authority. Neither posture is anchored in the actual mechanics of Kingdom governance as Jesus demonstrated and commissioned. What happened tonight — the intercession, the naming, the waiting, the specific covenantal prayer, the confirmation — is not extraordinary in the sense that it was invented; it is extraordinary only in the sense that it has been *forgotten*. The saints need to recover what it means to operate under the exousia of Jesus Christ in sober, grounded, scripturally informed authority. Tonight, the Lord let me glimpse that — and it has relit something in me that had grown dim. ## The Worldly Mind’s Interpretation The natural mind, encountering this account, will reach for the nearest available explanatory category: psychological projection, a friend’s mental health crisis, the suggestive power of shared belief, perhaps a dissociative episode amplified by alcohol. The worldly framework is not entirely without compassion — it will want to care for Samuel, it will want to protect him — but it will do so by reducing his experience to a problem of chemistry or cognition, and it will regard my interpretation of events as at best a sincere but mistaken overlay of religious narrative onto a naturalistic incident. From inside that framework, what I describe as an unclean spirit named Abigail is merely a label applied to erratic behavior, and what I describe as intercession and deliverance is merely the coincidental timing of a phone call that happened to find my friend in a calmer state. **The Limitation:** The difficulty is not that the natural mind is unintelligent — it is that the invisible architecture of reality is precisely invisible to it. The scriptural witness is unanimous that there exists a realm of spiritual agency, both holy and corrupted, that operates with genuine causative power in the material world. A mind formed entirely by empirical categories will always flatten this testimony into metaphor, because it has no faculty for perceiving it. But the inability to perceive a thing is not the same as that thing’s absence. The disciples on the water did not produce wind by believing in wind; the wind was already there. Tonight, the spirit called Abigail was already there — in that house, in that territory, operating with a consistency and an intelligence that no psychological model for erratic behavior could adequately account for. I am not reporting a feeling. I am reporting a sequence of specific, verifiable events, and I am doing so before the God who witnessed them. ## What the Spirit Reveals Through It I want to begin with the thing the Spirit pressed into me weeks before tonight — because nothing about this evening was disconnected from what came before. I had been sitting in Proverbs 23, specifically in the closing verses, those haunting lines about the drunkard who says *“They have struck me, but I was not hurt; they have beaten me, but I did not feel it. When shall I awake, that I may seek another drink?”* (Proverbs 23:35, NKJV). The surface reading is plain enough — it is a portrait of addiction’s deadening work, the progressive anesthesia that alcohol performs on the conscience and the body until a man cannot feel what is destroying him. I received that. But the Spirit did not stop there. He pressed me toward Mark chapter eight, and as I read that passage again, something cracked open. Jesus is on the boat with the disciples after the feeding of the five thousand. He rebukes them concerning the leaven of the Pharisees and the leaven of Herod. And Mark — only Mark, with that particular precision that characterizes his Gospel — adds this: *“they did not understand”* (Mark 8:21, NKJV) *“because their heart was hardened”* (Mark 6:52, NKJV). The hardening of the heart produces the same symptom as the Proverbs drunkard — a man can be struck, corrected, redirected, loved, and he does not feel it. Not because he is consciously resistant, but because something in him has become *calloused* to the movement of the Spirit. I began to see that Samuel’s pattern — the right track, then the inexplicable pivot away from everything sound — was not a personality defect. It was the signature of something that had gotten in through an unguarded door and was performing exactly this hardening function. The door, as I understand it, opened the night Samuel went to his grandfather’s house to clear out the pagan objects his grandfather’s widow had brought in — the idols, the objects of foreign devotion. His intention was righteous: he wanted to cleanse the space. But he entered presuming upon authority he had not yet learned to *inhabit* with sobriety. He had been drinking. And this is the thing I now understand with a clarity I did not have before: the influence of substances that alter consciousness does not merely impair judgment in a psychological sense. It *reduces the perimeter* of a man’s spiritual covering. The New Testament is not a document of rigid abstinence regarding wine — Jesus himself turned water to wine at Cana and drank with his disciples. But the boundary between liberty and vulnerability is the abiding. When the inward man is fully clothed in the identity given by the Father through the New Covenant, a man may drink wine and remain himself. When that inward man is still being formed, still learning to stand in his inheritance, the intoxication of the body can become an open seam in the spiritual architecture of a person, and something can walk through it. The Greek word that keeps surfacing in my spirit here is ἐξουσία (exousia, ex-OO-see-ah) — authority, the right and power to act within a defined jurisdiction. Jesus gave this to his disciples: *“I give you the authority to trample on serpents and scorpions, and over all the power of the enemy”* (Luke 10:19, NKJV). But ἐξουσία (ex-OO-see-ah) is not a sentiment. It is a *legal standing*. It operates the way a deed of title operates — you do not gain access to a property simply by wanting it or feeling strongly about it. You gain access because your name is on the document and the document has been executed in the proper court. Samuel entered that house with a feeling of authority rather than a grounded understanding of it, and the spirit that was present in that territory recognized the difference. What followed over the next several weeks was something I can now identify as the work of what I would call a consistent counterfeit spirit — a spiritual entity whose specific method is mimicry. It does not announce itself as opposition. It whispers in the cadence of wisdom, it speaks in the approximate frequency of the voice of God, and it uses that mimicry to redirect a person away from what the true voice of God had already established. This is precisely what Jesus warned in John 10: the sheep hear *His* voice, and a stranger they will not follow — *“for they do not know the voice of strangers”* (John 10:5, NKJV). Samuel had genuinely heard from the Lord. I know this because I was present in the hours after that original encounter. But the problem with learning to hear a voice is that hearing must be cultivated alongside *testing*, and the counterfeit is most dangerous in the period immediately after a genuine encounter, when a person’s confidence in hearing is established but their discernment of *which* voice is still being sharpened. The parallel to Mark 8 became almost surgical tonight. In that chapter, after the boat and the leaven, Jesus asks: *“Do you not yet perceive nor understand? Is your heart still hardened? Having eyes, do you not see? And having ears, do you not hear?”* (Mark 8:17–18, NKJV). There is no condemnation in the question — there is the ache of a teacher whose students are standing in the middle of a miracle they have already witnessed and cannot yet receive. That is where Samuel has been. Not in rebellion. In a hardening that something from outside him had been actively cultivating. And the manifestation of that hardening — the pivot from a sound business with clear vocational theology into propositions about gray market activity that were completely discontinuous with everything he had built — was the demonic equivalent of the drunkard saying *“when shall I awake that I may seek another drink?”* He could not remember what he had said. He could not account for the messages he had sent. This is not behavioral inconsistency. This is possession functioning exactly as the scriptural record describes it. When it spoke to me directly — first two days before tonight, telling me it was here to kill me, and then tonight from inside Samuel’s voice — something in me settled into a quietness I did not expect. The reason I could receive the threat without fear is not because I am unusually courageous. It is because I know what the Resurrection means legally. *“O Death, where is your sting? O Hades, where is your victory?”* (1 Corinthians 15:55, NKJV). I live in the shelter of the One who has already conquered death, and a spirit that threatens to kill me is threatening something that has already been sanctified for eternity. I told it as much: I live forever. Good luck. This is not bravado. It is the logical consequence of a settled New Covenant identity. What I did not do — and this matters — is attempt to command the spirit myself through force of will or accumulated religious vocabulary. I tried that once before, with a different situation, for half an hour, using Hebrew, Greek, tongues, every scriptural formula I knew, and I produced nothing. The sons of Sceva come to mind: *“Jesus I know, and Paul I know; but who are you?”* (Acts 19:15, NKJV). They had the words. They did not have the standing. The standing is not achieved through ritual or language. It is received through abiding. Tonight I went to my knees — head on the floor, weeping, interceding for Samuel — not because that posture is a formula, but because that posture is *true*. I was a man who could not deliver his brother through his own power asking the Father who could. And I made it specific, because I have learned that my God is a God of specificity. I said: when Samuel calls me again, Abigail will be gone. That is not a demand I made of God. It is an agreement I made *with* Him, from within the understanding of how intercession functions — as a co-laboring between the servant and the Lord, prayer that participates in the execution of a will that has already been decided in the heavens. Within the hour, Samuel called. The first words out of his mouth: *Abigail’s gone.* He named her himself — the name he had never been able to tell me while she was present because consciousness was eclipsed when she was speaking. Now he was back, present, himself, and she was absent. I want to sit in that for a moment without rushing past it, because the temptation is to move quickly from the testimony to the theology, and I do not want to do that. The heavens *moved* tonight. Not metaphorically. The Father executed a specific judgment on a specific unclean spirit in response to specific intercession rooted in covenantal understanding. This is the Kingdom of God functioning as it was always designed to function, as Jesus demonstrated it would function, as He commissioned his disciples to demonstrate it would function. I am not the hero of this story. I am, at best, the steward who finally understood what deed he was holding. And then — because God is never merely managing crises but is always also building something — Samuel delivered a prophetic word from the Lord to me in the moments before Abigail surfaced. A genuine word, spoken with the clarity of a man who was briefly and fully himself. It was a word about the corpus of writing I have been building, about its readiness for release, about timing. I receive that with sobriety, not with hype. But I do receive it, because the vessel that carried it was confirmed by what happened immediately after — a spirit attempting to drown out what had just been spoken by stepping forward and mimicking the voice it had just heard speaking life. The theological thread I cannot let go of tonight is the connection between the Jonah narrative and what I shared with my father at the end of the call. Jonah washes up on the shore of Nineveh — a city whose patron deity was a fish-man hybrid. When a man emerges from the belly of a great fish onto that shore, the Ninevites do not see a stranger. They see their own mythology walking. And that mythology kneels and says: the God of Israel told me to come here and tell you to repent. God used the very idolatrous framework of a pagan people as the visual grammar through which the true word of the Lord could land. And those people repented — *all of them* — because five hundred miles away and a generation earlier, the plagues of Egypt and the parting of the Red Sea had already carved the name of the God of Israel into the cultural memory of the ancient Near East. Pharaoh’s hardened heart, which from inside the story looks like nothing but devastation and judgment, had been functioning all along as proclamation. *“For this very purpose I have raised you up, that I may show My power in you, and that My name may be declared in all the earth”* (Romans 9:17, NKJV, citing Exodus 9:16). The sovereignty of God does not override human will — it *uses* it, in both its yielded and its hardened forms, to accomplish the purposes of the Kingdom. That is the same principle at work in Samuel’s trial. The weeks of affliction, the maddening pivots, the disrupted business, the drinking, the possession — none of it was wasted. It became the laboratory in which I was being trained to understand the legality of the Kingdom with enough precision to actually exercise it. The enemy’s attempt to destroy became the terrain on which my ἐξουσία (ex-OO-see-ah) was being road-tested. And tonight, it held. ## Implications and Challenges There is a confrontation embedded in tonight’s events for those of us who have grown comfortable with a Christianity that is theologically articulate but practically inert. We have developed the vocabulary of spiritual warfare without developing the standing from which that warfare is actually waged. We speak of authority in Christ while functioning, in practice, as though the demonic realm is either absent or inaccessible to the ordinary believer. Tonight challenges that posture directly, not through spectacle but through specificity: a man on his knees, head on the floor, praying with covenantal precision, waiting for the Father to confirm what had been entrusted to him. That is not extreme. That is the baseline the New Testament describes. The charismatic and Pentecostal traditions have, by and large, preserved the category of deliverance ministry — but they have sometimes done so at the cost of sobriety, allowing the dramatic to become performative, the authoritative to become theatrical. The shouts, the religious formulas, the prolonged rituals — I tried those. They produced nothing. What produced something was *surrender into specificity*: I do not know the details of what you will do with this spirit, Father, but I know you, and I know when Samuel calls me next, she will be gone. That kind of prayer requires a settled theological identity, not an emotional peak. At the same time, the more Reformed and cessationist corners of the Western Church carry their own limitation — the assumption that the gifts of the Spirit and the visible operations of the Kingdom were scaffolding for the apostolic age, now removed. But the commission of Luke 10 was not issued to a committee of apostles. It was issued to seventy-two ordinary people who returned astonished that *“even the demons are subject to us in Your name”* (Luke 10:17, NKJV), and Jesus replied not by tempering their amazement but by expanding it: *“I saw Satan fall like lightning from heaven”* (Luke 10:18, NKJV). The Lord was not surprised. He had already seen this coming. There is also a word here about the vulnerability created by unguarded entry into spiritually contaminated spaces. This is not superstition — it is the consistent testimony of the canon from the Mosaic warnings against the abominations of Canaan through the New Testament’s consistent instruction to *test the spirits* (1 John 4:1, NKJV). Samuel’s error was not in wanting to cleanse the house. It was in entering with presumption rather than with preparation — impaired, untested, performing a spiritual act with the authority of a title he had not yet learned to inhabit. The grace of God is that this error became a teacher rather than a sentence. Finally, what tonight reveals about intercession itself cannot be left unstated. My intercession for Samuel was not merely sentimental prayer for a friend in difficulty. It was priestly, covenantal, and specific — the kind of prayer that stands in the gap not because the intercessor is righteous in himself, but because he understands the legal reality of the New Covenant and makes his agreement with what has already been decided in the heavens. This is the intercession that Ezekiel mourned the absence of (Ezekiel 22:30, NKJV). It is the intercession that moves things. It is available to every believer who will learn to stand in their inheritance rather than merely admire it from a distance. ## What This Means For Us **For Spiritual Maturity:** Tonight is an invitation to move from theoretical theology to operational faith — to stop treating the commission of Luke 10 as historical record and start inhabiting it as living covenant. Spiritual maturity in this context means learning the difference between religious performance and covenantal authority, understanding that the latter is not generated by volume or vocabulary but by abiding. It also means being willing to sit with weeks of confusion, maddening patterns, unanswered questions — and not abandoning the post. I watched Samuel for a month. I stayed in the conversation. Maturity does not flee from the mess; it holds the line while the Father prepares the moment. **For Kingdom Obedience:** The obedience this calls forth is the obedience of the intercessor — the willingness to get on one’s knees, to weep, to make specific covenantal agreement with the Father, and then to *wait*. Not to act out of anxiety or the pressure to produce visible results, but to yield the outcome entirely to the One who holds all jurisdictional authority over every spirit, every territory, every name. Kingdom obedience in spiritual warfare is not the charging of a warrior but the precision of a legal advocate who knows exactly whose court they are standing in. **For Prophetic Posture:** The prophetic posture this event trains is one of *calibrated discernment* — the ability to distinguish between a genuine word from the Lord and the consistent counterfeit that comes immediately after it. The enemy’s most dangerous work is not in overt attack; it is in imitation. The saints who will stand in the days ahead must know the voice of the Shepherd with enough intimacy that the stranger’s voice — even when it sounds nearly identical — produces the same instinctive withdrawal that Jesus described in John 10. This discernment is not a gift reserved for a prophetic elite. It is the birthright of every believer who will consecrate themselves to abiding in the Vine. ## A Closing Prayer Father, I stand before You tonight as a man whose faith was shaking and whose knees found the floor, and I am undone by what You did. You are not impressed by volume or formula. You are not moved by religious performance. You are moved by sons and daughters who know who they are in You, who come to You in their poverty and their specificity and their surrender, and who trust that the covenant they carry is not their own invention but Your own blood-sealed promise. Thank You for Samuel. Thank You for keeping him. Thank You that Abigail is gone and that little Elijah is covered and that the corpus is ready and that Your Kingdom does not advance on the strength of my consistency but on the strength of Your faithfulness. Father, I repent of the seasons I have let thin — the secret place, the Word, the abiding — and I return to You not with shame but with the confidence of a son who knows the door is always open. Lord Jesus, I receive again the full weight of the ἐξουσία (ex-OO-see-ah) You have placed in my hands, and I consecrate it to You, asking for the wisdom to wield it in Your timing and by Your instruction. Let the body of Christ recover what has been forgotten. Let the intercessors rise. Let the stones cry out if we will not. And let every word that is ready be released in the fullness of its season. To You be the glory, the power, and the dominion, in time and in eternity.
